# Infinitely many closed free-elastic expanders with turning number zero

Consider the free elastic flow of immersed closed plane curves with signed curvature \(k\), arclength \(s\), unit tangent \(T\) and normal \(N=JT\). Normalize a pure homothetic expander by putting its dilation centre at the origin and its dilation coefficient equal to one. Its profile satisfies

\[
 X_s=T,\qquad T_s=kN,\qquad k_{ss}+k^3/2=-X\cdot N.
\]

**Theorem.** There are infinitely many geometrically distinct smooth closed profiles of this equation with turning number zero. They pass through their expansion centre with \(k=0\) and unbounded positive curvature slopes \(b=k_s(0)\). In fact, there are constants \(c>0\) and \(B_0<\infty\) such that for every \(B\ge B_0\) at least \(\lfloor cB\rfloor\) different primitive shapes have a centre slope in \((0,B]\). Similarities, changes of parameter, and multiple traversals are identified in this count. Under the flow each profile expands by the factor \((1+4t)^{1/4}\).

Miura and Wheeler ask for other self-similar solutions of the free elastic flow. Andrews and Wheeler already construct infinitely many dihedral solutions using arcs with turning angle \(\pi/m\). Here the total signed curvature is exactly zero for every constructed profile, and the count uses the normalized derivative at a centre crossing. The full classification, even with fixed turning number zero, remains open. [Original questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12619v3#S1.SS2) · [Andrews and Wheeler, Theorem 1.1 and §2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21227v1#S2.SS6)

## Proof

Start every shot at \(X=0,\ T=(1,0),\ k=0,\ k_s=b>0\). The aim is to reach \(k_s=X\cdot T=0\) simultaneously at some positive arclength. Two symmetries of the original differential equation then close the immersion. The shooting parameter stays free throughout the argument.

1. **Arrival near the vanishing averaged stress.** Set \(\delta=1/b,\ \tau=s/\sqrt b,\ Z=X/\sqrt b\). In the fast variable the leading curvature oscillator is \(U''=-U^3/2,\ U(0)=0,\ U'(0)=1\). Write \(P\) for its least period, \(\gamma=P^{-1}\int_0^P U^2/2>0\), \(\tau_*=\sqrt{3/\gamma}\), and \(z_*=\gamma\tau_*\). Fixed-time averaging gives \(Z\to\gamma\tau e_y\) while the normalized stress tends to \((1-\tau^2/\tau_*^2)e_y\) on compact intervals below \(\tau_*\). Two explicit oscillatory corrections control the actual trajectories down to stress of order \(\delta^{2/3}\). In a frame centred on the instantaneous radial direction, take a large fixed corrected incoming stress level \(H\). The shot meets that section transversely and continuously in \(b\). Its transverse coordinate has \(\limsup_{b\to\infty}|I_b|\le C/H\), its radius parameter \(\epsilon_b=|X|^{-4/3}\) tends to zero, and its lifted oscillator phase \(\Phi(b)\) tends to infinity. The constant \(C\) is independent of the chosen sufficiently large level. [Fixed-time averaging](../ReflectedExpanders/LargeMarkerNecessaryBounds.md) · [Estimates up to the section](RealIncomingLayer.md)

2. **A family of trajectories ending at a seam.** At \(\epsilon=0\), integrate backwards from seam data with normalized curvature \(c\in[-1/4,1]\). Every such trajectory reaches each sufficiently high incoming section. An explicit corrected transverse coordinate has integrable derivative along the tails. For \(c=-1/4\) the limiting impact is less than \(-0.033\), and for \(c=1\) it is greater than \(0.542\). The estimates use two validated finite integrations of a polynomial ordinary differential equation followed by analytic tail bounds. Thus on every sufficiently high common section these endpoint impacts strictly straddle, for instance, the interval \([-0.02,0.02]\). The impacts of the entire seam interval remain uniformly bounded. [Escape, endpoint enclosures and tail estimates](FrozenScattering.md) · [Independent replay and uniformity](IndependentMatchingAudit.md)

3. **Returning to finite physical radius.** Fix \(H\) large enough that the preceding bounds give strict separation from the two seam endpoints, then take \(b\) sufficiently large. The exact inner equations are regular at \(\epsilon=0\). The derivative of the incoming radius parameter with respect to its seam value equals one on the limiting trajectories. Continuous dependence and the implicit function theorem therefore produce a whole curve of seam data at the prescribed incoming \(\epsilon_b>0\). Its phase has a bounded real lift \(\Phi_\Gamma(c,\epsilon_b)\). Together with its transverse coordinate \(I_\Gamma(c,\epsilon_b)\), these data determine the complete state on the section. [Matching at fixed physical radius](MatchingAtTheLayer.md)

4. **A simultaneous zero at arbitrarily large slopes.** Start at any sufficiently large lower bound \(D\). Choose an integer \(n\) and then \(D'>D\) so that the phase difference \(\Phi(b)-\Phi_\Gamma(c,\epsilon_b)-nP\) has opposite strict signs on the two \(b\)-faces of \([D,D']\times[-1/4,1]\). The difference \(I_\Gamma(c,\epsilon_b)-I_b\) has opposite strict signs on the two \(c\)-faces. A planar rectangle crossing gives an exact simultaneous zero. The two original states coincide in their radial frames, with identical physical radius. Uniqueness therefore carries the centre shot to the actual seam at some \(s_*>0\). The two reflection identities extend it smoothly to period \(4s_*\) and give \(k(s+2s_*)=-k(s)\). The integrated curvature over that period is zero. The tangent, normal and support components return, closing the actual position. These curves are noncircular because \(k(0)=0,\ k_s(0)=b>0\). [Crossing and geometric closure](MatchingAtTheLayer.md) · [Lean rectangle theorem](../../SolitonClassification/RectangleCrossing.lean) · [Lean geometric reconstruction](../../SolitonClassification/ReflectedGeometry.lean)

5. **Counting primitive shapes.** On the incoming section \(\Phi(b)/b\to\pi\tau_*/4\). Indeed the initial fixed slow interval contributes the integral of \(\sqrt{1-\tau^2/\tau_*^2}\), and the remaining contribution to \(\Phi/b\) is \(O(a^{3/2})\) when the initial interval ends at averaged amplitude \(a\). For a fixed lower face and an upper face \(B\), the number of integer phase levels strictly between them thus grows at least linearly in \(B\). At a given marker \(b\), the compact seam phase lift has bounded oscillation, so the number of integer levels it can realize is uniformly bounded. A coefficient-one closed profile has a unique normalized scale and centre. On a reflected profile every zero-curvature visit to this centre has the same absolute curvature derivative, including on any cover. Therefore distinct successful markers correspond to different primitive shapes. [Counting details and scale uniqueness](IndependentMatchingAudit.md) · [Lean centre-marker theorem](../../SolitonClassification/ReflectedMarker.lean)

## Geometry at large slope

For the solutions just constructed, the chosen seam distance satisfies \(s_*/\sqrt b\to\tau_*\). In the normalization \(X/\sqrt b\) with the initial tangent fixed as above, the whole closed images converge in Hausdorff distance to the line segment \([-z_*e_y,z_*e_y]\). Their primitive lengths satisfy \(L/\sqrt b\to4\tau_*\).

Indeed, the section crossing occurs at \(\tau\to\tau_*\) and \(Z\to z_*e_y\). At fixed \(H\), the remaining flight to the seam takes bounded inner arclength uniformly on the compact seam family. There \(\epsilon_\sigma=O(\epsilon^2)\), so the radius stays comparable to its incoming value, of order \(\sqrt b\). Since \(ds=|X|^{-1/3}d\sigma\), this last physical flight has length \(O_H(b^{-1/6})\). On each fixed earlier slow interval the positions converge uniformly to \(\gamma\tau e_y\). Up to the section the remaining slow duration can be bounded by \(Ca\), while \(|Z_\tau|=1\); take \(a\) to zero. This proves that the first quarter images converge to \([0,z_*e_y]\).

For large \(b\), the quarter has no second visit to the centre. In the late part \(|Z|\) stays bounded away from zero. In the fixed earlier slow interval the estimate \(Z=\gamma\tau e_y+O(\delta)\) confines a hypothetical centre visit to bounded fast time \(x=\tau/\delta\). On compact fast intervals the position \(\sqrt b X\) and its fast derivative converge to \((U(x),\int_0^x U^2/2)\) and its derivative, by smooth dependence. The limiting second component is strictly positive at all \(x>0\). Near zero the first component of the fast derivative stays positive, uniformly. This excludes all other centre visits on the quarter.

Reflection across the normal line at the seam, which contains both the seam and the centre, gives the next quarter. This normal line tends to \(\mathbb R e_y\). Central inversion about the origin gives the other half of the image, proving the stated Hausdorff convergence. Over one traversal of length \(4s_*\) the only centre visits modulo this period are at \(0\) and \(2s_*\); the signed curvature derivatives there are \(b\) and \(-b\). Every smaller positive position period would have to take \(0\) to the second visit and preserve the curvature derivative. Hence \(4s_*\) is the primitive length.

The scale-invariant bending energy has the explicit asymptotic
\[
             \frac{E L}{b^2}\ \longrightarrow\ 32,
             \qquad E=\tfrac12\int_0^L k^2\,ds .
\]
On the initial fixed slow interval the quarter energy divided by \(b^{3/2}\) is \(\int A U(\phi)^2/2\,d\tau\). Replacing \(U^2/2\) by its phase mean \(\gamma\) incurs \(O_a(\delta)\): use the periodic primitive of \(A(U^2/2-\gamma)/\sqrt A\) and the bounded phase-speed error on that interval. The limiting integrand is \(\gamma(1-\tau^2/\tau_*^2)\). Between its right endpoint and the incoming section, \(A\le Ca\), \(|U|\le\sqrt2\), and the duration is \(O(a)\). In the final bounded inner flight, the inner curvature \(k/|X|^{1/3}\) stays bounded, so the physical contribution is \(O_H(b^{1/6})\) before division by \(b^{3/2}\). Taking \(a\) to zero gives \(E/b^{3/2}\to 4\gamma(2\tau_*/3)\), since the four quarter energies are identical. Multiply by the length limit and use \(\gamma\tau_*^2=3\).

The number \(N(b)\) of sign-changing curvature zeros on the primitive curve grows linearly in the marker, with \(N(b)/b\to\sqrt{3\pi/2}\). Up to the incoming section the oscillator phase counts two simple zeros per period. On the remaining inner flight the curvature is analytic and has a uniformly bounded number of zeros over the compact seam family. This follows by covering the family and its time intervals by finitely many boxes where a fixed derivative is nonzero, then applying Rolle's theorem. The period integral yields \(\gamma P^2=8\pi\). In particular \(N(b)^2/(EL)\to 3\pi/64\). [Proof of the uniform zero count and evaluation of the constant](IndependentMatchingAudit.md)
