# Finitely many plane image types under a length or energy cap

Normalize the dilation coefficient of a smooth closed free-elastic similarity profile to one. The rotation coefficient may be any real number. For a bound $R$ on the primitive arclength, put
$$
 K=\left\lfloor 3\big[1+2^{24}R(1+R)^2\big]^2\right\rfloor,\qquad
 B(K)=1+\sum_{e=1}^{K}2e\,(2e)! .
$$
The images realize at most $B(K)$ topological ambient isotopy types in the oriented plane. The same bound applies under a cap $EL\le A$ on the scale-invariant energy product, with $R=2^{1/4}\sqrt A$. Here $E=\tfrac12\int k^2\,ds$, and $E,L$ refer to the same traversal. For a dilation coefficient $a>0$, the normalized length is $a^{1/4}$ times the original length.

**From coincident labels to edges.** Use one primitive traversal on its parameter circle. The global crossing estimate bounds the number $P$ of ordered pairs of distinct labels at the same point by $K$. Mark each image point with $j\ge2$ preimages as a vertex. If there are no vertices, the image is a Jordan curve and gives one isotopy type. Otherwise, cutting the parameter circle at the preimages of all vertices produces exactly $e=\sum j$ open arcs. Their interiors are embedded and mutually disjoint. Their closures give the edges of a connected plane graph, allowing loops and parallel edges. Since $P=\sum j(j-1)$, we have $e\le P\le K$. The inequality counts tangencies and all multiplicities. The normalized identities $\int F^2=E$ and $\int kF=L$, where $F=k_{ss}+k^3/2$, imply $L^2\le 2E^2$, hence $L^2\le\sqrt2\,EL$. A cap on a full traversal also bounds its primitive traversal. [Crossing estimate and energy identities](CrossingComplexity.md)

**The plane embedding.** Each vertex has a disk in which the image is topologically a star with $2j$ rays. Indeed, the distance from the vertex increases strictly along the initial part of each outgoing half-edge, since it has nonzero one-sided velocity. Compactness excludes the rest of the curve from a sufficiently small disk. On each concentric circle the $2j$ points have a fixed cyclic order. Interpolating their angles in that order straightens the branches to fixed rays, continuously at the centre since distances are preserved. This includes coincident tangent directions. After choosing disjoint vertex disks, take a narrow band along each remaining edge. These disks and bands make a connected regular neighbourhood of the graph.

Label the $2e$ half-edges so the two ends of each edge are paired by the fixed involution $\alpha=(1\,2)(3\,4)\cdots(2e-1\,\,2e)$. The counterclockwise successor at each vertex specifies a permutation $\sigma$ of the half-edges. Its cycles give all vertices and their cyclic orders. The cycles of $\sigma\alpha$ trace the face boundaries under a consistent choice of side. Mark the cycle corresponding to the unbounded face. There are at most $(2e)!$ permutations and at most $2e$ choices for this mark.

The data determine the plane embedding up to ambient isotopy. Matching disks and bands produces an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of the two regular neighbourhoods carrying the graphs to each other. On the sphere the complementary components of a connected regular neighbourhood are disks bounded by disjoint Jordan curves, even when a boundary walk of the original graph repeats vertices. Extend over the bounded complementary disks by the Schoenflies theorem. Matching the marked exterior boundaries allows extension across the exterior annuli to the identity on the boundary of a large disk containing both graphs. The resulting homeomorphism of that disk is isotopic to the identity relative to its boundary by the Alexander trick. Counting all codes, including unused and equivalent ones, gives $B(K)$.

**Traversals and scope.** For primitive immersions up to topological ambient isotopy and orientation-preserving reparametrization of the source circle, additionally pair the half-edges continued through each of the $j$ visits at a vertex and record the direction of traversal. Only pairings yielding one circuit occur. There are at most $(2j-1)!!\le3^{j(j-1)/2}$ choices at a vertex, since $2h-1\le3^{h-1}$ for $h\ge1$. Thus $2\,3^{K/2}B(K)$ also bounds these primitive traversals. Covering degree is an additional integer if multiple traversals are distinguished. A cap on primitive length alone permits all degrees. For a cap on total normalized length $R$, the primitive lower bound $L_{\rm prim}>2^{-11}$ bounds the degree by $2^{11}R$. For a cap $A$ on total energy product, Fenchel's inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz give $E_{\rm prim}L_{\rm prim}\ge2\pi^2$, so the degree is at most $\lfloor\sqrt{A/(2\pi^2)}\rfloor$. The isotopies above are continuous. Smooth ambient equivalence can retain additional data such as the projective cross-ratio of four distinct tangent lines at one point. This argument gives a finite upper bound on topological types. Determining which codes solve the profile equation or classifying their metric shapes requires further analysis. The passage from the global crossing estimate to the plane codes and isotopy is a written proof. The linked estimate identifies its Lean-verified local results and its written global steps.
