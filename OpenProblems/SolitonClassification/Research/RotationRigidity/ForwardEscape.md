# Forward escape near twelve pure expanders

Fix any of the twelve pure profiles in the [validated quarter-return calculation](RotationRigidity.md). There are a tube about its marked support orbit, a pitch interval about zero and a constant \(C\) such that a trajectory at nonzero pitch \(\omega\) stays continuously inside this tube for at most \(C/|\omega|\) units of support arclength. In particular, trapping of an entire positive semiorbit is excluded. The statement is local to each of the twelve profiles.

**Lower bound for a physical return step.** Let \(z\) be an initial point in the seam section and \(T=T(z,\omega)\) its marked return time, close to the reference time. Use the augmented notation \(z\) for the support state and its constant pitch. With the flow denoted by \(\phi\) and the reversing isometry by \(R\), write \(G(z,T)=\phi_T(z)-z\). The injective odd derivative and strict differentiability give
\[
 c|\omega|\le \|G(z,T)-G(Rz,T)\|.
\]
Here \(T\) belongs to the even coordinates. The same numerical value of \(T\) is used on both sides, irrespective of the return time associated with \(Rz\). Smooth dependence of the inverse flow on state and time on a compact neighborhood gives a uniform constant \(K\). Thus
\[
 \|G(Rz,T)\|=\|\phi_{-T}(z)-z\|
 =\|\phi_{-T}(z)-\phi_{-T}(\phi_T(z))\|
 \le K\|G(z,T)\|.
\]
All evaluations take place near the reference orbit and over times near its fixed marked period. Returning to the seam section sets the velocity coordinate to zero, while pitch stays fixed. Equivalence of norms in the fixed linear section coordinates therefore gives a lower bound \(g=c'|\omega|\) for every return displacement.

**Number of returns in a tube.** In the fixed-pitch eigencoordinates of the [direct cone criterion](DirectConeRigidity.md), let \(v_i=x_{i+1}-x_i\) and \(m_i=\|v_i\|_\infty\). Throughout a small section neighborhood let \(\|x_i\|_\infty\le D\), so \(g\le m_i\le2D\). Write \(q=|a|+\varepsilon<1\) and \(p=|u|-\varepsilon>1\), choosing \(\varepsilon>0\) so that \(q<1-\varepsilon\) and \(p>1+\varepsilon\).

If the unstable coordinate is maximal at index \(i\), it is strictly maximal at index \(i+1\), with \(m_{i+1}\ge p m_i\). If the stable coordinate is maximal at index \(i+1\), we have \(m_{i+1}\le q m_i\). Unstable dominance at \(i\) is then impossible, and neutral dominance would give \(m_{i+1}\ge(1-\varepsilon)m_i>q m_i\). So stable dominance propagates backwards. Consequently the stable-maximal steps form an initial segment and the unstable-maximal steps a terminal segment. There can be a tie at their common endpoint. Their lengths are bounded respectively by \(1+\log(2D/g)/(-\log q)\) and \(1+\log(2D/g)/\log p\). All other steps are neutral-maximal, with neutral displacements of a common sign and magnitude at least \(g\). Their count is at most \(2D/g\) by telescoping the neutral coordinates. This bounds the total by \(O(1/|\omega|)\), uniformly for sufficiently small nonzero pitch.

The phase coordinate and uniform bounds on the marked return times from the [tube construction](RotationRigidity.md) convert the bound into an arclength estimate. A partial revolution at each endpoint adds only a bounded amount. The estimate concerns a contiguous stay; a trajectory may leave and subsequently revisit the tube.

There is also a uniform bound on the **sum** of the section displacements in such a passage. Write \(d=\min(1,1-q,p-1)>0\). On an unstable-maximal step assign the scalar value \(6D+m_i\); on a stable-maximal step assign \(-m_i\); on all remaining steps assign \(3D+\operatorname{sgn}((v_i)_c)(x_i)_c\). Resolve a tie by giving the unstable case priority. At each successive pair of steps the assigned value increases by at least \(d m_i\). This follows from the contraction and expansion in the two outer blocks and the same sign of central displacement inside the middle block. A transition to a later block gains at least \(2D\), enough to cover \(d m_i\). The assigned value always belongs to \([-2D,8D]\), giving \(d\sum m_i\le10D\). In an infinite trapped semiorbit the sum is finite. The section points are then Cauchy and converge to a fixed point in the closure of the trap.

The physical application uses the validated interval enclosures of the quarter flow and the smooth, reversible Poincaré construction. The [bounded-step theorem](../../SolitonClassification/ForwardConeEscape.lean) establishes arbitrarily small displacement along bounded semiorbits, and the [compact trapping theorem](../../SolitonClassification/ForwardTrapRigidity.lean) applies it to each of the twelve spectral boxes under the section hypotheses. Lean also verifies the [uniform finite-passage variation estimate](../../SolitonClassification/FinitePassage.lean) and the [pitch-dependent bound on return count](../../SolitonClassification/PitchResidence.lean). The latter takes the spectral coordinates and displacement estimate for the given section map as hypotheses. Its companion lemma obtains the displacement estimate from odd derivative separation and control of the reversed residual. The physical construction of the section and enclosure of the ODE remain in the [earlier computer-assisted argument](RotationRigidity.md).
