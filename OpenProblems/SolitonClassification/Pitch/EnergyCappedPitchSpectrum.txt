Finite-interval structure of the rotation spectrum at fixed energy bound

Human proof. The estimate for a single orbit is in PitchOscillationBound.
The structural result here uses real analytic geometry and is a separate
claim. No isolation, generic transversality or exact list of pitches is
assumed.

Normalize an expanding soliton by spatial dilation so that a=1, keeping
w=b/a fixed, and translate its similarity center to the origin. Let S_K
denote the set of nonzero w for which there exists a closed noncircular
expander with E L <= K. Here L is a full geometric period, E=1/2 int k^2
on that same period, and curves may be immersed or multiply covered.

Theorem. For fixed finite K, the spectrum including homothetic noncircular
profiles is a finite union of compact intervals and points. Hence S_K is
relatively closed away from zero and is a finite union of points and
intervals, possibly with an endpoint at zero removed.
If it is infinite, some open interval of pitches is realized by a family
of actual closed profiles varying locally real analytically in w (with
fixed turning number). Thus over all energy levels either the pitch set is
countable or it contains an interval realized by such a family. This
alternative does not decide which case occurs.

1. Compactness of the shooting parameters within their flow domain

Let D=int F^2, F=k''+k^3/2. The existing periodic support identities give
D=E and int kF=L, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives E>=L/sqrt(2). Consequently
L^2 <= sqrt(2)K. The closed-curve stress coercivity estimate gives

       M^6 <= 2^24 E D = 2^24 E^2,   M=max|k|.

This is the static pointwise inequality in StressCoercivity, with the
same energy convention. Since E<=(L/2)M^2, it gives E<=128^3 L^3.
Thus L is bounded away from zero through E>=L/sqrt(2), and E<=K/L,
M and D are bounded above. The signed curvature has a critical point,
so |v|=|k'|<=int|k''|<=sqrt(LD)+(L/2) M^3. The pitch inequality gives
|w|<=sqrt(K/8) because 16w^2<=2EL. All bounds depend only on K.

Bound the initial position and its support coordinates. Set C=(I+wJ)beta(0),
J being positive quarter-turn. If C is nonzero take e=C/|C|. At an extremum
of the periodic scalar projection beta.e, N=+e or -e. Since |N'|<=M, over
a following arc of length d=min(L/2,1/(2 max(1,M))) the absolute value of
N.e is at least 1/2. Hence int(C.N)^2 >= d|C|^2/4. But
F=-N.(I+wJ)beta with |beta(s)-beta(0)|<=L, so

       ||C.N||_2 <= sqrt(D) + sqrt(1+w^2) L^(3/2).

The bound on d away from zero proves |C| and |beta(0)| uniformly bounded.
It also trivially covers C=0. Since all points are within L of beta(0),
p,q are uniformly bounded over the entire full period. The closed position
was essential when choosing a normal parallel to e.

Let z=(k(0),v(0),p(0),q(0)). The finite coordinates (z,w,L) are bounded.
Let U be the open set of triples with L>0 for which the polynomial support
system exists on [0,L]. The closure of these candidate triples is compact
INSIDE U. Indeed, for any convergent parameter sequence use u=s/L on [0,1].
The uniformly bounded trajectories and their bounded right-hand sides are
equicontinuous. Arzela--Ascoli supplies a uniformly convergent subsequence,
and its integral equation passes to the limit. Uniqueness identifies the
limiting trajectory as the solution at the limiting parameters, and its
bounded endpoint permits continuation beyond the limiting L. The previous
positive lower bound prevents L=0 in the closure.

The condition of being noncircular also survives the limit, even at w=0.
For a normalized circle the two equilibria are independent of w, with
k=c=+/-2^(1/4), v=q=0, p=-1/c. At either equilibrium its Jacobian A
has characteristic polynomial

       y^4+(5/2)c^2 y^2-(w/c)y+4.

It has no purely imaginary root. For w!=0 the imaginary part forces y=0;
for w=0 the quadratic in the squared frequency has discriminant -7/2.
Thus exp(L A)-I is invertible for every L>0 by spectral mapping. The
analytic implicit function theorem applied to Phi_L(z,w)-z at this
equilibrium gives a unique local solution z for each nearby pair (w,L).
The same constant equilibrium is already that solution for all (w,L).
A sequence of non-equilibrium recurrence points cannot approach it.
Periodicity, integer angle holonomy (passing to a constant m subsequence),
and the energy bound all survive the limit. The family allowing w=0 is
therefore compact in U, whereas imposing w!=0 only removes a closed slice.

2. Finite-dimensional real analytic geometry

On U the endpoint map z -> Phi_L(z,w), integrated angle H=int k and
B=int k^2 jointly depend real analytically on z,w,L, by adjoining H'=k
and B'=k^2 to the polynomial field. For each integer m the set

       Phi_L(z,w)=z,     H=2 pi m,     LB<=2K,
                        |f_w(z)|^2>0

is semianalytic in U. Here f_w(z) is the support-system vector field.
Its equilibria are exactly the centered circle data; with non-equilibrium
and positive period, constant k is impossible (the periodic support
constant-curvature theorem). Every point of this set reconstructs a closed
unit-speed profile, since the supports recur and integer angle holonomy
returns the frame. Conversely every sought full period gives such a point.
Only finitely many m occur since (2 pi m)^2<=2EL<=2K.

By step 1 their union is compact in U. A relatively compact semianalytic
subset has finitely many connected components. Each component is compact.
Project each continuously to the w-axis. Its image is compact and connected
and hence a point or a closed interval. Removing w=0 proves the stated
claim about the genuinely rotating spectrum.
To see analytic families when the projection has interior, use the
locally finite semianalytic stratification compatible with these sets and
with the constant rank of the w projection. On the compact closure only
finitely many local strata are needed; the strata can be taken connected.
If every stratum has rank zero there are only finitely many image values.
Otherwise a rank-one stratum gives a local real analytic section by the
analytic submersion theorem. The reconstructed support curve and frame,
parametrized by the fixed u in [0,1], vary real analytically. Finally take
integer K tending to infinity to obtain the all-energy alternative.

If nonzero pitches approach zero while EL stays bounded, a subsequence
of the shooting parameters converges to an actual noncircular homothetic
closed profile. At this limiting point, the real analytic curve selection
lemma for the semianalytic set cut out by w!=0 gives an analytic arc of
such exact recurrences ending there. The analytic parameter on this arc
can have high-order contact with the plane w=0. No span or invertibility of
a specific shooting Jacobian follows from curve selection.

At every nonempty fixed cap the strict pitch inequality and compactness
give a uniform gap

       EL - 2 pi^2 m^2 - 8 w^2 >= d_K > 0

over all its noncircular solutions. Indeed the left side is the continuous
quantity EL-H^2/2-8w^2 on a compact set and is everywhere positive.
For any fixed turning m, *without* an energy cap, this also implies
EL >= 2 pi^2 m^2 + d_m with some d_m>0. Set K=2 pi^2 m^2+1. If the set
under that cap is empty take d_m=1; otherwise take d_m=min(1,d_K).
The constants from compactness are qualitative. When a prescribed
nonzero pitch is realized, the least EL among its closed realizations is
attained, by restricting to the cap of any one realization.

References for the analytic geometry: E. Bierstone and P. D. Milman,
Semianalytic and subanalytic sets, Publ. Math. IHES 67 (1988), 5-42,
Corollary 2.7 and Lemma 3.4 (the latter used locally on the graph).
https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.1007/BF02699126/
https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1988__67__5_0.pdf

3. Distinction from the Jellyfish shooting arcs

The free elastic flow construction in
https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21227v1
uses a family of homothetic blocks (w=0). Varying the block parameter
changes the angle at the seam. A rational angle in units of pi closes the
reflected blocks after finitely many repeats. Rational angles are dense
where that angle has nonzero derivative. This shows why analyticity of a
one-block shooting map alone does not imply discreteness of primitive
block data satisfying some rational closure. It gives no interval of
nonzero rotation pitches. Nor does density in block parameters imply
density of *full* closed curves at bounded EL, since increasing the
denominator can increase the required period and energy.
