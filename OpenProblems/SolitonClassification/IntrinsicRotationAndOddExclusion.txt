INTRINSIC ROTATION CONSTRAINTS AND EXCLUSION AT EVEN BIFURCATIONS
12 November 2026. Analytic deductions, not Lean formalized.
The unrestricted classification of closed similarity profiles is NOT solved.
No rotating closed profile, interval root or novelty claim is asserted here.

1. SETTING AND THE TWO STRESS VARIABLES

Let beta be a smooth closed unit-speed planar immersion of period L>0.
Use N=JT, T'=kN, p=beta.N, q=beta.T and F=k''+k^3/2. An actual fixed-center
similarity profile obeys

             F=-a p-b q,     p'=-kq,     q'=1+kp.       (1)

For closed profiles a>0 is already known. Circles may have arbitrary b
because rotation only reparametrizes their image. Define h=aq-bp. Then

             F'=k h-b,             h'=a-k F.            (2)

These follow by differentiating (1), without dividing by k. They are
regular at inflection points and are the main identities used below.
The familiar stress W=(k^2/2)T+k'N satisfies W'=FN. Its torque primitive
C=W.Jbeta-k has C'=Fq. Thus integration still gives only

               a integral(pq)+b integral(q^2)=0.

The cross term integral(pq) is not set to zero. Pointwise beta.Jbeta=0
does not imply orthogonality of their normal components.

2. A FOURTH-ORDER EQUATION INVOLVING CURVATURE ONLY

Eliminating h from (2) without division gives

      k F''-k'(F'+b)+k^3 F-a k^2=0.                     (3)

Equivalently, for v=k', w=k'', j=k''',

 k k''''-v j+(3/2)k^2 v^2+(5/2)k^3 w+k^6/2-a k^2-b v=0. (4)

This is necessary for every actual profile, including at k=0. Integrate
(4) over a period and use periodic integration by parts. The result is

 2 integral(w^2)-6 integral(k^2 v^2)+(1/2)integral(k^6)
                   = a integral(k^2),

or a=integral(F^2)/E, where E=(1/2)integral(k^2), as also follows from
the geometric dilation identity. This intrinsic derivation agrees with
the already established gradient identity; it is not a new sign argument.

Multiplying (4) by v gives an additional explicit intrinsic formula:

 b integral(v^2) = (9/4) integral[v (2w^2-k^2 v^2)].       (5)

For transparency, if S is the left-hand side of (4), the exact local
integration-by-parts identity is

 S v = H' +(9/4)v(2w^2-k^2v^2)-b v^2,
 H = k v j-2v^2 w-(1/2)k w^2+(5/4)k^3v^2+k^7/14-a k^3/3.

Thus every NONCONSTANT periodic curvature determines b uniquely by (5),
without choosing an origin or solving a Gram system. Constant-curvature
circles have integral(v^2)=0, exactly matching their rotation redundancy.
The numerator need not vanish for a general periodic function. Formula
(5) constrains rotating profiles but does not exclude them universally.

3. INFLECTION JETS AND A NEW LOCAL CONSTITUTIVE EXCLUSION

At every curvature zero z, (2) gives the exact condition

                          k'''(z)=-b.                    (6)

Suppose a sign-changing periodic profile has the following additional
property: on neighborhoods of ALL its inflection points, the force has
the same differentiable dependence on curvature,

                    F(s)=G(k(s)),     G in C^1 near 0.    (7)

Then b=0. Indeed, if any zero has k'=0, differentiating (7) and using
(2) gives b=0 immediately. Otherwise all zeros are simple. There are
upward and downward crossings of a sign-changing periodic function;
at them (6)--(7) give -b=G'(0)k'(z). Opposite nonzero signs of k'(z)
cannot give the same nonzero value. Hence again b=0.

The previously proved one-sign-curvature theorem says that a nonconstant
closed profile must change curvature sign. Consequently a genuinely
rotating noncircular closed profile cannot satisfy (7). It requires
different force-versus-curvature branches near its inflections. This
excludes all globally autonomous scalar curvature ansatzes F=G(k), but
assumption (7) is substantially local: no dependence away from k=0 is
required. It does not assume reflection symmetry or integral(pq)=0.

For b!=0, actual curvature zeros have order at most three: (6) gives a
nonzero third derivative whenever k=k'=k''=0. All triple zeros then cross
in the same direction, sign(-b), so periodic sign-changing curvature
cannot have only triple crossings. At least one opposite crossing is
simple. Double zeros can be present but do not change sign. Polynomial
ODE solutions are analytic, and k is not identically zero, so there are
only finitely many zeros in a period. These are restrictions on admissible
profiles, not an existence proof or exclusion of all b!=0.

4. CONVERSE: AN EXACT INTRINSIC REDUCTION WITH SIMPLE INFLECTIONS

Let k be a smooth L-periodic real function, not identically zero, with
all zeros simple (the no-zero case is allowed). Suppose constants a>0,b
make (3) hold everywhere, and integral_0^L k=2pi m for an integer m.
Then k reconstructs an ACTUAL smooth closed unit-speed similarity profile.

Proof. On {k!=0}, define h=(F'+b)/k. At a zero z, evaluating (3) yields
-k'(z)(F'(z)+b)=0; because k'(z)!=0, the numerator vanishes. The smooth
division lemma at a simple zero extends h smoothly, with
h(z)=F''(z)/k'(z). Its periodic extension is unique. Equation (3), divided
by k^2 away from the zeros, says h'=a-kF. Both sides are continuous,
so this holds at the zeros too. The identity F'=kh-b holds everywhere.

Put A=a^2+b^2>0 and set

       p=(-aF-bh)/A,          q=(-bF+ah)/A.

Direct differentiation gives p'=-kq and q'=1+kp, while F=-ap-bq.
Let theta'=k, T=(cos theta,sin theta), N=JT, and beta=qT+pN.
Then beta'=T exactly. The integer holonomy makes T,N periodic, and p,q
are already periodic; hence beta is closed, smooth and unit speed with
signed curvature k and the required profile equation.

Therefore (3) plus integer holonomy is necessary and sufficient within
the simple-inflection class. This is a scalar reduction, not a solution
of its periodic boundary-value problem. For multiple zeros, (3) alone
does not supply the required smooth divisibility; the converse above
does not silently include them. More generally the same reconstruction
works if smooth periodic divisibility F'+b=k h is supplied explicitly.

5. LOCAL NONROTATION DOES NOT REQUIRE FULL SHOOTING RANK

Normalize a=1. Write the support field
 f_omega(k,v,p,q)=(v,-k^3/2-p-omega*q,-kq,1+kp)
and R=diag(1,-1,1,-1). The exact reversing relation is
 f_omega(Rz)=-R f_{-omega}(z).
Consider a periodic base orbit with omega=0 and initial v=q=0. Use the
phase slice q0=0 and parameters

 e=(k0,p0,L) in R^3,        o=(v0,omega) in R^2.

Let Phi_omega(t,z) denote the local flow, which is defined over the
needed compact time interval near this periodic base. Instead of the
usual residual P=Phi_omega(L,z0)-z0, use

 G(e,o)=Phi_omega(L/2,z0)-Phi_omega(-L/2,z0).              (8)

The conditions G=0 and P=0 are equivalent. Reversibility gives exactly

                       G(e,-o)=-R G(e,o).

Thus its (k,p) components G_odd are odd in o and vanish identically at
o=0 for every nearby e; its (v,q) components are even in o.

THEOREM. If the 2x2 matrix D_o G_odd is invertible at the base orbit,
then every nearby periodic support orbit on the phase slice has
v0=omega=0. This conclusion does NOT require rank4 for DP, rank2 for a
half-return map, or a smooth one-dimensional full periodic zero set.

Proof. Apply the implicit function theorem to G_odd(e,o)=0. It has a
unique nearby solution o=o(e). Since o=0 is already a solution for every
e by reversibility, uniqueness gives o(e)=0. Every full G=0 must satisfy
this subsystem. If 1+k0p0!=0, the usual phase implicit function theorem
puts all sufficiently nearby periodic orbits on q0=0, so the statement
also holds modulo parameter shifts.

At an exact periodic base, this condition can be checked using the
ordinary shooting Jacobian: differentiating the flow composition gives

       DG = D_z Phi_0(-L/2,z0) DP.

The prefactor is invertible. Equivariance already puts D_oG entirely in
the two odd target components. Hence rank(DP restricted to v0,omega)=2
is equivalent to the required invertibility of D_oG_odd.

This theorem improves the earlier local reversible-branch criterion:
an even seam-swap or period-doubling degeneracy need not permit rotation.
A chiral branch approaching this base requires the odd block to lose
rank, even when the even subsystem is already singular. This is a local
conditional theorem, not a global classification or a validated rank
statement about a numerical seed.

7. WHAT REMAINS

There is no general proof that the right side of (5) vanishes, that force
is locally a single-valued function of curvature, or that every reversible
orbit has a nonsingular odd block. Large nonsymmetric rotating profiles
remain possible under these results. The intrinsic scalar reduction and
odd-rank criterion identify concrete missing conditions, while preserving
the distinction between periodic support data and integer planar closure.
