This strengthens Section4 of IntrinsicRotationAndOddExclusion.txt. The
simple-inflection assumption can be removed for smooth curvature. It cannot
be removed indiscriminately for every classical C4 solution of the degenerate
scalar equation: Section6 below gives even a C5 local counterexample.

1. GLOBAL DIVISIBILITY THEOREM

Let k:Real->Real be C6, not identically zero, and let a,b be real constants.
Set F=k''+k^3/2 and n=F'+b. Suppose, at every s,

             k n' - k' n = k^2 (a-kF).                 (S)

Then there is a UNIQUE function H satisfying everywhere

                   n=kH,        H'=a-kF.              (R)

It is obtained without division at zeros: choose s0 with k(s0)!=0 and set

 H(s)=n(s0)/k(s0)+ integral from s0 to s of [a-k(u)F(u)] du.             (1)

Initially H is C5. In fact (k,k',k'',H) is a smooth solution of the regular
polynomial system in Section3, so k and H are C-infinity. In particular the
theorem applies to every smooth k. No simple-zero, finite-zero, or a priori
smooth-divisibility assumption is used. The conclusion is independent of
the selected nonzero anchor s0.

Proof.
Write r=n-kH, using r rather than q to avoid confusion with the tangential
support function q in Section4. The definition of H and (S) give

                         k r' - k' r=0.                (2)

On an interval where k is nonzero, (r/k)'=0. At s0 we have r(s0)=0, so r
vanishes on a neighborhood of s0. Let I be the maximal open interval
containing s0 on which r is identically zero. This interval exists as the
connected component containing s0 of the interior of the zero set of r.

Suppose z is a finite endpoint of I. All functions and their indicated
derivatives extend across z because the original data are defined on Real.
Since r is C3 and identically zero on the I side, continuity gives

                       r^(j)(z)=0,       j=0,1,2,3.     (3)

If k(z)!=0, equation (2) and r(z)=0 immediately extend r=0 to a neighborhood
of z, contradicting maximality. It remains to consider k(z)=0.

On I, the identity r=0 is precisely

            k'''=kH-b-(3/2)k^2 k'.                      (4)

It holds at z by continuity. If k'(z)!=0, the zero of k has order1. If
k'(z)=0 but k''(z)!=0, it has order2. If k'(z)=k''(z)=0 and b!=0, (4) gives
k'''(z)=-b!=0, so it has order3.

The remaining case k(z)=k'(z)=k''(z)=0 and b=0 is impossible. For this
claim regard the already defined smooth-enough function H(s) as a known
coefficient. On I, (k,k',k'') solves the regular nonautonomous system

               x'=v,   v'=w,   w'=H(s)x-(3/2)x^2 v.     (5)

Its right side is continuous in s and locally Lipschitz in (x,v,w).
The zero solution has the same limiting data at z. Uniqueness also holds
backwards from this endpoint: on a sufficiently small closed interval ending
at z, the integral difference estimate and Gronwall give (k,k',k'')=0.
One may then start uniqueness at an interior point and continue over the
connected interval I. This forces k(s0)=0, a contradiction. Thus the zero
at z has finite order m in {1,2,3}.

Taylor's formula and (3) now give

 k(s)=c(s-z)^m+o(|s-z|^m),  c!=0,
 r(s)=o(|s-z|^m).

Consequently k has no other zero sufficiently close to z, and r/k extends
continuously to z with value0. Equation (2) makes this quotient constant on
each of the two punctured sides. Continuity forces both constants to be0.
Hence r=0 across z, again contradicting maximality. For smooth input, the
same step can be phrased using Hadamard division: both k and r factor by
(s-z)^m, the remaining factor of k is nonzero, and the quotient for r has
value0. Smooth divisibility is concluded here, not assumed.

Both finite endpoints are excluded by the same argument, so I=Real.
This proves (R). If H1 and H2 both satisfy (R), their derivatives agree;
their difference is constant and vanishes at s0 because k(s0)!=0. Therefore
H1=H2. Applying this uniqueness to the constructions from different anchors
proves anchor independence.

2. PERIODICITY IS DERIVED, NOT ADDED

Suppose additionally that k has period L>0. Then F,n and H'=a-kF are
L-periodic. The difference d(s)=H(s+L)-H(s) has derivative zero and is
therefore constant. At s0, the identity kH=n and periodicity of k,n give
k(s0)d(s0)=0. Since k(s0)!=0, d=0. Thus H itself is L-periodic.

An immediate necessary identity is

 a L = integral_0^L kF
     = -integral_0^L (k')^2 + (1/2)integral_0^L k^4.     (6)

This is obtained from the periodicity just proved. In particular there is
no separate unproved periodic smooth-division premise in the converse.

3. ZERO STRUCTURE AND AUTOMATIC SMOOTHNESS

The recovered identities are equivalent to the regular first-order system

 k'=v,
 v'=w,
 w'=kH-b-(3/2)k^2 v,
 H'=a-k(w+k^3/2).                                       (7)

Its right side is polynomial. A C1 solution is C2 by substitution into (7),
and repeated differentiation gives C-infinity regularity. This proves the
smoothness assertion for the C6 input theorem without invoking any analytic
regularity theorem.

At every zero z of a nonzero solution, k'''(z)=-b. If b!=0 the zero has
order at most3. If b=0, uniqueness in (5) rules out simultaneous vanishing
of k,k',k'', so the order is at most2. Every zero is consequently isolated.
There are finitely many zeros on every compact interval: an infinite set
would have an accumulation point, which is a zero by continuity and cannot
be isolated. In the periodic case there are finitely many in a period.
These facts are conclusions, not hypotheses used to construct H.

4. EXACT CLOSED-PROFILE RECONSTRUCTION, WITH ARBITRARY ZERO MULTIPLICITY

Let k be C6, nonzero as a function, and L-periodic, with L>0. Suppose a>0,
b is real, (S) holds, and

                    integral_0^L k = 2*pi*m,   m integer.              (8)

The equivalent unexpanded scalar equation is

             kF''-k'(F'+b)+k^3F-a k^2=0.                (9)

By Sections1--3, k,H are smooth and periodic and satisfy (R). Put

 A=a^2+b^2>0,
 p=(-aF-bH)/A,               q=(-bF+aH)/A.              (10)

Direct differentiation using (R) gives

                 p'=-kq,       q'=1+kp,
                         F=-ap-bq.                     (11)

Choose any constant theta0 and set
 theta(s)=theta0+integral_0^s k,
 T=(cos theta,sin theta), N=JT, beta=qT+pN.
Then T'=kN and N'=-kT, while (11) gives

          beta'=(q'-kp)T+(p'+kq)N=T.                   (12)

Thus beta is an actual smooth unit-speed immersion with signed curvature k.
All of p,q,T,N are L-periodic, the last two by (8); hence beta is closed.
Its actual support components are beta.N=p and beta.T=q, so its actual
elastic force obeys the fixed-center similarity profile equation

                 k''+k^3/2=-a(beta.N)-b(beta.T).        (13)

There is no additional unproved positional closure integral: (12) and the
periodicity of the explicitly constructed beta imply integral_0^L T=0.
The remaining choice theta0 rotates the constructed profile about its centre.

Conversely, every smooth closed unit-speed profile satisfying (13) has
F'=kH-b and H'=a-kF for H=aq-bp; these imply (9), and its tangent has
integer holonomy (8). Its curvature is not identically zero, because a
closed unit-speed curve cannot have a constant tangent. Hence (9) together
with integer holonomy is necessary and sufficient for the entire smooth
closed-profile class at fixed a>0,b, including every multiple inflection.

The statement concerns the specified period L. If a scalar curvature is
initially given with a smaller period P, rational holonomy over P can become
integer holonomy after an integer multiple of P; one must use that larger
period when applying this criterion. Noninteger holonomy must not be silently
treated as positional closure over the original period.

5. DEGENERATE CASES THAT MUST NOT BE DROPPED

The nonzero-function hypothesis is essential. k identically0 satisfies (9)
for every a,b and has holonomy0, but cannot represent a closed unit-speed
immersion. When b!=0 it does not even satisfy n=kH for any H. When b=0 the
identities (R) admit arbitrary additive constants, so uniqueness also fails.

The divisibility theorem itself allows arbitrary a,b. The closed-profile
converse above uses a>0 to ensure A>0 and the intended expanding class.
For example, a=b=0 and nontrivial periodic solutions of k''+k^3/2=0 satisfy
(S), with H=0, but the support formulas (10) are undefined. No conclusion
about closed profiles is asserted by that degenerate parameter case.

Constant nonzero curvature is included. Equation (9) then imposes
a=k^4/2, H=b/k, and (10) gives p=-1/k,q=0: precisely a centered circle.
Arbitrary b only rotates/reparametrizes its image, consistent with the
previous rotation redundancy rather than a new rotating noncircular example.

6. A C5 LOCAL COUNTEREXAMPLE TO A LOWER-REGULARITY CONVERSE

Fix a>0 and b!=0, and choose distinct real numbers Hminus,Hplus. On a small
neighborhood of0, take the two smooth local solutions of the regular system
(7) with initial data

              (k,v,w,H)(0)=(0,0,0,Hminus),
              (k,v,w,H)(0)=(0,0,0,Hplus).

Local existence and uniqueness follow directly from the polynomial vector
field. In each solution, evaluation and differentiation of (7) give

 k(0)=k'(0)=k''(0)=0,
 k'''(0)=-b,     k''''(0)=k'''''(0)=0,
                        k''''''(0)=-b*H(0).             (14)

For the last identity, k has order3 and k' has order2, so the third
derivative of k^2*k' vanishes at0; only k'''(0)H(0) survives in the third
derivative of kH. The a terms multiply zero lower jets of k.

Use the Hminus solution's k on s<=0 and the Hplus solution's k on s>=0.
The first five derivatives match, so the resulting k is C5. The unequal
sixth one-sided derivatives show that it is not C6. On both punctured
sides it satisfies (9) because it comes from (7). At0 all terms of (9)
vanish since k(0)=k'(0)=0, and the required fourth derivative exists and
is continuous. Therefore it is a genuine classical C5 solution of the
degenerate fourth-order equation on a neighborhood of0.

Yet k(s)=(-b/6)s^3+o(s^3) is nonzero for sufficiently small s!=0, and

        (F'(s)+b)/k(s)=Hminus(s) on the negative side,
        (F'(s)+b)/k(s)=Hplus(s) on the positive side.

The one-sided limits Hminus and Hplus differ. No continuous global H can
satisfy n=kH across0. This local example is not periodic and does not claim
an extra closed similarity profile. It shows precisely why one cannot use
only the apparent C4 regularity of the scalar equation to deduce the smooth
converse. C6 suffices by Section1, while C5 does not suffice even locally.

7. CONSEQUENCE AND REMAINING OPEN PROBLEM

Section4 of the earlier note may now be strengthened from simple inflections
to all smooth curvature functions. Multiple, flat or accumulating zeros are
not an unaddressed smooth loophole: finite-order zeros are derived, and flat
or accumulating zeros are excluded for every nonzero smooth scalar solution.

This is a complete equivalence/reconstruction reduction, not a classification
of the periodic solutions of (9) satisfying integer holonomy. It neither
constructs a new rotating closed profile nor proves all closed profiles are
nonrotating. The full SolitonClassification problem remains unresolved.
