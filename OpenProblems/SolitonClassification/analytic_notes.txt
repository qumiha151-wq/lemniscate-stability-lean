CLOSED SIMILARITY SOLUTIONS: FIXED-CENTER REDUCTION AND SYMMETRY OBSTRUCTIONS
Date: 2026-11-12
Status: analytic deductions, not a classification of all profile solutions.
No Lean verification or novelty claim is made for this note.

1. CONVENTIONS AND THE ACTUAL REMAINING QUESTION

Let beta: R/LZ -> R^2 be a smooth closed unit-speed immersion. Set
 T=beta_s, N=JT, T_s=kN, F=k_ss+k^3/2, E=(1/2) integral k^2 ds,
 p=beta.N, q=beta.T,
where J is rotation by +pi/2. The free elastic flow has normal speed -F.
Here self-similarity is allowed to include simultaneous rotations,
translations, dilations, and tangential reparametrizations. Restricting
the search from the outset to F=-a p excludes possible rotating expanders.

The result below reduces the general similarity problem to fixed-center
rotating expanders. It does NOT show that their rotation coefficient is
zero, nor classify all periodic profiles of the reduced ODE.

2. ELEMENTARY PERIODIC IDENTITIES

Direct differentiation gives p_s=-kq and q_s=1+kp. Therefore
 integral kp ds=-L, integral kq ds=0, integral kN ds=0.
The last identity is integral T_s=0.

Define the elastic stress Q=(k^2/2)T+k_s N. Then Q_s=F N. Consequently
 integral F N ds=0.
Integrating the derivative of Q.beta gives
 integral F p ds=-E.
For rotation, (Q.Jbeta)_s=Fq+k_s, because T.JT=0 and N.JT=1.
Thus integral Fq ds=0. These formulas hold for EVERY smooth closed
immersed curve, independently of a soliton equation.

In particular E>0: if E=0, continuity gives k=0, so T is constant and
beta(s+L)-beta(s)=LT is nonzero. This contradicts closure.
Likewise F is not identically zero, by integral Fp=-E.

3. KERNEL OF NORMAL SIMILARITY GENERATORS

Suppose that for constants a,b in R and c in R^2,
                 (a beta+b Jbeta+c).N=0                 (K)
at every parameter value. Multiply by k and integrate. The identities
above give -a L=0; hence a=0.

If b=0 and c is nonzero, (K) says T(s) is parallel to c for every s.
Continuity and |T|=1 force T to equal one fixed unit vector, contradicting
closure. Thus b=0 implies c=0.

If b is nonzero, put z=Jc/b. Then bJbeta+c=bJ(beta-z), so (K) implies
 (beta-z).T=0. Hence |beta-z|^2 is constant. The radius is positive since
beta is an immersion. Therefore beta is a multiply-covered round circle.

Conclusion: for a noncircular closed immersion the linear map
 (a,b,c) -> [s -> (a beta+b Jbeta+c).N]
is injective. For a round circle its kernel consists of infinitesimal
rotations about the circle center. This is a geometric kernel theorem,
not an assumption of generic nondegeneracy.

4. ALL NONCIRCULAR SIMILARITY FLOWS HAVE A FIXED CENTER

Assume a noncircular closed solution, as an immersion with multiplicity,
can be written up to a smooth time-dependent parameter diffeomorphism as
 gamma(s,t)=c(t)+r(t) R(theta(t)) beta(s), r(t)>0,
with differentiable similarity parameters. A time-dependent
reparametrization may first be removed, because it only changes the
tangential velocity. Curvature scales as r^-1, so the normal equation is

 F = -[(r^3 r') beta+(r^4 theta')Jbeta+r^3 R(-theta)c'].N.       (S)

By the injectivity in Section 3, all coefficients on the right are
independent of time. Write them as a,b,w. Thus
 r^3 r'=a, r^4 theta'=b, c'=r^-3 R(theta)w,
 F=-(a beta+b Jbeta+w).N.
Multiply this last identity by F and integrate using Section 2:
                 integral F^2 ds=aE.
Since both integrals are positive, a>0. This proves, in particular,
nonexistence of closed shrinking, stationary, rigidly translating, and
rigidly rotating free-elastic solitons.

Let A=aI+bJ. Since det A=a^2+b^2>0, define z=A^-1 w and beta0=beta+z.
Then F=-(A beta0).N. The physical expansion center
                 C=c(t)-r(t)R(theta(t))z
is independent of time, since (rR(theta))'=r^-3 R(theta)A.
Thus gamma=C+rR(theta)beta0.

For any reference time t0, letting r0=r(t0), theta0=theta(t0),
 r(t)^4=r0^4+4a(t-t0),
 theta(t)-theta0=(b/a)log(r(t)/r0).
The motion is a fixed-center logarithmic spiral expansion. In particular
every self-similar closed solution has exact quarter-power length growth,
even if b is nonzero. A counterexample to quarter-power growth cannot be
a similarity solution of this type.

For a circular profile the same conclusion about the images holds
directly: comparing the first Fourier modes of the circle's normal
equation fixes its physical center; its radius R obeys R'=1/(2R^3), so
R^4=R0^4+2(t-t0). An apparent rotation only reparametrizes a circle.

5. EXACT GRAM FORMULAS FOR THE ROTATION COEFFICIENT

Use the fixed-center profile beta0 and omit its subscript. Put
 P=integral p^2 ds, Q0=integral pq ds, R0=integral q^2 ds.
The soliton equation F=-ap-bq and Section 2 yield
                    aP+bQ0=E,
                    aQ0+bR0=0.
For a noncircular profile R0>0: R0=0 would imply q=0 and constant radius.
Moreover Delta=P R0-Q0^2>0. Equality in Cauchy-Schwarz would give
p=lambda q pointwise, hence (beta-lambda Jbeta).N=0, contrary to the
kernel theorem (its dilation coefficient is 1).
Therefore
             a=E R0/Delta, b=-E Q0/Delta, b/a=-Q0/R0.
Thus b=0 is EQUIVALENT to Q0=0 for an actual noncircular soliton.
Neither closure nor the general Noether identity integral Fq=0 alone
implies Q0=0. A proof excluding rotating expanders needs another argument.

6. REFLECTION SYMMETRY EXCLUDES GENUINE ROTATION

Suppose a noncircular profile admits an ambient reflection symmetry with
a smooth arclength reparametrization (the standard symmetry of an immersed
curve, including multiplicities). Reflecting its soliton equation changes
b to -b, preserves a, and reflects the fixed center. Pulling the reflected
curve back to the original parametrization gives a second normal
similarity generator for the same beta. Uniqueness from Section 3 gives
b=-b, hence b=0; it also places the fixed center on the reflection axis.

For an axis through the fixed center this can also be checked directly.
Under the orientation-reversing parameter symmetry one has
 p -> p, q -> -q, F -> F. Subtracting the two equations gives 2bq=0.
Noncircularity implies q is not identically zero, so b=0. For a reflection
whose parameter isometry preserves orientation, the transformations are
instead p -> -p, q -> q, F -> -F; comparison gives the same 2bq=0.

Consequently every genuinely rotating noncircular closed expander must
have no reflection symmetry. Symmetry-based fundamental-arc gluing by
reflections cannot find such a rotating profile.

7. CONVEX-HULL AND MIGRATION CRITERIA, INCLUDING ROTATION

Every fixed-center similarity flow has hull C+rR(theta)conv(beta).
If 0 lies in conv(beta), then C belongs to every hull. No two time slices
can then lie in opposite STRICT half-planes of the same fixed line.

Conversely, assume 0 is outside conv(beta). Strict separation supplies a
unit vector e and 0<m<=beta.e<=M.

For b=0, choose the initial rotation to be zero and translate the expansion
center so that C.e=-A with A>r0 M. The initial curve lies strictly in the
negative half-plane, whereas any later r>A/m puts it entirely in the
positive half-plane. A reflection reverses the requested direction.

For b!=0, take C=0 and theta0=0. Initially every beta.e is positive. When
the rotation has reached sign(b) pi, the entire curve lies in the negative
half-plane. The exact positive time increment is
             (r0^4/(4a)) [exp(4a*pi/|b|)-1].
Thus the existence of a strict-half-plane rotating expander is itself a
migration example, without an additional center translation. The
necessary-and-sufficient hull criterion extends to all the similarity
motions in Section 4 (with the usual freedom to translate initial data).

There is a useful symmetry obstruction. If a noncircular soliton admits
a nontrivial finite ambient rotation symmetry, uniqueness of the
generator forces its fixed center to equal that rotation's center. The
orbit of any profile point under that rotation has average equal to the
center, so the center lies in the convex hull. Such a profile cannot
provide migration by similarity. Therefore a strictly one-sided rotating
expander must have trivial Euclidean symmetry: reflection is excluded by
Section 6, and nontrivial finite rotations are excluded by this hull argument.
For an irrational rotation, the orbit is dense in a circle and its center
also belongs to the closed convex hull. Finally a compact image cannot
admit a nonzero translation or a glide-reflection symmetry, since iterating
either (or squaring a glide reflection) produces an unbounded orbit.

8. A NONSINGULAR REDUCED PROFILE SYSTEM AND CLOSURE

After scaling so a=1, write omega=b/a and use the four support variables.
The entire profile equation is equivalent to the autonomous polynomial ODE
 k'=v,
 v'=-k^3/2-p-omega q,
 p'=-kq,
 q'=1+kp.                                                    (ODE)
It is nonsingular even at inflection points k=0. Given a solution, choose
theta'=k, T=(cos theta,sin theta), N=JT and reconstruct beta=qT+pN.
Then beta'=T exactly, and F=-p-omega q.

A periodic ODE orbit of period L whose total curvature satisfies
integral_0^L k ds in 2pi Z reconstructs a closed smooth immersed profile:
T,N,p,q are all periodic. Conversely every closed profile yields such an
ODE orbit. This is an exact reduction to a periodic-orbit problem, NOT a
solution of that periodic-orbit classification.

Shooting constrained by reflection symmetry cannot detect a genuinely
rotating profile. The condition v(0)=0 alone remains an admissible phase
condition: periodic curvature has extrema. Alternatively one can fix a
radial extremum q(0)=0, leaving k(0),v(0),
p(0),omega,L unknown. The four periodic endpoint conditions and the
integer total-curvature condition are five closing equations. No
interval-certified chiral rotating root has been obtained in this note.

To convert a reconstructed profile into the strict normal-vector PDE
gauge, a time-dependent parameter diffeomorphism cancels the tangential
part of the similarity velocity. Its smooth vector field on the compact
parameter circle gives that diffeomorphism on every finite time interval.

9. LITERATURE AND LIMITS

Primary papers checked 2026-11-12:
 Miura--Wheeler, arXiv:2404.12619v3, Section 1.2 (the original questions).
 Andrews--Wheeler, Jellyfish exist, arXiv:2601.21227v1,
 https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21227v1 : constructs infinitely many
 homothetic expanders. It does not give all closed similarity profiles.
 Novaga, Non-migration for planar elastic flows, arXiv:2611.07242v1,
 https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07242v1 : its quantitative low-energy
 non-migration theorem does not cover arbitrary initial curves.

The deductions in Sections 3--8 are provided with proofs, but have not
been established to be new in the literature. They do not eliminate
omega!=0 or one-sided profiles, and do not settle unrestricted migration.
