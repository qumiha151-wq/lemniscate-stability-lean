VERDICT

The global divisibility theorem, all-inflection closed-profile converse,
and local C5 obstruction are correct as stated. I found no hidden simple-
zero assumption, unsupported closure condition, missing parameter case,
sign error, or derivative-order gap. The result is a reconstruction
equivalence; it does not classify the resulting periodic solutions.

1. REGULARITY AND THE MAXIMAL INTERVAL

For k in C6, F=k''+k^3/2 is C4, n=F'+b is C3, g=a-kF is C4,
and the globally defined H(s)=n(s0)/k(s0)+integral_s0^s g is C5.
Consequently r=n-kH is C3. Direct expansion gives
  k r' - k' r = k n' - k' n - k^2 H' = 0.
Thus r/k is constant on every nonzero-curvature interval. The anchor
makes this constant zero initially, so the maximal open interval I of
identically zero r containing s0 is nonempty.

At a finite endpoint z, continuity of r and its first three derivatives
gives r^(j)(z)=0 for j=0,1,2,3. The regular identity on I extends to z:
  k'''=kH-b-(3/2)k^2 k'.
If k(z)=0, its first nonzero derivative is k', k'', or, when b is nonzero
and both lower ones vanish, k'''=-b. The only omitted possibility would
be b=0 and (k,k',k'')(z)=(0,0,0). Treating the already globally defined
H as a continuous coefficient gives a regular, locally Lipschitz third-
order system. Its zero solution is unique backwards from z. Uniqueness
propagates across connected I and contradicts k(s0) nonzero. This uses
neither an ODE uniqueness theorem for the degenerate scalar equation nor
any assertion that its arbitrary solutions are already smooth.

Hence the endpoint zero has finite order m at most3. Taylor expansions
under C3 regularity give k~c(s-z)^m, c nonzero, and r=o(|s-z|^m).
Therefore r/k tends to zero at z and its constant on the adjacent side
must vanish. This contradicts maximality. Equivalently, on that side
r=Ck, and continuity of the m-th derivatives gives
  0=r^(m)(z)=C k^(m)(z),
so C=0. This alternative argument makes clear that only three derivatives
of r are used; smooth Hadamard division is not needed for the C6 theorem.
The same reasoning treats either finite endpoint. Thus I is all Real.

2. UNIQUENESS, PERIODICITY AND BOOTSTRAP

Two differentiable H satisfying the recovered identities have identical
derivative g, so differ by a constant. The nonzero anchor fixes that
constant. Here H' has its usual classical meaning; a formal statement
should use C1/HasDerivAt rather than a bare totalized derivative equality.

For periodic k, its derivatives, F, n and g are periodic. The period
difference H(s+L)-H(s) has zero derivative. The identity kH=n at one
nonzero anchor sets this constant difference to zero. Thus periodicity
of H is proved and no separate divisibility premise has been introduced.

The vector (k,k',k'',H) is already C1 and solves the displayed polynomial
first-order system. Repeated substitution/differentiation gives C-infinity
regularity. No analytic-regularity theorem is needed. At zeros, the same
regular uniqueness argument yields order at most2 for b=0 and at most3
for b nonzero. Isolated zeros are finite on a compact interval because
their zero set is closed. Flat zeros, zero intervals and accumulating zeros
are all excluded as consequences, not used as assumptions.

3. SUPPORT RECONSTRUCTION AND INTEGER HOLONOMY

For A=a^2+b^2 and a>0, the formulas
  p=(-aF-bH)/A, q=(-bF+aH)/A
give, using F'=kH-b and H'=a-kF,
  p'=-kq, q'=1+kp, F=-ap-bq.
With theta'=k, T=(cos theta,sin theta), N=JT, and beta=qT+pN,
  beta'=(q'-kp)T+(p'+kq)N=T.
This is an actual smooth unit-speed immersion with signed curvature k.
Integer holonomy over the specified period L makes T,N periodic. The
already proved periodicity of p,q then closes beta itself. No independent
integral-of-tangent closing condition is needed or assumed.

Conversely, for an actual closed unit-speed profile the support equations
produce H=aq-bp and the regular identities, and tangent periodicity gives
integer total curvature. A closed unit-speed curve cannot have k identically
zero. These observations establish the full smooth fixed-a,b equivalence.
The statement correctly distinguishes integer holonomy over L from a merely
periodic support orbit with noninteger angle change. Multiple covers and
a larger period are not incorrectly excluded.

4. PARAMETER AND CONSTANT-CURVATURE CASES

The divisibility theorem may use arbitrary a,b. The geometric reconstruction
uses A>0 and the intended expanding assumption a>0. When a=b=0, nonzero
periodic solutions of k''+k^3/2=0 illustrate why the support inverse cannot
be used: A=0. The source does not claim they give closed profiles.

For constant k nonzero, the scalar equation gives a=k^4/2 and H=b/k.
The reconstructed values are p=-1/k and q=0, a centered circle. Arbitrary
b is the usual rotational reparametrization of that circle. The identically
zero function must be excluded: it solves the scalar equation but gives no
closed unit-speed immersion; for b=0 it also destroys uniqueness of H.

5. C5 GLUING COUNTEREXAMPLE

For the regular polynomial system with initial data (k,v,w,H)=(0,0,0,H0)
and b nonzero, direct differentiation yields
  k0=k1=k2=0, k3=-b, k4=k5=0, k6=-b H0.
Indeed (kH)''' at zero is k''' H0, while (k^2 k')''' vanishes; all
other product terms have a zero lower jet of k. The a-dependent terms
first enter higher jets and do not change this identity.

Taking different H0 values on the two sides gives matching k jets through
order5 and different sixth jets. The glued curvature is C5 but not C6.
Away from zero the scalar equation holds by the regular ODE; at zero each
term vanishes since k=k'=0 and k'''' exists continuously. It is therefore
a classical solution of that fourth-order equation. Its leading term
(-b/6)s^3 makes k nonzero on both sufficiently small punctured sides, so
n/k equals the respective smooth H there and has distinct one-sided
limits. No continuous H can reconstruct this local solution.

This proves sharpness of C6 versus C5 for general local reconstruction.
It does NOT provide a periodic C5 example or a new closed profile, as the
source explicitly states. The regularity distinction is substantive.

OPTIONAL SHARPENING (NOT NEEDED BY THE REVIEWED THEOREM)

For b=0 the same proof already works under k in C5: r is C2 and every
possible endpoint zero has order at most2. A local C4 obstruction is obtained
by gluing regular solutions with common (k,v,w)(0)=(0,0,c), c nonzero,
but different H0. Their matching lower jets are k2=c,k3=k4=0 and
k5=cH0; hence k is C4 but not C5 and n/k has different limits. This
refinement is an analytic observation only and is not a further classified
periodic solution or a Lean theorem.

REMAINING SCOPE

The nonlinear periodic scalar equation with integer holonomy remains to
be solved/classified. This equivalence neither rules out all rotating
noncircular profiles nor constructs one. It does not address migration
or general long-time length growth.
