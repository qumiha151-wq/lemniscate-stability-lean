Generic point-matrix inverse checker, independent of grid evaluation
2026-11-13

PointMatrixInverse, PointMatrixCertificate and PointMatrixGridBudget are
frozen in PointMatrixInverseFrozenManifest.csv. Their actual final
compilations are sessions22052,4322,52096, respectively: all exited0,
all final logs are warning-free, and every recursively audited declaration
uses only the standard three Lean axioms. Earlier development failures
concerned explicit argument/coercion and simplifier handling, not a
failed candidate numerical inequality.

PointMatrixInverse first derives a genuine two-sided inverse of B from
units CB and BC. Neumann series certify those products when both actual
operator defects are bounded by q<1. It then identifies Ring.inverse B
with Ring.inverse(CB)*C and bounds its true operator norm. C is a proposed
matrix; it is never silently assumed equal to the actual inverse.

PointMatrixCertificate represents every entry by an integer divided by a
shared positive mesh. Indices are column first and row second. It proves
the full real matrix products agree with integer sums divided by mesh^2,
then converts full row-sum integer checks into actual operator norms.
Both left and right product defects are checked in the same orientation
as the genuine operator compositions. No floating point arithmetic or
JSON predicate is part of these proof hypotheses.

PointMatrixGridBudget fixes mesh1e60 and a decidable finite predicate:
row norm B<=2000, row norm C<=3999, and both product defects<=1e-20.
Its soundness theorem derives norm B<=2000, B a true unit, and
norm(Ring.inverse B)<=4000. This is a conditional finite checker, not
an assertion that any complete candidate grid passes it. Numerical point
instances and the one-leaf42/full22 benchmark are a separate subsequent
batch; all4000/8000 steps and the final root remain unfinished.
