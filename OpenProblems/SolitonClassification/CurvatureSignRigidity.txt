CURVATURE SIGN RIGIDITY AND A HALF-RMS NECESSARY CONDITION
Date: 2026-11-12. Human proof, independently checked algebraically by root.
No novelty claim. OneSignCurvature.lean now formalizes the stated subclass.

Theorem. Let beta:R/LZ -> R^2 be a smooth closed unit-speed immersion,
L>0, with T=beta_s, N=JT, k the signed curvature, p=beta.N, q=beta.T.
Suppose the actual similarity equation is
 F:=k_ss+k^3/2 = -a p-b q,                 a>0, b real.
Set B=int k^2 ds and R=sqrt(B/L)>0.
If k>=-R/2 everywhere, beta is a positively oriented multiply-covered round
circle centered at the similarity center0, with k=R and R^4=2a.
Likewise, if k<=R/2 everywhere, beta is a negatively oriented such circle.
Consequently EVERY noncircular closed similarity expander satisfies
           min k < -R/2,                 max k > R/2.
In particular every closed similarity expander whose curvature has one weak
sign is a multiply-covered circle. Neither embeddedness nor turning number1
is assumed. The conclusion includes nonrotating and rotating profiles; on a
circle the rotation coefficient changes only parametrization.

This is a partial classification and a necessary condition for strict
half-plane migration candidates. It does NOT rule out general sign-changing
profiles or prove nonmigration for arbitrary free elastic flows.

1. Derive the three integral identities from the actual support ODE

Write v=k_s. The actual scalar system is
 k'=v, v'=-k^3/2-a p-bq, p'=-kq, q'=1+kp.
All variables are L-periodic for a closed profile. Define F=-a p-bq.
Then k''+k^3/2=F, and integrating the v equation gives
                  int F ds = C/2,        C:=int k^3 ds.             (I)
The p,q equations give int kq=0 and int kp=-L, whence
                  int kF ds = aL.                                 (II)

The following polynomial primitives make the stress/Noether steps explicit:
 G = v p + (k^2/2)q,
 H = v q - (k^2/2)p - k.
Directly using the four differential equations,
 G'=Fp+k^2/2,                       H'=Fq.
Integrating over a period yields int Fp=-B/2 and int Fq=0. Therefore
                  D:=int F^2 ds = aB/2.                            (III)
This also derives the identities without supplying a stress tensor or any
integral equality as an extra hypothesis.

B>0. If B=0, continuity gives k=0, whereas (II) would give0=aL>0.

2. Moment bounds, valid for arbitrary curvature sign

Cauchy--Schwarz applied to (II) and (III) gives
 (aL)^2 <= B D = a B^2/2,
so                  B^2 >= 2aL^2.                                 (M1)
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (I) and (III) gives
                    C^2 <= 4LD=2aLB.                              (M2)
Since B,L>0, combining these inequalities yields
                    |C| <= B^(3/2)/sqrt(L) = L R^3.                (M3)
Thus the normalized third signed-curvature moment lies in[-1,1].
This last bound is NOT valid for all immersed curves; it uses the actual
similarity equation through all three identities.

3. Half-RMS rigidity using a nonnegative cubic

Assume k>=-R/2. The continuous integrand
              P(k)=(k+R/2)(k-R)^2
is nonnegative. Its algebraic expansion is
              P(k)=k^3-(3R/2)k^2+R^3/2.
As B=LR^2, its integral equals C-LR^3. By (M3) this is nonpositive.
Consequently the integral is zero, and continuity implies P(k(s))=0 for
every s. Hence k(s) belongs to the two-point set{-R/2,R}.
The parameter circle is connected, so continuous k is constant. The choice
-R/2 is inconsistent with its own RMS R>0. Thus k=R everywhere.
Replacing the orientation reverses k and proves the other assertion.
For a nonconstant curvature both inequalities in the theorem are therefore
strict, not weak. The case of constant nonzero curvature is handled below.

Alternative for the weaker one-sign statement: if k>=0, Holder gives
C>=B^(3/2)/sqrt(L); with(M2) this gives B^2<=2aL^2. Equality in the bounds
then forces k constant. The cubic argument is stronger and avoids Holder.

4. Locate the center and radius, rather than merely say constant curvature

Let k=c!=0. Then v=0 and the v equation gives ap+bq=-c^3/2. Differentiating
this relation using p'=-cq,q'=1+cp gives
                    bc p-ac q=-b.
The coefficient determinant is -c(a^2+b^2)!=0, so p and q are constant.
Then p'=-cq implies q=0, and q'=1+cp implies p=-1/c. Substitution in the
v equation gives c^4=2a. Reconstruction beta=qT+pN=-N/c has constant
distance1/|c| from0. Since T'=cN and N'=-cT, it traces the circle at unit
speed; closure forces cL in2piZ, giving a nonzero integer covering number.
This identifies the geometric circle and its similarity center.

5. Why the earlier balance counterexample does not invalidate this result

The quadrilateral in work/one_sided/migration/balance_not_hull_obstruction.txt
has edge-turn cross products (-4.3575,-1.9519,3.3156,.91), mixed signs, and
total signed turning0. It is self-intersecting and nonconvex; small compatible
rounding preserves the immersed turning class. More fundamentally, it was
constructed only to satisfy int pN=0. Nothing in that construction supplies
the additional identities(II)--(III), so it cannot defeat the moment proof.

6. Literature check and status

Focused searches of primary papers/pages on convex free-elastic expanders
did not locate an explicit theorem matching this statement. This bounded
search does not establish novelty. Sources checked include Miura--Wheeler
arXiv:2404.12619v3 (Section1.2 gives a small-oscillation rigidity consequence),
Andrews--Wheeler Jellyfish exist arXiv:2601.21227v1, and the primary paper
Gazwani--McCoy arXiv:2411.14806 / NoDEA32(2025),89. The last concerns open
curves in cones with smallness hypotheses; it is not an unrestricted convex
closed-profile classification theorem. No contraction or convexity-preserving
flow theorem has been imported into the proof above.
