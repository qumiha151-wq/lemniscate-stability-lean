INDEPENDENT ANALYTIC AUDIT OF similarity_reduction.txt
Date: 2026-11-12
Reviewer: length_new_approach subagent
Scope: the mathematical deductions in Sections 2--8. This is not a literature
priority review, not a Lean verification, and not a classification theorem.

OVERALL RESULT

I found no substantive error in the fixed-center reduction, the signs and
scalings of its equations, the Gram identities, or the migration time formula.
The conclusions are valid for smooth closed immersed profiles, with positive
length, and differentiable similarity parameters representing the immersion
up to smooth time-dependent reparametrization. They do not eliminate rotating
profiles or classify periodic orbits. Several wording/coverage details below
should be made explicit before these working notes are used as a theorem.

1. FORCE, VIRIAL, AND TORQUE IDENTITIES: CHECKED

For N=JT, p=beta.N, q=beta.T, direct differentiation gives
 p'=-kq, q'=1+kp.
Hence integral kp=-L and integral kq=0. With
 S=(k^2/2)T+k_s N,
 S_s=(k_ss+k^3/2)N=F N.
Thus integral F N=0. Also
 (S.beta)_s=Fp+k^2/2,
 (S.Jbeta)_s=Fq+k_s,
which give integral Fp=-E and integral Fq=0 with the stated signs. No soliton
hypothesis is used. A smooth closed immersion has E>0, and these identities
then force D=integral F^2>0.

2. KERNEL AND FIXED CENTER: CHECKED

The normal similarity generator a beta+bJbeta+c has zero normal component
only if a=0, by multiplication by k and periodic integration. For b=0, c!=0
would force the continuous unit tangent to be a fixed one of the two unit
vectors parallel to c, contradicting closure. For b!=0 the center is
z=Jc/b, since bJ(beta-z)=bJbeta+c. Orthogonality to N gives
(beta-z).T=0, hence constant positive radius. Smooth unit-speed immersion on
this circle has constant orientation and is a multiple cover of it.

In the similarity ansatz gamma=c+r R(theta)beta, normal velocity is
 R(-theta)c'.N + r' p + r theta' q,
whereas the scaled elastic force is r^(-3)F. Multiplication by r^3 gives
exactly the coefficients r^3r', r^4theta', r^3R(-theta)c' stated in (S).
For a noncircular profile, injectivity makes these coefficients constant.
The identity D=aE forces a>0. With A=aI+bJ and z=A^(-1)w,
beta0=beta+z and C=c-rR(theta)z have the correct signs; differentiating gives
C'=r^(-3)R(theta)(w-Az)=0. The logarithmic angle law and quarter-power radius
law follow exactly.

For a circle of physical center O(t) and radius R(t), comparison of constant
and first Fourier modes in the normal equation gives O'=0 and R'=1/(2R^3).
This also excludes circle translations and shrinking circles. Circular
rotations are changes of parametrization, so the noncircular injectivity
argument must not be applied to extract a unique b in that case.

3. GRAM FORMULAE: CHECKED

From F=-ap-bq one obtains aP+bQ0=E and aQ0+bR0=0. Noncircularity implies
R0>0. Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz would give p=lambda q pointwise and a
zero normal similarity generator with nonzero dilation coefficient, which
is impossible by the kernel calculation. Thus Delta>0 and the formulas
a=E R0/Delta, b=-E Q0/Delta are valid. The note correctly avoids inferring
Q0=0 from torque balance alone.

4. REFLECTIONS: MAIN CONCLUSION CHECKED; ADD BOTH PARAMETER ORIENTATIONS

Let H(x)=Sx+d be an affine reflection, det S=-1. Reflecting the geometric
normal-flow equation changes A=aI+bJ to SAS^(-1)=aI-bJ and the center C to
H(C). If H preserves the immersed profile up to an arclength isometry of
its parameter circle, the reflected generator is another generator of the
same profile. Injectivity gives b=-b, hence b=0 and H(C)=C.

The secondary pointwise check in Section 6 treats a parameter reversal only.
An ambient reflection symmetry of an immersed curve can also preserve the
orientation of the parameter circle (a figure eight supplies examples).
This does not invalidate the main proof, which works in both cases, but it
is worth making the distinction explicit. If h has derivative epsilon=+-1
and beta(h(s))=S beta(s), for an axis through the center one has

  p(h)=-epsilon p, q(h)=epsilon q, F(h)=-epsilon F.

For epsilon=-1 these are exactly the transformations in the note. For
epsilon=+1 they are p->-p, q->q, F->-F. Either case yields 2bq=0 on comparing
the original and reflected soliton equations, so b=0 for a noncircle.

5. HULL CRITERION AND ROTATING MIGRATION TIME: CHECKED, WITH SCOPE

If 0 belongs to conv(beta), the same fixed C belongs to the hull of every
time slice; strict opposite half-planes therefore cannot contain two slices.
If 0 is outside the compact hull, strict separation gives 0<m<=beta.e<=M.
For b=0, the chosen translation C.e=-A, A>r0 M, makes the initial profile
strictly negative; r>A/m makes it strictly positive. For b!=0, C=0 and
theta0=0 put the initial profile strictly positive and a rotation by
sign(b) pi puts it strictly negative, independent of its growing size.
From theta-theta0=(b/a)log(r/r0), the elapsed time is exactly

  r0^4/(4a) * [exp(4a pi/|b|)-1] > 0.

The iff statement concerns whether SOME translate/placement of the profile
produces migration. It is not an iff statement about every already placed
flow: a nonrotating positive-half-plane profile with C=0 does not change
half-planes. The existing parenthetical qualification in the note expresses
this correctly and should be retained in any final theorem statement.

The finite rotation symmetry obstruction is correct: uniqueness makes C the
rotation center, and the average of any finite orbit places C in the hull.
The final phrase 'trivial Euclidean symmetry' uses two additional elementary
compactness facts not supplied there: a compact set cannot have a nonzero
translation or glide-reflection symmetry; an irrational rotation symmetry
puts its center in the hull as well. For the latter, no classification of the
profile image is needed: take the orbit of any point, use density on its circle
and closedness/convexity of the hull, and average antipodal limit points.
Thus the same no-migration argument covers ALL nontrivial rotation symmetries,
not only finite ones. This is a short gap in exposition, not a false claim.

6. PROFILE ODE AND RECONSTRUCTION: CHECKED

If beta is scaled by lambda=a^(1/4), its new coefficient is a/lambda^4=1
and its new rotational coefficient is b/lambda^4=b/a. Thus the stated
normalization is legitimate and the ratio omega=b/a is invariant.

Given the four-variable ODE and theta'=k, differentiating beta=qT+pN gives
 beta'=(q'-kp)T+(p'+kq)N=T.
Consequently this is a unit-speed immersion of curvature k, with actual
F=v'+k^3/2=-p-omega q. Periodicity of k,v,p,q and an integral of k in 2pi Z
make T,N and beta periodic with all derivatives. Conversely any closed
profile has these properties. The system is nonsingular at k=0.

An exact profile constructed this way gives the self-similar normal-flow
family. If the original FEF is required in the strictly normal vector gauge,
a smooth time-dependent reparametrization must cancel its tangential speed;
the compact parameter circle gives that reparametrization on each finite
time interval. This is a routine but separate link to include when claiming
an actual PDE solution from an ODE root.

7. TWO WORDING CHANGES RECOMMENDED

(a) The phrase 'images ... can be written' should mean equivalence as immersed
curves up to a smooth parameter diffeomorphism, not mere equality of sets.
For self-intersecting images, set equality alone does not in general specify
the order and multiplicity of traversed arcs. The displayed differentiable
ansatz is sufficient for every deduction, so formulate the hypothesis using
that ansatz directly.

(b) 'Reflection shooting with v(0)=0 is inappropriate' should distinguish
reflection gluing from the scalar condition v(0)=0. Every nonconstant smooth
periodic curvature has an extremum, so one may ALWAYS choose v(0)=0 as a phase
condition, including for a putative rotating profile. It is imposed reflection
symmetry that rules out b!=0, not v(0)=0 itself. Suggested replacement:
 'Shooting constrained by reflection symmetry cannot detect a genuinely
 rotating profile. The condition v(0)=0 alone is still an admissible phase
 condition; alternatively one may choose the radial extremum q(0)=0.'

CONCLUSION AND LIMITS

The reduction genuinely narrows classification to periodic orbits of a
polynomial ODE with a total-curvature closure constraint, while retaining
possible rotating expanders. No deduction reviewed here proves omega=0,
provides a chiral periodic orbit, or settles unrestricted migration. There is
no substantive mathematical counterexample to the stated conditional results
in this audit; the representation hypothesis and the noted wording fixes are
the material caveats.
