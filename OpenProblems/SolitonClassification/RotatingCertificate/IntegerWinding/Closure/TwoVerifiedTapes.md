# What remains to certify the one-turn rotating curve

The Lean theorem [closed_immersion_of_certified_tapes](../../../SolitonClassification/Rotating/IntegerTwoArmImmersion.lean) reduces existence of this particular closed rotating expander to two finite tables. Each table has 4,000 polynomial cells. They advance from the same rounded initial state in opposite directions. The theorem requires every cell and its interfaces to pass the integer arithmetic checks, the initial rows to equal the stated initial node and the sums of the last polynomial coefficients to equal the two stated terminal rows. It also accounts for the rounding error between the exact initial state and the common initial node.

Under precisely those hypotheses, there is a smooth closed arclength-parametrized immersion, with period between 60 and 62 and signed turning number \(-1\) on that period. The curve has curvature exceeding \(7\) at the initial arclength label and negative curvature somewhere else. Its equation is \(k_{ss}+k^3/2=-X\cdot N-bX\cdot T\), where \(0.10<b<0.11\) and \(bX\cdot T\ne0\) at the initial label. Thus a zero of the five-dimensional shooting equation yields the asserted geometric curve.

The parameter cube allows the half-period itself to vary by \(10^{-30}\). Lean identifies the table solution with a single autonomous two-sided flow, differentiates that flow jointly in time and the initial state, and follows the additional signed time from the fixed table endpoint. It proves that both moving endpoint states stay within \(10^{-12}\) of the rational proxies throughout the cube and within \(10^{-50}\) at the centre. Each of the five spatial derivative columns is within \(10^{-11}\) per projected entry. The existence and uniqueness step uses the exact angle \(2\pi\).
