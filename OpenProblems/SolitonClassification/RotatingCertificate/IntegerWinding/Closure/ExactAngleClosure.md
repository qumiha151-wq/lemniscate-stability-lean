# Exact one-turn angle in the rotating-profile shooting equation

Take the five parameters \(c=(k_0,p_0,\ell,\alpha,b)\) and the phase line \(e\) given by the rational constants in the Lean data. If \(\Phi\) is the normalized support flow, the two endpoints occur at times \(+\ell\) and \(-\ell\) from the same initial state \((k_0,\alpha e_0/8,p_0,\alpha e_1,b,0)\). Take their difference in \((k,8u,p,q,\theta)\) and add \((0,0,0,0,2\pi)\). A zero matches the four support coordinates and gives total angle \(-2\pi\). The constant coordinate \(b\) agrees along both arms of the physical ODE.

Lean proves the following certificate for that equation. In the closed supremum ball of radius \(10^{-30}\) about the specified rational centre there is a unique zero provided that:

1. The two true endpoints at the centre each differ by at most \(10^{-50}\) in supremum norm from their stated rational proxies.
2. The flow has its indicated joint derivative at the two endpoint inputs throughout the ball.
3. The full derivative of the five-component residual throughout the ball differs from the specified rational matrix \(J\) by at most \(10^{-8}\) in operator norm.

All other bounds in this step are proved in Lean. The two dyadic endpoints and their five variational columns determine \(J\). The moving half-length differentiates in opposite time directions at the two ends, so the length column uses the **sum** of their vector fields. The phase column uses both components of the phase line. A concrete rational matrix \(B\) has \(\|B\|\le13\) and \(\|I-BJ\|\le10^{-12}\). The rational residual at the centre is at most \(10^{-40}\); replacing the rational angle by the exact \(2\pi\) changes it by at most \(2\cdot10^{-56}\). One hundred iterations of the nested half-angle square root prove the two rational bounds for \(\pi\) in the Lean kernel. Banach's theorem now gives the unique zero under the three stated hypotheses.

For a physical flow one can verify the derivative enclosure from the two arms individually. At every point of the parameter cube each true endpoint must lie within \(10^{-12}\) of its rational state. Each of its five initial-value derivative columns must agree with the rational endpoint data to \(10^{-11}\) per projected component. The derivative in endpoint time equals the original ODE field. Lean derives the full five-parameter Jacobian from these conditions, including the change in both endpoint times and the two entries of the phase direction.
