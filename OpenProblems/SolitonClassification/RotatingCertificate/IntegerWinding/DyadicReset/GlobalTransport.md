# Moving-frame verification of the winding-one shooting calculation

The normalized cells have length one in their own parameter. The two signed physical steps are the exact rational half-period divided by 4000 and its negative. The reference on a cell is a dyadic state polynomial Q and a six-by-six dyadic matrix polynomial M. Their integer residual certificates imply uniform bounds of 10⁻⁶⁴ for the six state coordinates and 6·10⁻⁶⁴ in matrix operator norm. The matrix is the complete linearized reference, including the constant angle column and the parameter row.

## Transport across an arbitrary number of cells

Lean proves that a rational approximate inverse at the left endpoint suffices for invertibility throughout the cell. It differentiates B(t) = M(t) M(0)⁻¹ − I, uses ‖h DF(Q)‖ ≤ 4/25 and the variational residual, and bounds the maximum of ‖B(t)‖ on the closed interval by 1/5. A Neumann estimate then bounds both ‖M(t)‖ and ‖M(t)⁻¹‖ by 5000. The left endpoint assumptions are supplied by finite integer row sums and two matrix products.

For the original nonlinear solution x, the moving error is M⁻¹(x−Q). A quantitative local existence theorem constructs the true solution for either sign of the time step. Its moving error has an additive one-cell bound. At every interface both Q and M match exactly, and the next interval starts at the previous true terminal value. Lean carries this recurrence across an arbitrary finite number of certified cells. For 4000 cells the uniform physical state error is at most 5·10⁻⁵² for a centre mismatch at most 10⁻⁷¹, or 5·10⁻²⁶ for an initial mismatch at most 2·10⁻³⁰ in the moving coordinates.

The linearized equation is solved along that same nonlinear solution. Writing J = M U yields a small linear coefficient for U. Lean reconstructs J, checks J′ = h DF(x) J, proves exact matching of J at interfaces, and checks the budgets ‖J−M‖ ≤ 3·10⁻³⁸ at the centre and ≤ 3·10⁻¹² in the parameter cube over 4000 cells. The initial matrix is the starting reference matrix. The differentiability proof uses the exact cubic field expansion, a one-cell quadratic error estimate and finite induction to identify J with the Fréchet derivative of the endpoint whenever the initial matrix is the identity and the input states range over an open neighborhood. For time variations |τ| ≤ 10⁻³⁰, the short-tail theorem propagates the original equation and its linearization from the fixed endpoint and bounds their changes by 110|τ| and 400080|τ|.
