## A whole-period trigonometric enclosure

In the arclength variables $Y=(k,v,p,q,b)$ of the shooting proof, $v=k_s/8$, $p=X\cdot N$, $q=X\cdot T$, with fixed pitch $b$. The vector field is $(8v,-k^3/16-(p+bq)/8,-kq,1+kp,0)$. Set $s=\lambda_*\xi$ with $\lambda_*=l_*/\pi$, where $2l_*$ is the true period. Each of the four nonconstant comparison coordinates is a real trigonometric polynomial $\hat Y(\xi)$ of degree 3200. Its complex coefficients, $\hat b$ and $\hat\lambda$ are represented by integers with denominator $10^{42}$. There is no requirement of interval integration inside the checker for the polynomial defect: ordinary integer polynomial products give **all** Fourier coefficients of $\hat Y_\xi-\hat\lambda f(\hat Y)$ up to frequency 9600. The sum of absolute real and imaginary parts for each component is below $5\cdot10^{-36}$. The zeroth-mode pitch has identically zero defect.

Here is how the Fourier enclosure is transferred to the actual orbit. We use the already validated shooting root in the cube of radius $10^{-30}$. The [existing full-interval fundamental matrix estimates](../IntegerRotatingWinding.md) give both $\|P(s)\|,\|P(s)^{-1}\|\le 80000$ on each signed half interval of the true orbit based at its initial point. The 5-dimensional autonomous subsystem inherits the bounds from the 6-dimensional system since the first five coordinates are independent of the angle. This also holds for the leading block of the inverse. The comparison point at zero and its pitch differ by less than $10^{-38}$ from the frozen shooting centre data, and $|\hat\lambda\pi-l_0|<10^{-38}$. Both checks are performed when decoding the witness. Allowing the entire root cube gives initial state error below $2\cdot10^{-30}$.

Compare at **equal signed physical arclength** first, using $\hat Y(s/\hat\lambda)$ as the comparison curve. Its defect is below $5\cdot10^{-36}/9$ per unit arclength. If the state error has maximum norm at most $\epsilon=10^{-22}$ so far, the quadratic remainder of the field expanded along the true orbit is at most $2\epsilon^2$, using the Hessian bound four and the previously validated spare margin in the tube. Multiplication by the inverse true fundamental matrix followed by integration gives the uniform error bound
\[
 80000(2\cdot10^{-30})+31(80000)^2(5\cdot10^{-36}/9+2\epsilon^2)<\epsilon .
\]
First contact therefore cannot occur on either half interval. Signed time reversal preserves the norm bounds. For evaluation at a common normalized angle use $s=\lambda_*\xi$, $|\xi|\le\pi$. Since $|l_*-\pi\hat\lambda|<2\cdot10^{-30}$, shifting the argument of the comparison by this amount in physical time changes it by less than $2\cdot10^{-28}$. On the overlap its speed is bounded by 90 plus the defect. At a possibly longer endpoint the trigonometric polynomial extends smoothly, and the same speed bound with 91 in place of 90 holds by first exit from the spare tube. The final uniform coordinate error is less than $10^{-19}$. Periodicity extends this statement across the chosen cut.

## Counting all zeros between samples

The [replay](replay.py) evaluates the four finite Fourier polynomials at all 65536 equally spaced angles using complex ball arithmetic at 140 bits, adds the uniform transfer error, and encloses the continuous orbit between successive nodes. The physical length of a grid cell is at most $61/65536$. A bound of 90 on the velocity gives the first enclosure. Three substitutions of the enclosed state into the explicit vector field narrow the enclosure. Each such substitution remains valid on the entire cell by the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Every node enclosure excludes zero in every coordinate. For each coordinate and each intervening cell, either the enclosure excludes zero, or the derivative enclosure excludes zero. In the latter case strict monotonicity and the two endpoint signs decide whether there is exactly one transversal zero. This includes the final wraparound cell. The [output](shape_report.json) records each individual crossing cell. The smallest certified magnitude of a physical derivative in a crossing cell exceeds 0.37. Since $k_s=8v$, simple zeros of $v$ give nondegenerate curvature extrema. Since $(|X|^2/2)_s=q$ and $X$ never hits the centre, simple zeros of $q$ give nondegenerate extrema of the radius. The tangent line through $X$ contains the origin exactly when $p=0$.

The fixed integer Fourier data were obtained by numerical fitting, then checked independently of that fit. The proof uses exact integer arithmetic, interval evaluation, and the prior analytic shooting certificate. These global counts have not been independently replayed in the Lean kernel. They classify the features of this particular primitive orbit.
