1. FIELD, SCALING, AND LOCAL COEFFICIENT MAJORANTS

The normalized state u=v/8 gives exactly
 (k',u',p',q',b',theta')=(8u,-k^3/16-p/8-bq/8,-kq,1+kp,0,k).
This is the actual support ODE with dilation coefficient 1. On the specified
outer tube the infinity norm bounds 75,17,4 for f,Df,D^2f are valid. In
particular the u row of Df has sum 14.736875; its bilinear Hessian row bound
is 3.4375. Both p and q Hessian row bounds are 2.

The expansion about any allowed center is coefficientwise dominated by
75+17w+2w^2+w^3/16, and 75(1+w/10)^3 has respective coefficients
75,22.5,2.25,.075, all large enough. Its scalar solution is
w=10((1-15t)^(-1/2)-1). Therefore the state tail
10*(15|h|)^61/(1-15|h|) after degree 60 is valid. This is a convergent
power-series existence argument, not a posterior assertion of small error.
It works in both time directions by absolute coefficient majorization.
The unrestricted theta coordinate causes no field-bound issue because the
field is independent of theta.

For the matrix equation the coefficient norm of Df(X) is bounded by
17+4w+(3/16)w^2, which is in turn dominated by g'(w).
The scalar fundamental majorant is (1-15t)^(-3/2), whose coefficient is
(2n+1)*binom(2n,n)*15^n/4^n <= (2n+1)*15^n. Summing its tail yields exactly
s^61*(123/(1-s)+2s/(1-s)^2), s=15|h|. Multiplication by ||M0|| is required
and is included. This is the matrix operator infinity norm, not an
entrywise estimate with a missing dimension factor.

2. IMPLEMENTATION OF STATE AND MATRIX RECURRENCES

The state recurrence is the coefficient recurrence of the displayed field.
The matrix recurrence differentiates all its nonlinear terms. In particular
the b-sensitivity term -q[n]/8 in the u row is present. Updating the state
coefficient of degree n+1 before the matrix coefficient of degree n+1 is
harmless: the latter only uses state coefficients through degree n.

The stored 5-by-5 block is completed to the full 6-by-6 matrix. Its b row
is exactly the b-coordinate unit row, and its initial-theta column is the
theta-coordinate unit column. These are exact invariants of the augmented
ODE, so no parameter sensitivity has been omitted. Norms and inverses are
computed on that completed matrix at every grid point, including the final
point. All acceptance tests use Arb inequalities; floating-point conversions
are confined to progress diagnostics or the choice of a subsequently
validated rational preconditioner.

3. LOCAL TUBE, ROUNDING, AND RESET ERRORS

The four inner-to-outer margins after .008 units of worst-case coordinate
motion are .072, .0592675, .612, and .604. All exceed .05. This validates
the first-exit tube argument for each exact local segment. The b coordinate
is constant. Initial decimal-to-dyadic rounding is explicitly included.

The radius of each finite polynomial evaluation is recorded before replacing
it by its exact dyadic midpoint. State reset error is bounded by that radius
plus the state analytic tail. Matrix reset error is bounded by the row-sum
of entry radii plus ||M_j|| times the analytic matrix tail. The code records
both rounding sums and these full jump sums. The checker independently uses
the larger uniform tail budgets, rather than relying only on the latter sums.
Serialization is through Arb ball strings, preserving containment.

The step is constructed from the exact center decimal half-length divided by
4000. The revised checker verifies that each stored step ball contains that
signed rational step, as well as checking sign, order, precision, completion,
and equality of both center/phase lists.

4. GLOBAL METRIC BOOTSTRAP AND ENTIRE PARAMETER CUBE

For w=M^{-1}(x-X), differentiating on each exact reference segment cancels
the linear error, leaving ||w'|| <= (I*4*K^2/2)||w||^2. At a reset the
correct multiplier is Id+M_new^{-1}(M_old_end-M_new), and the correct
additive term is M_new^{-1}(X_old_end-X_new). Both are included.
Discrete Gronwall therefore gives the displayed (sigma+T*C*R^2)exp(epsilon)
bound. Initially M=Id, so the initial error d0 is correctly unmultiplied;
later state jumps carry the inverse bound I.

The strict bootstrap inequality holds for R=1e-46. For every initial state
coming from the parameter cube, ||Dz||<=1 and R_u=2e-32 give the second
strict bootstrap. The time-coordinate change contributes at most 75e-32
additional displacement. The spare tube margin is far larger than these
errors. All non-angular coordinates are bounded, and theta'=k with theta(0)=0
gives |theta|<=8.5*32=272. Thus the compact-set continuation step applies
to the full polynomial ODE. Strict margins also give existence on an open
neighborhood of the closed parameter cube, as needed for derivatives.

5. VARIATIONAL ERROR, INVERSE BOUND, AND SHOOTING HESSIAN

For V=M^{-1}P the coefficient norm is <=I*4*K^2*R. Its jump matrices are
the same ones used above. The product/integral norm estimates give
||V-Id||<=exp(T*I*4*K^2*R+epsilon)-1. Every inverse jump exists because
each jump norm is below epsilon<1/4; (1-d)^(-1)<=exp(2d) justifies the
inverse product estimate. With R_u, the forward and inverse bounds are
uniform over the entire parameter cube, not only along the center trajectory.
The extra terminal-time interval accounts for the additional factor 2.
Consequently ||D_z Phi||<=4K and ||(D_z Phi)^(-1)||<=4I are justified.

The second-variation integral formula is valid for this smooth polynomial
flow. With |t|<32 it yields
32*(4K)*(4I)*4*(4K)^2. Mixed initial/time and double-time derivatives are
bounded by 17*(4K) and 17*75. The linear initial-data map has norm at most
1 and zero second derivative. The two time branches and output projection
of norm 8 produce the factor 16. The resulting expression is
16777216000004372400 < 1e20. Thus the Hessian bound really is uniform on
the cube, and gives ||DF(c)-DF(c0)||<=1e20*r with no dimension factor lost.

The center shooting Jacobian has the correct length column f(C+)+f(C-).
Its other columns use (M+-M-)Dz. The bound 16D+16*17E covers their combined
operator error; inflating every entry by that whole bound is conservative.
The residual inflation 16E likewise covers both branches and output scaling.
The irrational target 112*pi/57 is obtained from the Arb pi enclosure.

6. BANACH AND THE ACTUAL GEOMETRIC CURVE

The finite-decimal matrix B is a legitimate exact rational preconditioner.
Its floating-point origin has no logical role after validating its nonzero
determinant, norm, and the residual/Jacobian inequalities. The checker gives
 q <= about 1.732e-7,
 eta <= 2.60e-40,
 eta+q*r < r=1e-32.
These certify a contraction of the closed infinity-norm cube into itself.
Completeness gives a fixed point, and invertibility of B makes it F(c)=0.
There is no unstated assumption that the numerical center is already a root.

At this exact root the four support variables match at -l and l. Polynomial
ODE uniqueness permits a smooth periodic extension with period 2l. The exact
angle increment is -112*pi/57. With T=(cos theta,sin theta), N=JT and
beta=qT+pN, direct differentiation gives beta'=T and signed curvature k.
After 57 support periods the angle increment is -112*pi=-56*(2*pi), so
both position and all derivatives agree. This is an actual closed immersion,
not just closure of the tangent or support variables. Its turning number
is -56. The strict bound v'(0)<-200 rules out constant curvature, and b>.009
rules out zero rotational coefficient.

The stated lambda(t)=(1+4t)^(1/4) and angle=(b/4)log(1+4t) have normal
velocity lambda^(-3)(p+bq), equal to minus the scaled force. The remaining
tangential velocity can be removed by a smooth periodic reparametrization.
This sign and scaling check supports the rotating expanding-profile claim.
The rotational symmetry also explains why no strict-half-plane migration
conclusion follows from this example.

IndependentRecheckCertificate.json is byte-identical to the authoritative

independent rechecks are the evidence actually reviewed here.
