\begin{abstract}
This note records partial deductions concerning the questions in Section 1.2 of Miura--Wheeler, arXiv:2404.12619v3. It is not a solution of all those questions. For every smooth closed solution we derive a restart estimate, sub-square-root length and centroid growth, and divergence of length. Uniform boundedness of normalized energy is sufficient for two-sided quarter-power growth. A one-sided homothetic profile gives both migration and an escaping centroid, but existence of such a closed expander is not proved. The accompanying Lean files verify scalar analytic and geometric reductions; they do not formalize the underlying geometric PDE or settle the remaining existence and classification problems. No novelty or priority claim is made for these elementary deductions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and literature status}
The supplied manuscript treats local stability of each finite cover of Bernoulli's lemniscate. We use that to identify the already addressed question, but none of the deductions below depends on its spectral certificate. The remaining questions concern classification of self-similar solutions, possible growth different from quarter-power growth, unbounded centroid displacement, and complete migration between opposite half-planes.

Andrews--Wheeler \cite{AW} construct infinitely many geometrically distinct closed homothetic expanders with dihedral symmetry. Their introduction explicitly leaves complete classification and rigorous existence of the illustrated nonsymmetric one-sided examples open. These results therefore rule out a proposed classification consisting only of circles and lemniscates; they do not provide a complete classification. Dihedral symmetry makes the arclength centroid equal to the expansion center.

Novaga \cite{N}, posted 4 November 2026, proves non-migration for free and length-preserving flows under
\[
 L(0)\int k(0)^2\,ds\le 4\pi^2+\frac83.
\]
This is a restricted-energy theorem, not a resolution for arbitrary initial curves. The source is currently identified as a preprint; no journal publication is asserted here. Its free-flow time convention is twice that of \cite{MW}, which does not affect non-migration. Our search did not locate a complete resolution of the remaining unrestricted questions; that search is not a proof that no such publication exists.

\section{Dissipation and length}
Let $\gamma$ be a smooth immersed closed curve evolving by
\[
 \gamma_t=-F\nu,\qquad F=k_{ss}+\tfrac12 k^3,
 \qquad E=\tfrac12\int k^2\,ds,\quad L=\int ds,
 \quad D=\int F^2\,ds.
\]
Tangential reparametrizations do not change the quantities below. The standard variation identities give
\begin{equation}\label{basic}
 E'=-D,\qquad L'=\int kF\,ds,\qquad
 (L')^2\le2ED=-(E^2)'.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Restart bound and sub-square-root growth]\label{restart}
For $0\le s\le t$,
\begin{equation}\label{secant}
 |L(t)-L(s)|^2\le(t-s)(E(s)^2-E(t)^2).
\end{equation}
In particular $L(t)\le L(0)+E(0)\sqrt t$ and $L(t)=o(\sqrt t)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Integrate $L'$ on $[s,t]$ and apply Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{basic}:
\[
 |L(t)-L(s)|^2\le(t-s)\int_s^t(L')^2\,du
 \le(t-s)\int_s^t2ED\,du.
\]
The last integral is $E(s)^2-E(t)^2$. Since $E$ decreases to a limit $E_\infty\ge0$, for fixed $s$ we obtain
\[
 \limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{L(t)}{\sqrt t}
 \le\sqrt{E(s)^2-E_\infty^2}.
\]
Let $s\to\infty$. This does not require $E_\infty=0$.
\end{proof}

The Lean proof of the analytic core of \eqref{secant} avoids integration. For continuous functions $L,H$ with interior derivatives $l,h$ and $l^2\le-h$, put $a=(L(t)-L(s))/(t-s)$. Then
\[
 \frac{d}{du}\bigl(H(u)+2aL(u)-a^2u\bigr)
 =h(u)+2al(u)-a^2\le-(l(u)-a)^2\le0.
\]
Endpoint comparison gives $(L(t)-L(s))^2\le(t-s)(H(s)-H(t))$. Setting $H=E^2$ gives the desired application once the geometric identities are supplied. The formal theorem states all these scalar assumptions explicitly.

\begin{proposition}[Virial estimate]\label{virial}
At every time,
\begin{equation}\label{virialbound}
 D\ge\frac{4\pi^2 E^2}{L^3}\ge\frac{16\pi^6}{L^5},
 \qquad EL\ge2\pi^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Frenet identities imply
\[
 \partial_s\bigl(k_s\nu+\tfrac12k^2\tau\bigr)=F\nu.
\]
For any constant vector $p$, integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{virialidentity}
 \int F\langle\gamma-p,\nu\rangle\,ds=-E.
\end{equation}
Choose $p=L^{-1}\int\gamma\,ds$. The periodic Wirtinger inequality and $|\gamma_s|=1$ give
\[
 \int|\gamma-p|^2\,ds\le\frac{L^2}{4\pi^2}\int|\gamma_s|^2\,ds
 =\frac{L^3}{4\pi^2}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz in \eqref{virialidentity} proves the first inequality. Fenchel's total absolute curvature bound and Cauchy--Schwarz give
$4\pi^2\le(\int|k|\,ds)^2\le2EL$, proving the others.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Divergence of length]
Every smooth closed free elastic flow satisfies $L(t)\to\infty$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise there are $M<\infty$ and $t_n\to\infty$ with $L(t_n)\le M$. The restart bound gives $L(u)\le M+E(0)$ on $[t_n,t_n+1]$. Hence \eqref{virialbound} implies
\[
 E(t_n)-E(t_n+1)=\int_{t_n}^{t_n+1}D(u)\,du
 \ge\frac{16\pi^6}{(M+E(0))^5}>0.
\]
The left side tends to zero, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A lower bound for the running maximum]
Set $M(t)=\max_{0\le u\le t}L(u)$ and $a=2\pi^2/E(0)$. Then
\[
 M(t)^4\ge a^4+8\pi^4t.
\]
This is not a pointwise quarter-power lower bound for $L(t)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{virialbound}, $(1/E)'=D/E^2\ge4\pi^2/L^3$ and $L\ge2\pi^2/E$. Thus
\[
 L(t)\ge a+8\pi^4\int_0^tL(u)^{-3}\,du
 \ge a+\frac{8\pi^4t}{M(t)^3}.
\]
Since $M\ge L(t)$ and $M\ge a$, multiplication by $M^3$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional quarter-power growth]\label{conditional}
Assume additionally $E(t)L(t)\le K<\infty$ for every $t\ge0$. Then
\begin{align*}
 L(t)&\le\max\{2L(0),\,2\sqrt K\,t^{1/4}\},\\
 L(t)&\ge2\pi^2\left(E(0)^{-4}+\frac{16\pi^2}{K^3}t\right)^{1/4}.
\end{align*}
Consequently $L(t)\asymp t^{1/4}$ as $t\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $L(t)>2L(0)$, continuity supplies $s\in(0,t)$ with $L(s)=L(t)/2$. No monotonicity of length is used. The restart estimate gives
\[
 \frac{L(t)^2}{4}\le tE(s)^2\le\frac{4K^2t}{L(t)^2},
\]
which proves the upper bound. For the lower bound, $L\le K/E$ and \eqref{virialbound} imply $D\ge4\pi^2E^5/K^3$. Thus $(E^{-4})'=4D/E^5\ge16\pi^2/K^3$. Integrate, then use $L\ge2\pi^2/E$.
\end{proof}
The unresolved step is a uniform bound for $EL$ for arbitrary initial data, or another argument or counterexample determining the unrestricted growth. Theorem~\ref{conditional} does not establish such a bound.

\section{Centroid and homothetic migration}
Define the arclength centroid $c=L^{-1}\int\gamma\,ds$. Translation invariance, or the stress identity above, gives $\int F\nu\,ds=0$. Because $(ds)_t=kF\,ds$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{centroid}
 c'=\frac1L\int(\gamma-c)kF\,ds.
\end{equation}
In particular, vanishing average velocity does not imply a constant centroid: the measure itself changes.

For any arclength point $s_0$, the average of the shorter intrinsic distance to $s_0$ is $L/4$. Therefore
\[
 |\gamma(s_0)-c|\le\frac1L\int|\gamma(s_0)-\gamma(s)|\,ds\le\frac L4.
\]
Combining with \eqref{centroid} yields
\[
 |c'|\le\tfrac14\sqrt{2ED},\qquad
 |c(t)-c(s)|\le\tfrac14\sqrt{(t-s)(E(s)^2-E(t)^2)}.
\]
The same limiting argument as before gives $c(t)=o(\sqrt t)$, which allows slower divergence.

\begin{proposition}[Exact criterion within the homothetic class]\label{migration}
Let $\beta$ be a nonempty compact immersed profile of a genuine closed homothetic expander, with expansion center zero. Write
$\gamma(t)=p+\rho(t)\beta$, where $\rho(0)=1$ and $\rho$ continuously increases to infinity. A fixed rotation and translation make the curve lie strictly in the upper half-plane initially and strictly in the lower half-plane at some later time if and only if
\[
 0\notin\operatorname{conv}(\beta).
\]
For any such profile the arclength centroid diverges in norm.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Along a chosen direction let $a=\min y$, $b=\max y$, with $y=\beta\cdot e$. If $b<0$, choose
\[
 p_e=1+|a|,\qquad \rho_1=1+\frac{p_e+1}{-b}>1.
\]
Then $p_e+y>0$ everywhere and
$p_e+\rho_1y\le p_e+\rho_1b=b-1<0$ everywhere. The scale $\rho_1$ is reached at a finite time. Conversely, subtracting the two strict half-plane inequalities gives $(\rho_1-1)y<0$ at every point. Compactness and attainment of the maximum imply $b<0$. Strict separation of the compact convex hull from zero gives the equivalence with the stated convex-hull condition.

Under homothety the arclength centroid is $p+\rho c_\beta$. Since $c_\beta\cdot e\le b<0$, we have $c_\beta\ne0$, and its norm diverges.
\end{proof}
The scalar inequalities, their necessity, and the attained-maximum equivalence are verified in Lean. Convex separation, the centroid integral, and expander existence are not formalized in those files. Proposition~\ref{migration} is a conditional reduction: no one-sided expander is constructed here.

\section{Elementary exclusions and remaining gaps}
A closed solution evolving only by rigid motions would keep $E$ constant, forcing $D=0$. The virial identity then forces $E=0$, impossible for a closed immersion by Fenchel. Thus there are no closed pure translators or pure rotators, nor closed stationary free elasticae.

For a homothetic profile satisfying $F_\beta=-\sigma\langle\beta,\nu\rangle$, \eqref{virialidentity} gives
\[
 \sigma\int\langle\beta,\nu\rangle^2\,ds=E[\beta]>0.
\]
Hence $\sigma>0$ and $\rho'=\sigma\rho^{-3}$, so $\rho(t)^4=\rho(0)^4+4\sigma t$. This class necessarily has quarter-power length growth. These exclusions do not classify homothetic profiles or more general rotating and dilating similarities, and they do not address unrestricted complete open curves.

The supplied local stability rates also do not settle centroid escape. An error of order $t^{-\mu/30}$ after division by $\rho\sim t^{1/4}$ allows an unscaled error of order $t^{1/4-\mu/30}$. Neither boundedness nor divergence of the physical centroid follows from that estimate alone.

\section{Formalization boundary}
The accompanying independent Lean modules use Lean 4.33.1 and the following pinned Mathlib commit:
\begin{center}\small\texttt{0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474}.\end{center}
They prove:
\begin{itemize}
\item the scalar dissipation-to-secant implication from explicitly stated continuity, derivative, and dissipation hypotheses;
\item the conditional fourth-power upper bound, using the intermediate value theorem and explicitly assuming a uniform bound for $EL$ and the restart estimate;
\item the scalar one-sided homothetic migration construction and converse;
\item the exact attained-maximum criterion for a bounded height profile.
\end{itemize}
The source comments and theorem types are authoritative for the exact formal scope. They do not contain an unrestricted free-elastic-flow existence, classification, length-growth, or migration theorem. The differential-geometric identities and remaining analytic applications in this note are human-readable proofs, not claimed kernel-checked theorems. Kernel axiom checks must be read together with these assumptions; absence of extra axioms alone does not identify a theorem with the original research question.
