# Arbitrarily high increasing records on primitive curves of turning number zero

For every integer $d\ge3$ there is a smooth primitive closed free elastic flow of turning number zero for which $U=EL$ is strictly increasing from its initial slice, $U(t)\to d^2U_*$, and for every $H<d^2U_*$ the full positive nodal charge on the increasing records above $H$ has infinite integral. Its leading physical rate is
\[
  2\sqrt{LD}\,B(t)\sim \frac{12d^2\sqrt{L_\beta D_\beta}}{30t}.
\]
Here $U_*$, $L_\beta$ and $D_\beta$ refer to one reference traversal. Each individual example has bounded product and fourth-root length growth. The initial energy may be prescribed by an overall spatial dilation. In particular one may place the entire future strictly above any prescribed product height by choosing $d$ sufficiently large and restarting at a sufficiently late time. The examples retain a point visited exactly once on every time slice after that restart. They give an obstruction even when multiply covered *evolving curves* are excluded.

We use the graph equation, visible real modes and weighted right inverse of [the single-traversal construction](IncreasingLemniscateRecords.md). Put $P$ for one $x$-period of the reference and $b=2\pi/P$. On the $d$-fold reference choose a real eigenfunction $v$ periodic over $P$ and visible to $DU$. The single-period argument provides arbitrarily high odd-frequency annuli with such an eigenvalue $\lambda=-4b^4 n^4(1+O(1/n))$. The derivative of $U$ on the repeated $v$ is $d^2$ times the derivative for one traversal. The nonlinear correction associated to this first direction may preserve all the traversals, so we add an independently decaying symmetry-breaking mode.

Fix $\alpha=1/d$. On complex functions satisfying $w(x+P)=e^{2\pi i\alpha}w(x)$, the principal part $-4\partial_x^4$ has the simple real spectrum $-4b^4(j+\alpha)^4$ for $j\in\mathbb Z$. Since $0<\alpha<1/2$ is fixed, the distance of a large positive-$j$ eigenvalue from every other is at least $c_d j^3$ with $c_d>0$. Indeed the two interlaced nonnegative frequency sequences have their closest spacings bounded below by $\min(2\alpha,1-2\alpha)$. On the circle of half this separation around that eigenvalue the Fourier resolvent gives $\|(A+4\partial_x^4)(z+4\partial_x^4)^{-1}\|_{L^2\to L^2}=O_d(j^{-1})$. The same continuous deformation as for one traversal retains exactly one eigenvalue, algebraic multiplicity one, in this disk. Reflection followed by complex conjugation is an antilinear involution of this precise Bloch space and commutes with $A$ because $q^2,q^4$ are even and $qq_x$ is odd. Thus the unique eigenvalue in the real-centered disk is real. Taking $j=\lfloor 11n/10\rfloor$ and $n$ sufficiently large among the visible annuli yields one such eigenvalue $\nu$ with $2\lambda<\nu<\lambda<0$, since $(11/10)^4$ lies strictly between one and two.

Take a nonzero smooth Bloch eigenfunction $w$ for $\nu$. Its extension is periodic on the $d$-fold domain. Select a value $x_0$ visited only once by the reference during one traversal, with $w(x_0)\ne0$. The ordinary differential eigenvalue equation prevents a nonzero $w$ from vanishing on any interval; the reference has an open embedded arc disjoint from the rest of its image except at the arc ends. There is a phase $c$ for which the following $d$ real numbers are pairwise different:
\[
                \operatorname{Re}(c e^{2\pi i j/d}w(x_0)),\qquad 0\le j<d.
\]
Each equality excludes a proper real line of values of $c$, and there are only finitely many. Real coefficients make $\operatorname{Re}(cw)$ a real eigenfunction of the full $d$-periodic problem.

Choose $-\nu<\mu<-2\lambda$ with no spectrum on $\operatorname{Re}z=-\mu$. Apply the single-traversal construction over the full $d$-periodic real space to the prescribed linear approximation
\[
    f_0(\tau)=\epsilon e^{\lambda\tau} v+\epsilon e^{\nu\tau}\operatorname{Re}(cw).
\]
Both modes solve the homogeneous linear equation. Their quadratic remainder is in weighted $L^2_\mu(H^4)$ because $\mu+2\lambda<0$. The same contraction gives $f=f_0+O_{H^6}(\epsilon^2e^{-\mu\tau})$ and the corresponding differentiated estimate in $H^2$. All constants may depend on $d$ and the chosen modes. Since $\nu<\lambda$ the first term determines the eventual sign of $U_\tau$ after the sign of $\epsilon$ is chosen. Since $\mu>-\nu$, the error is smaller than the second term. The $d$ normal graph heights above the reference point $x_0$ differ at order $\epsilon e^{\nu\tau}$, because the first mode has the same value on every traversal. On a narrow embedded reference arc around $x_0$ the normal tubular coordinates are injective. A smaller neighborhood of its central point misses the closed reference complement. For all sufficiently large times each of the $d$ displayed normal heights therefore specifies an image point with exactly one parameter preimage on the full $d$-fold time slice. A curve tracing any other curve two or more times cannot have such a point. This proves primitivity and distinguishes the solutions from exact covers.

The limiting product and the positive charge follow by the same scaling as in the one-traversal proof, with $\widehat L\to dL_\beta$, $\widehat D\to dD_\beta$ and $\widehat B\to6d$. Proximity on the $d$-fold parameter circle preserves turning number zero; restricting to a late tail gives the asserted smooth initial curve. Overall dilation changes the initial energy by its reciprocal and preserves the coefficient of $1/t$. This is a written argument. Its examples do not establish or refute the existence of one flow with unbounded product.
