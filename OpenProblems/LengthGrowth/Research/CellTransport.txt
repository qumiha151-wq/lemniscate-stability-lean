CELL TRANSPORT IN THE ACTUAL FREE ELASTIC FLOW
13 November 2026. Analytic research note; not Lean formalized.

STATUS AND SCOPE

This note does not prove unrestricted quarter-power length growth, does
not prove that the total bending energy tends to zero, and does not
construct a counterexample trajectory. It records exact transport laws,
one genuine finite-action consequence of the already established PDE
estimates, and two obstructions to turning cell displacement into a
complete argument. All algebra below can be checked directly. The
companion Python check verifies the rational jets and scaling exponents
only; it does not verify PDE existence or a trajectory.

Read alongside length_energy_limit_independent.txt,
length_dynamic_flattening.txt, length_absolute_curvature_entropy.txt,
length_stress_coercivity.txt and the static obstruction/audit notes.
In particular, no static monotonicity of EL is invoked. The newly
established L=o(sqrt(t)) is compatible with the unresolved scenarios.

1. CONVENTIONS AND EXACT MOVING-CELL IDENTITIES

Let gamma(t,u) be a genuine smooth closed free elastic flow in normal
gauge, with fixed material parameter u. Put

  g=|gamma_u|,  partial_s=g^(-1) partial_u,  T=gamma_s, N=JT,
  gamma_t=-F N,  F=k_ss+k^3/2,  W=(k^2/2)T+k_s N.

The actual evolution and spatial stress identities are

  g_t=kF g,  T_t=-F_s N,  k_t=-F_ss-k^2 F,
  W_s=F N,  gamma_t=-W_s.                              (1)

The time derivatives here are at fixed material u. For a cell with
endpoints a(t),b(t) in the material coordinate, write

  sigma_a=g(t,a)a'(t),  sigma_b=g(t,b)b'(t),
  d=gamma(t,b)-gamma(t,a),  ell=integral_a^b ds,
  E_I=(1/2)integral_a^b k^2 ds,  Theta=integral_a^b k ds,
  D_I=integral_a^b F^2 ds.

For a continuously chosen oriented arc, differentiation gives

  d' = -F_b N_b+F_a N_a +sigma_b T_b-sigma_a T_a,
  ell' = integral_I kF ds +[sigma]_a^b,
  E_I' = -D_I+[k_s F-k F_s+(k^2/2)sigma]_a^b,
  Theta' = [-F_s+sigma k]_a^b.                         (2)

For example the energy bulk integrand is
  k(-F_ss-k^2F)+(1/2)k^3F=-kF_ss-(1/2)k^3F;
two integrations by parts give its boundary term in (2).
For fixed material endpoints sigma=0. At curvature-zero endpoints the
sigma terms in energy and turning vanish, but the sigma terms in chord
transport do not vanish. Confusing these two facts loses the main issue.

There is also an exact local virial identity at each time:

  partial_s [W dot (gamma-gamma(a))]
     =F N dot (gamma-gamma(a))+k^2/2,
  E_I=W(b) dot d-integral_I F <gamma-gamma(a),N> ds.      (3)

Using |gamma(s)-gamma(a)|<=s-s(a) and Cauchy--Schwarz,

  |E_I-W(b) dot d| <= sqrt(ell^3/3) sqrt(D_I).           (4)

For a rectangular-elastica-like cell of scale lambda, the stationary
orders are ell~lambda, |d|~lambda, E_I~lambda^(-1),
|W|~lambda^(-2). With epsilon^2=lambda^5 D_I the error in
(4) has order epsilon/lambda. Thus (4) identifies the natural residual
scale. It is a static identity and by itself supplies no cell lifetime,
birth estimate, or integrated boundary-velocity estimate.

2. A GENUINE FINITE MATERIAL-CHORD ACTION

For fixed material endpoints, the derivative of the cell chord obeys

  d'=integral_I partial_s gamma_t ds,
  partial_s gamma_t=-F_s N+kF T.

Consequently

  |d'|^2/ell <= integral_I (F_s^2+k^2F^2) ds.            (5)

For any finite fixed material partition into disjoint arcs, summing (5)
gives the simultaneous estimate

  sum_j |d_j'|^2/ell_j <= integral_whole (F_s^2+k^2F^2) ds.
                                                                  (6)
Likewise |ell_j'|^2/ell_j<=integral_Ij k^2 F^2 ds.

The previously derived genuine PDE estimates give
  integral_0^infinity integral F_s^2 ds dt < infinity,
  E'=-D,  integral_0^infinity D dt<=E(0),  M=||k||_infinity ->0.
Smoothness on compact time intervals together with M->0 makes M
globally bounded. Hence integral M^2 D dt is finite and (6) proves

  integral_0^infinity sum_j |d_j'|^2/ell_j dt < infinity. (7)

The action tail tends to zero, uniformly over the choice of such a
partition. This is a consequence of the already known H1 spatial
velocity bound, not a new independent Lyapunov estimate.

For one material cell, (5) only yields

  |d(t)-d(S)| <= [integral_S^t ell(r)dr]^(1/2)
                 [integral_S^t |d'(r)|^2/ell(r)dr]^(1/2). (8)

The first factor need not stay bounded. Small squared action does not
give finite total displacement. A scale lambda=t^alpha has dilational
action density lambda'^2/lambda~t^(alpha-2), integrable for every
alpha<1. An order-lambda change over an order-lambda^4 time interval
costs order lambda^(-3) in this action. These costs can be summable
at increasing scales. These statements test the estimate, not the PDE.

Equation (7) also concerns fixed material partitions. A time-dependent
partition acquires the sigma terms in (2), and even a fixed partition
does not label all future curvature cells if new oscillations appear
inside its members.

3. EXACT SYMMETRY: TRANSLATION AND ROTATION DIFFER

Suppose an actual open flow has an exact material translation symmetry

  gamma(t,u+P)=gamma(t,u)+d(t),

where P is fixed. The tangent, curvature, normal and F at corresponding
points agree. Differentiating this equality in the same normal gauge
therefore gives d'(t)=0. This proves the proposed conservation law for
an exactly translation-periodic flow. It does not assert that every
approximate cell in a closed curve is translation-periodic.

The small-rotation cells used in closed fixed-turn constructions instead
have an exact Euclidean symmetry of the form

  gamma(t,u+P)=R gamma(t,u)+c(t),

with fixed rotation R. Equivariance of the normal velocity gives c'=0.
For R not the identity, the rotation center is therefore fixed. But
the ordinary chord between the corresponding endpoints is

  d_cell=(R-I)gamma(t,u)+c,
  d_cell'=(R-I)gamma_t(t,u),
  |d_cell'|<=2|sin(delta/2)| |F(t,u)|,                 (9)

where delta is the rotation angle. Thus the chord is generally NOT
conserved. Conservation of the symmetry center is not conservation of
each cell displacement. In fixed-turn N-cell constructions the relevant
rotation is delta=2*pi*q/N, not an exact nonzero translation.

Moreover an initially fixed exact symmetry order N does not bound the
number of curvature oscillations which may arise inside one fundamental
symmetry interval. Preserving the symmetry group cannot substitute for
a bound on the number of dynamically formed cells.

One useful conditional consequence is precise: if a persistent material
cell satisfies |d(t)|>=c lambda(t), lambda(t)->infinity, and

  integral_0^infinity |F_b N_b-F_a N_a|dt < infinity,   (10)

then (2) would make d bounded, a contradiction. Condition (10) is the
missing L1 boundary-velocity control; (7) does not imply it.

4. ACTUAL CLOSED INITIAL CURVES WITH FAST ZERO MARKERS

The failure of a norm-only estimate for moving inflection markers is
visible on explicit smooth closed initial curves, not merely on scalar
data. Let 0<epsilon<=1 and put

  a=-257/480, b=17/480, c=epsilon/4,
  phi(u)=a sin u+(b/2)sin(2u)-c cos u+c,
  theta_epsilon(s)=s+phi(2s),
  T_epsilon(s)=(cos theta_epsilon(s),sin theta_epsilon(s)),
  gamma_epsilon(s)=integral_0^s T_epsilon(r)dr.

The function phi is 2*pi-periodic, so theta(s+pi)=theta(s)+pi.
Thus T(s+pi)=-T(s), and gamma closes smoothly with length 2*pi,
unit speed, and turning number one. All fixed-order initial C^m norms
are bounded uniformly in epsilon. The curvature is exactly

  k(s)=1+2a cos(2s)+2b cos(4s)+(epsilon/2)sin(2s).

At s=0 its exact jets are

  theta=0, T=(1,0), N=(0,1),
  k=0, k_s=epsilon, k_ss=63/20, k_sss=-4epsilon,
  k_ssss=1;
  F=63/20, F_s=-4epsilon, F_ss=1, k_t=-1.             (11)

Here k_t is the instantaneous actual normal-flow derivative from (1).
For any smooth short-time normal-gauge flow through this initial curve,
the implicit function theorem follows the simple zero near s=0 as a
material marker a(t). At t=0 its physical tangential speed satisfies

  0=k_t+sigma_a k_s,  sigma_a=1/epsilon.

The velocity of the tracked spatial point at that instant is

  gamma_t+sigma_a T=(1/epsilon,-63/20).                (12)

All initial E,D and integral F_s^2, and indeed all fixed-order derivative
norms, stay uniformly bounded in this family. Yet the inflection-marker
speed is unbounded as epsilon->0. Therefore a marker-speed estimate
depending only on those uniformly bounded quantities is false without
some nondegeneracy input such as a lower bound for |k_s| at the marker.

This example has a strict limitation. As epsilon tends to zero, the
zero near 0 and a second nearby zero coalesce at a double zero. From
k(s)=epsilon*s+(63/40)s^2+O(epsilon*s^3+s^4), their separation is order
epsilon and the enclosed tangent excursion is order epsilon^3. It is
NOT creation of a fixed-angle rectangular cell and does NOT show that
A grows without bound along a flow. The large sigma cancels out of
the energy and turning boundary terms when k=0. It must not be used
to infer a large A' merely from a large marker speed. Its role is to
identify the singularity in naive zero-tracking displacement arguments.

5. SCALE DIAGNOSTIC: THE NEW ACTION DOES NOT EXCLUDE DIFFUSE ENERGY

The following is a consistency test of exponents only. It is not an
actual curve or solution, and N below is a continuous effective cell
count. Let u=1+t, E_infinity>0, c>0 and set

  lambda=u^(1/5), E=E_infinity+c*u^(-1/10),
  D=-E'=(c/10)*u^(-11/10), N~E*lambda.

Rectangular-cell orders then suggest

  L~N lambda~u^(2/5), M~lambda^(-1), A~N~u^(1/5),
  Y~N lambda^(-3)~u^(-2/5), Z~N lambda^(-7)~u^(-6/5).

These orders are compatible with L=o(sqrt(t)), finite integral D,
finite integral M^6 and Z, and the scalar stress/virial/length bounds:

  ED/M^6~u^(1/10),
  D/(E^2/L^3)~u^(1/10),
  (L')^2/(ED)~u^(-1/10).

If the dissipation is spread over cells of this scale, the dimensionless
residual per cell and the force-gradient budget have orders

  epsilon_cell^2~(D/N)*lambda^5~u^(-3/10)->0,
  H:=D/lambda^2~u^(-3/2),

where H represents the order of integral(F_s^2+k^2F^2) for residual
forces varying on the cell scale. It is integrable. The action required
by dilation of the N persistent cells has order

  N lambda'^2/lambda~u^(-8/5),
  (N lambda'^2/lambda)/H~u^(-1/10)->0.                 (13)

Thus the new dynamic bound (6), even with its integrable tail, does not
exclude this positive-energy scenario at the scaling level. All '~'
statements allow cell constants; checking the exponents does not claim
that arbitrary constants satisfy every geometric identity. Neither
integer cell births nor a flow have been constructed here.

6. WHAT WOULD STILL BE NEEDED

The proposed conservation route works for one exact translation-periodic
cell, but it has not extended to the actual closed-flow problem. A proof
must resolve at least the following concrete points:

  (i) Select and follow cells carrying a fixed nonvanishing tangent
      excursion, not just arbitrary inflection intervals.
 (ii) Control material/marker exchange and boundary energy flux in (2).
(iii) Obtain an integrated displacement or fixed-angle-cell-creation
      estimate stronger than the weighted squared action (7).

A possible target is a dissipation toll at least c/lambda for every
genuinely new fixed-angle cell at scale lambda. In a quasiuniform
positive-energy regime N~lambda, summing such a toll over new cells
would have a harmonic divergence. This is only a proposed estimate:
it has NOT been proved; cells can receive energy through
[k_sF-kF_s], and a newly born tiny oscillation is not yet a fixed-angle
cell. Even this proposed toll would not automatically bound A when
E->0, since scale-weighted costs can then be summable.

The present rigorous gain is therefore the exact transport package
(1)--(9), the finite material-chord action (7), and the actual closed
zero-marker countertest (11)--(12). They locate the remaining dynamic
gap but do not settle the general length-growth question.
