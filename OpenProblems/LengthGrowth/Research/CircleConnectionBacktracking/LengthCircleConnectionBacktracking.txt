Geometric backtracking for an oriented circle-to-circle connection
2026-11-13. Necessary conditions only; no existence theorem or general
quarter-power length-growth bound is asserted.

Let U,p,q be C1 real functions on R satisfying the Frenet support equations
  p'=-Uq, q'=1+Up,
and suppose
  (U,p,q)(-infinity)=(-1,1,0),
  (U,p,q)(+infinity)=(1,-1,0).
Write v=U', w=p^2+q^2 and H=Uw+2p. These hypotheses involve no elastic
profile equation and no condition on p at the zeros of U. Thus every true
connection in the normalized elastic support system, for any Omega,
obeys the conclusions below. Conversely these conclusions do not certify
an elastic profile or a geometric-flow solution.

1. Exact differential identities and unrestricted backtracking

Direct differentiation gives
  w'=2p(-Uq)+2q(1+Up)=2q,
  H'=v*w+2Uq-2Uq=v*w.
The end states give H(-infinity)=1 and H(+infinity)=-1.
If v>=0 everywhere, then H'>=0 everywhere, so H is nondecreasing, which
contradicts those two limits. Therefore v(s)<0 at some s. This argument
needs only differentiability and the stated limits; no integrability,
exponential tail rate or scalar fourth-order ODE is needed. For C1 U the
negative derivative persists on an open interval.

For finite A<B one also has the exact signed identity
  integral_A^B v*w = [Uw+2p]_A^B.
As A tends to -infinity and B to +infinity the right side tends to -2.
This is a two-ended improper limit; it must not be silently called an
absolutely convergent integral for an arbitrary C1 profile. If integral
|v| is finite, w is bounded and absolute convergence does follow.

2. Strict lower bounds for the total negative variation

Put a=inf_R w, b=sup_R w, and N=integral_R max(-v,0), allowing N=infinity.
The endpoint limits and continuity imply 0<=a<=1<=b<infinity. Also b>a:
otherwise w is constant, so q=0 and p'=0, contrary to p(-infinity)=1
and p(+infinity)=-1. In particular b>0 and b-a>0.

On every finite interval write P_AB=integral max(v,0) and N_AB=integral
max(-v,0). Then P_AB-N_AB=U(B)-U(A) and
  integral_A^B v*w >= a*(U(B)-U(A))-(b-a)*N_AB.
Pass to the endpoint limits and monotone limits for N_AB to obtain
  N >= 2*(1+a)/(b-a) >= 2/b.
The latter comparison uses a*(b+1)>=0. Infinite N is allowed throughout.

Both bounds are actually strict. It suffices to treat N finite. Since U
has end values -1 and 1, P=N+2 is then finite as well. The nonnegative
defects give
  -2 = a*(N+2)-b*N
       + integral (w-a)*max(v,0)
       + integral (b-w)*max(-v,0).
Equality in the first bound forces the second defect to vanish. The first
part proved that v<0 somewhere, and v is continuous, so an open interval
has v<0. On that interval continuity and the vanishing nonnegative
integral force w=b. Hence q=w'/2=0, p'=0, and q'=1+Up=0 there. Since b>=1,
p is a nonzero constant, whence U=-1/p is constant, contradicting v<0.
Therefore
  N > 2*(1+a)/(b-a) >= 2/b.
Writing a=m^2 and b=Rmax^2 gives the radius notation used in the other
wall notes. These are dimensionless geometric bounds; a fixed numerical
radius cap or a tail window is an additional hypothesis, not a conclusion.
