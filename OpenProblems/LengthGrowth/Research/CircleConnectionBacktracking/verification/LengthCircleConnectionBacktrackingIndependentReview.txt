INDEPENDENT ANALYTIC REVIEW OF CIRCLE-CONNECTION BACKTRACKING
13 November 2026. Reviewer: finish_odd_rigidity.

Reviewed in full: LengthCircleConnectionBacktracking.txt. This review is
read-only with respect to that note and its Lean module. It is an analytic
review; the Lean module was not recompiled or independently inspected here.

The hypotheses C1 U,p,q, the two actual Frenet support equations, and the
specified two endpoint limits suffice for every analytic conclusion in
Sections 1 and 2. No elastic force equation or zero-alignment hypothesis
is needed. In particular, w'=2q and (Uw+2p)'=U'w hold with the displayed
signs. The latter quantity decreases from 1 to -1 between the ends, so
U' cannot be everywhere nonnegative.

The distinction between the two-ended improper integral and an absolutely
convergent integral is correct. Continuity and finite endpoint limits
give a bounded w, but do not by themselves give integrable |U'|. When the
negative variation N is finite, the finite-interval identity P-N=Delta U
does imply that P=N+2 is finite and legitimizes the two nonnegative defect
integrals used in the equality argument.

The endpoint bounds 0<=a<=1<=b<infinity and the strict inequality b>a are
valid. A constant w would give q=0, then p constant, contrary to its two
end states. The finite-interval estimate has the correct direction:
  integral v*w >= a*Delta U-(b-a)*N_AB.
Exhausting R yields N>=2(1+a)/(b-a), including the case N=infinity.

The strictness argument is also valid. Equality would force w=b on an
open interval where v<0. There q=0, p'=0 and 1+Up=0; b>=1 ensures p is
nonzero, so U is constant on that interval, contradicting v<0. Thus
N>2(1+a)/(b-a)>=2/b. The last scalar comparison is equivalent to
a*(b+1)>=0. This proves strictness for the weaker radius bound as well.

No mathematical gap was found. The note correctly limits the Lean claim
to the differential identities, endpoint limits and existence of a point
with negative curvature derivative. Its quantitative integral argument
is explicitly presented as an analytic derivation. Nothing here proves
nonexistence of a connection, a fixed radius cap, or the original general
quarter-power length-growth statement.
