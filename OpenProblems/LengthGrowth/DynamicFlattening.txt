ACTUAL EVOLUTION CONSEQUENCES OF THE STRESS ESTIMATE
12 November 2026. Analytic derivation; not Lean formalized.
This note does NOT settle unrestricted quarter-power length growth.
No novelty claim is made for asymptotic flattening.

Use E=(1/2) integral k^2, F=k_ss+k^3/2, D=integral F^2,
M=||k||_infinity, Y=integral k_s^2, X=integral k_ss^2,
Z=integral k_sss^2. All quantities refer to one genuine smooth closed
free elastic flow with normal speed -F. The independently reviewed stress
estimate gives

                     M^6 <= 2^24 E D.                     (1)

1. A HIGHER-ORDER LYAPUNOV COMBINATION.

Lemma 3.1 of the original Miura--Wheeler paper gives the exact identity

 Y'=-2Z+5 integral k^2 k_ss^2
          -(5/3) integral k_s^4-(11/2) integral k^4 k_s^2. (2)

Periodic integration by parts and Cauchy--Schwarz give

 Y <= sqrt(2E) sqrt(X),    X <= sqrt(Y) sqrt(Z),
 X <= (2E)^(1/3) Z^(2/3),    Y^3 <= 4 E^2 Z.              (3)

No zero-average or uniform-in-length interpolation theorem is used here.
For a,z>=0, a z^(2/3)<=z+4a^3/27. Hence

 5 M^2 X <= Z+(1000/27) E M^6.

Combining (1)--(2), and using (E^3)'=-3E^2D, yields

 [Y+C_* E^3]'
   <= -Z-(5/3) integral k_s^4-(11/2) integral k^4 k_s^2,  (4)

where C_*=1000*2^24/81. This is an actual differential inequality for
the original geometric flow, not a scalar hypothesis imposed on it.
The coefficient is very coarse, inherited from the stress proof.

In particular, the quantity Y+C_* E^3 is nonincreasing and nonnegative.
For all s<t,

 integral_s^t [Z+(5/3)integral k_s^4+(11/2)integral k^4 k_s^2] du
  <= Y(s)-Y(t)+C_*[E(s)^3-E(t)^3].                         (5)

Thus all three nonnegative space-time terms on the left are integrable
on [0,infinity). Equation (3), with E<=E(0), gives integral_0^infinity
Y^3 dt<infinity. Since both E and Y+C_*E^3 have limits, Y has a limit.
The integrability of Y^3 forces

                           Y(t) -> 0.                    (6)

2. THE LENGTH GOES TO INFINITY, NOT MERELY ALONG A SUBSEQUENCE.

This uses already established estimates and is included to make the
subsequent inference self-contained. The restart inequality is

 |L(u)-L(t)| <= sqrt(u-t) E(t)   for u>=t.

If L(t_n)<=R for an unbounded sequence t_n, pass to a subsequence with
pairwise disjoint intervals [t_n,t_n+1]. Throughout each interval,
L<=R+E(0). Fenchel and the virial inequality then imply

 E >= 2pi^2/[R+E(0)],
 D >= 4pi^2 E^2/L^3 >= 16pi^6/[R+E(0)]^5 > 0.

This contradicts integral_0^infinity D<=E(0). Therefore L(t)->infinity.

3. UNCONDITIONAL POINTWISE FLATTENING.

The elementary periodic estimate used in the stress proof gives

 M(t)^2 <= 2E(t)/L(t)+2sqrt(2E(t))sqrt(Y(t)).

Together with (6), E<=E(0), and L->infinity, this implies

                        ||k(t)||_infinity -> 0.           (7)

This is a concrete unconditional consequence of the actual PDE and the
stress estimate. It is NOT a claim that E(t)->0: a nonzero limiting L2
curvature energy could still be spread over a domain whose length grows.

For an additional genuine dynamical bound, F_s=k_sss+(3/2)k^2k_s gives

 integral F_s^2 <= 2Z+(9/2)integral k^4 k_s^2.

By (5), integral_0^infinity integral F_s^2 ds dt<infinity. In normal
gauge T_t=-F_s N, so the arclength-weighted L2 tangent velocity has
finite squared space-time integral. This too does not control total
absolute curvature uniformly on an expanding domain.

4. EXACT REMAINING OBSTRUCTION.

Let A=integral |k|. At simple curvature zeros the real evolution is
A'=2 sum sign(k_s) k_sss. There is no sign or absolute-value contraction
in this fourth-order expression. The independent note gives smooth
closed examples with A'>0. Estimates (1)--(7) do not by themselves
bound the number of inflection points or the total variation of the
tangent. Such a bound, or a counterexample to it along ONE trajectory,
remains the missing step in the exact equivalence with quarter growth.

The additional scalar constraints can still coexist with non-quarter
growth. For example put u=1+t, C=100, and

 L=C u^(1/3), E=C u^(-1/6), D=(C/6)u^(-7/6),
 M=2u^(-1/4), A=C u^(1/12),
 Y=C u^(-2/3), Z=(C/4)u^(-5/3).

These are NOT claimed to come from a curve. They obey E'=-D,
(L')^2<=2ED, the virial and Fenchel inequalities, M^6<=2^24 ED,
2E<=MA, A^2<=2EL, M^2<=2E/L+2sqrt(2E)sqrt(Y),
Y^3=4E^2 Z, and Y'<=-Z+(1000/27)E M^6. Thus they also satisfy the
Lyapunov inequality [Y+C_*E^3]'<=-Z. Both M and Y tend to zero;
the integrals of M^6, Z, and Y^3 over [0,infinity) are finite.
Nevertheless L grows like t^(1/3) and A is unbounded.

For clarity, this checks the listed SCALAR consequences only, not the
full geometric moment identities or the curvature-zero formula for A'.
It shows that the new scalar bounds and flattening conclusion alone
still cannot establish quarter growth. A further genuine geometric
input is necessary.

5. LITERATURE AND VALIDATION BOUNDARY.

The evolution formula (2) is Lemma 3.1 of arXiv:2404.12619v3.
Miura--Rupp, arXiv:2504.03395v2, Theorem 2.7 and Section 7.4, obtain
asymptotic flattening for proper noncompact finite-energy free elastic
flows. That result is not directly invoked here, and its proof may also
adapt to expanding closed domains. Accordingly, no novelty claim is
made for (7). The explicit stress bound and its Lyapunov consequence
above should be assessed on their proofs, independently of priority.
