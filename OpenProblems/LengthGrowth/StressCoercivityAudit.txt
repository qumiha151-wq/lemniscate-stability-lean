INDEPENDENT ANALYTIC REVIEW OF THE ELASTIC-STRESS LENGTH REDUCTION
2026-11-12. Reviewer: length_migration subagent.
Reviewed source: work/next_research/length_stress_coercivity.txt.
No frozen Lean source or proof document was modified by this review.

Verdict
-------
The static estimate ED >= 2^-24 M^6 is proved by the argument supplied.
The derived estimate D >= 2^-18 E^5/A^6 is also correct. I found no
substantive sign, constant, closing-condition, or hidden length-uniformity
error. The flow consequences and the example with A'(0)>0 check out.
This is an independent line-by-line derivation, not a Lean verification,
and this review does not establish a literature-priority claim.

Two presentation clarifications are recommended:
(i) State N=JT explicitly, where J is the fixed counterclockwise quarter
turn. The Frenet equations alone also allow the opposite normal. Formula
(11) uses the convention N=JT. This convention is always available and
does not change E,D,M,A or the resulting theorem.
(ii) In the record-bound proof, use the connected component of {U>K}
containing the target time, or a last crossing of K. The current phrase
"an interval after its first crossing" can be made precise this way;
it is not a mathematical obstruction to the stated bound.

1. Assumptions actually needed
-----------------------------
The curve is a genuine smooth periodic regular immersion gamma:R/LZ->R^2,
parametrized by arclength, with L>0. Thus T,N,k,k_s and the subsequent
stress variables are periodic. The closing identity is integral T ds=0.
Embeddedness, nonzero signed turning number, and a sign for curvature are
not needed. In particular, a turning-zero immersed loop is included.
Fenchel gives A>=2pi and, by Cauchy--Schwarz, EL>=2pi^2>2. This implies
E,M,A>0, so every division by these quantities in the argument is valid.
The argument does not presuppose D>0; it excludes D=0 as part of the proof.

Genuine closure is essential. A nonzero periodic solution of
k_ss+k^3/2=0 has D=0 and gives a counterexample to the same statement for
arbitrary periodic curvature data. Its reconstructed Frenet curve is not
closed. The reviewed proof correctly invokes actual closure at (12),
rather than treating periodic curvature or periodic support variables as
sufficient.

2. Curvature and energy ratios
------------------------------
Writing B^2=integral k_s^2, periodic integration by parts gives
  B^2=(1/2)integral k^4-integral kF
     <= E M^2+sqrt(2ED).
At a point below the mean of k^2, comparison with its maximum yields
  M^2 <= 2E/L+2sqrt(2E)B <= E^2+2sqrt(2E)B.
The second inequality uses EL>=2, not a hidden upper bound for L.

Under r=sqrt(ED)/M^3<=1/4096 and x=M/E, substitution gives exactly
  x^2 <= 1+2sqrt(2)sqrt(x^2+sqrt(2)r x^3).
For x>=64, r<=1 and 1+sqrt(2)x<=2x make the right side at most
  1+4x sqrt(x) <= 1+x^2/2 < x^2.
Hence x<64, so rx<=1/64. In particular
  B^2 <= E M^2(1+sqrt(2)rx) <= 4E M^2,
and B<=2sqrt(E)M as claimed.

3. Stress magnitude and direction
---------------------------------
Let a=k^2/2, b=k_s and W=aT+bN, with N=JT. Direct differentiation gives
  W_s=(kk_s-kk_s)T+(k_ss+k^3/2)N=FN,
  (|W|^2)_s=2k_sF.
At a maximum of |k|, k_s=0 since M>0, and |W|^2=M^4/4.
Integration over an arc of at most one period therefore gives
  ||W(s)|^2-M^4/4| <= 2B sqrt(D) <= 4rM^4 <= M^4/1024.
This is stronger than the used bounds M^2/4<=|W|<=M^2. W never vanishes.

For w=|W| and e=W/w,
  |e_s|=|det(W,W_s)|/w^2=k^2|F|/(2w^2).
Consequently
  integral |e_s| <= (8/M^4) integral k^2|F|
                 <= (8/M^4) M sqrt(2E)sqrt(D)
                 =8sqrt(2)r <1/2.
For v=e(s0), |e(s)-v|<1/2, hence e(s).v>=1/2. All estimates use an
integral over the actual circle. No uncontrolled sqrt(L) enters here.

4. Inversion, algebraic sign, and closing integral
-------------------------------------------------
For V=W/w^2,
  V_s=w^-2(I-2e tensor e)W_s.
The parenthesized map is an orthogonal reflection, so
  |V_s|=|F|/w^2 <=16|F|/M^4.
Since JW=aN-bT,
  aV-bJV=((a^2+b^2)T)/w^2=T.
Thus the minus sign in T=(k^2/2)V-k_sJV is correct under N=JT.

Integrating T=0 gives
  0=integral (k^2/2)V-integral k_sJV
   =integral (k^2/2)V+integral kJV_s.
The sign in (12) is therefore also correct. The boundary term vanishes
because k and V are actual periodic fields and W has no zero.

Projection of the first integral onto v is at least E/(2M^2), using
v.V=(v.e)/w>=1/(2M^2). The norm of the second integral is at most
  (16/M^4) integral |kF| <=16sqrt(2ED)/M^4.
It follows that
  1 <=32sqrt(2) r x <=32sqrt(2)*(1/4096)*64=sqrt(2)/2<1,
the required contradiction. In fact the argument gives a strict bound
ED>2^-24M^6; the recorded non-strict version is valid.

Finally 2E<=MA, so
  ED >=2^-24(2E/A)^6=2^-18E^6/A^6,
and division by E>0 proves the stated second estimate.

5. Flow consequences and record-bound crossing
---------------------------------------------
For normal velocity -F, ds_t=kF ds and the standard first variations give
E'=-D, L'=integral kF, and (L')^2<=2ED. Therefore
  integral_s^t M(u)^6 du <=2^24 integral_s^t E D du
                         =2^23(E(s)^2-E(t)^2).
The factor2^23 in the note is correct.

If A<=A0 along the same global smooth flow, then
  (E^-4)'=4D/E^5 >=2^-16/A0^6.
Integration gives exactly the recorded energy decay. The restart estimate
and dyadic sum in the previously reviewed quarter_growth_equivalence.txt
then give quarter-power length growth. Conversely A^2<=2EL, so bounded EL
implies bounded A. Combined with the existing individual-flow equivalence
between bounded EL and quarter-power growth, statement(14) follows.

For U=EL, if U'>0 then LD<E L'<=E sqrt(2ED). Here D>0 follows already
from U'>0, or from the static bound. Dividing and squaring gives
  D<2E^3/L^2.
Combining with the new estimate gives
  U^2<2^19 A^6,
hence U<512sqrt(2)A^3<1024A^3 as stated.

A precise proof of (16) is: fix t and put
K=max(U(0),1024 max_[0,t]A^3). A is continuous, so this maximum exists.
If U(t)>K, let a be the left endpoint of the connected component of
{s in [0,t]:U(s)>K} containing t. Then U(a)=K, and U>K on(a,t].
The mean-value theorem on[a,t] gives c in(a,t) with U'(c)>0, contradicting
U(c)<1024A(c)^3<=K. This also handles a=0 when U(0)=K.

These deductions concern one actual evolving curve. They do not replace
an unbounded-in-time construction by a sequence of different initial data.

6. The nonmonotonicity example
------------------------------
For simple curvature zeros, differentiating A and using
k_t=-F_ss-k^2F, ds_t=kF ds cancels the zero-order terms and yields
  A'=-integral sign(k)F_ss
    =2 sum_{k(z)=0} sign(k_s(z))F_s(z)
    =2 sum sign(k_s(z))k_sss(z).
The sign and factor2 in(17) are correct. Tangential reparametrization does
not alter this integral identity; the pure-normal convention already used
by the note suffices.

For K(u)=sin u-(1/9)sin3u and phi'=K, the T-cell geometric-sum argument
does close the actual unit-speed curve because0<|q|<N. Its curvature is
k=q+NK(Ns), not merely an assigned periodic curvature lacking closure.
At a zero, z=sin(Ns) solves the strictly increasing equation
(2/3)z+(4/9)z^3=-q/N, with |z|<=3/8. Thus there are exactly2N simple zeros.
The stated derivatives satisfy
  K'=cos u[(2/3)+(4/3)sin^2u],
  K'''=2cos u(1-6sin^2u).
Also k_s=N^2K' and k_sss=N^4K'''. Therefore the sum in(17) is exactly
  A'=8N^5 sqrt(1-z^2)(1-6z^2)>0.
The example excludes monotonicity of A, but does not give its divergence
on one long-time trajectory. The draft correctly preserves that limit.

Remaining open issue
--------------------
No step above proves a uniform-in-time bound for A for every free elastic
flow, nor constructs a flow with unbounded A. Thus the unrestricted
quarter-growth question remains open in this work. The new result is a
valid coercive estimate and an exact reduction of the dynamic obstruction.
The bibliography and novelty statements were outside this mathematical
review; no independent priority conclusion is drawn here.
