INDEPENDENT SEMANTIC AUDIT OF THE STATIC LENGTHGROWTH FORMALIZATION
12 November 2026. Review only; no frozen Lean source was changed or rebuilt.

Verdict
-------
The final two theorem statements have the intended static geometric meaning.
Their only geometric input is one actual sufficiently regular closed immersed
unit-speed planar curve, packaged as ClosedFrenetCurve. No sign restriction on
curvature, turning-number restriction, embeddedness, convexity, smallness,
stress nonvanishing, or coercivity conclusion is smuggled into that input.
All appearances of energy, dissipation, curvature and stress in the final
proof refer to the SAME record c and its actual derivative data.

The result is a static estimate, not a resolution of the paper's unrestricted
long-time length question. The missing uniform bound on the actual trajectory's
total absolute curvature is not supplied by this project. The abstract passage
from an arbitrary parametrized smooth immersion S1 -> R2 to its arclength lift
is mathematically standard but is NOT itself a theorem in these 14 modules.
State the exact formal scope as closed Frenet curves in arclength coordinates.

1. The input really contains a closed curve, not just a formal curvature
------------------------------------------------------------------------
ElasticStress.lean, structure ClosedFrenetCurve (lines26-43), contains:
  a real period L>0; an actual gamma:R->C; functions T,k,k1,k2;
  periodic gamma,T,k,k1;
  HasDerivAt gamma T, HasDerivAt T (k*(I*T));
  HasDerivAt k k1, HasDerivAt k1 k2; Continuous k2; norm(T)=1.

Uniqueness of derivatives ties these fields to the same gamma. T=gamma',
k1=k', k2=k'', and the unit tangent Frenet equation uniquely fixes signed k.
They cannot be selected independently to make the desired inequality true.
Energy and dissipation are not arbitrary record fields. The forces and
integrals are subsequent DEFINITIONS from these same functions.

The real domain of HasDerivAt makes these real derivatives in the Euclidean
plane C. Unit speed implies immersion everywhere and makes the parameter
interval length L equal to the arclength of one parametrized traversal.
There is no assumption that L is the least period. Repeated coverings and
self-intersections are admitted, as are rotation index zero and inflections.

Regularity is sufficient: k is C2 by the derivative data and continuity of k2;
T'=k*I*T and gamma'=T then bootstrap to a C4 arclength curve. A smooth closed
immersion from the paper, lifted and reparametrized by arclength, supplies
these data. Periodicity of T,k,k1 is automatic for that smooth periodic lift;
including it explicitly is not an extra geometric restriction. The project
does not formalize that reparametrization/construction interface itself.

The periodic POSITION field is essential: tangent_integral_zero is proved
by FTC from gamma(L)=gamma(0). A periodic curvature/Frenet frame whose
integrated tangent fails to close does not satisfy this record. Thus there
is no missing angle-holonomy or position-closing condition in this theorem,
unlike a statement made solely about periodic scalar support ODE data.

The normal is DEFINED as N=I*T, and N'=-kT is proved. This orientation choice
does not impose inward convexity. Reversing/reflection conventions changes
signed quantities consistently; the final even-power/integral bounds are
unaffected. There is also no chosen expansion center or origin condition.

The input is not vacuous at the mathematical level: gamma(s)=exp(i*s),
T=i*exp(i*s), k=1, k1=k2=0, L=2*pi meets all listed fields. A Lean constructor
for this example is not needed by the universal theorem and was not added.

2. The actual quantities match the paper
---------------------------------------
ElasticStress.lean defines F=k2+k^3/2, W=(k^2/2)T+k1*N, and V=1/conj(W).
For W nonzero, V=W/|W|^2, rather than the un-conjugated reciprocal. The proved
identities are W'=F*N and (|W|^2)'=2*k1*F. Their signs agree with the chosen
N=I*T and T'=k*N.

StressIntegralEstimates.lean lines16-19 defines actual interval integrals:
  E=(1/2) integral_0^L k^2,
  D=integral_0^L F^2,
  A=integral_0^L |k|,
  B2=integral_0^L k1^2.
Continuity and L>0 give genuine finite nonnegative integrals. There is no
normalized average, different domain, externally assigned scalar energy,
or motion-dependent surrogate hidden in these names.

This E and F agree with the source paper's introduction (cached paper.txt,
lines25-35): E=1/2 integral k^2 ds and velocity=-(k_ss+k^3/2)N. Consequently D
is the squared L2 elastic force and equals energy dissipation WHEN the
separately required free-elastic-flow evolution/energy identity is available.
The static Lean theorem itself does not assume or formalize time evolution.

3. Intermediate hypotheses are discharged before the final theorem
------------------------------------------------------------------
The proof uses conditional auxiliary lemmas, but their conditions are all
proved for the same actual c in StressCoercivity.lean:

* Closure + FTC yields A>=1 and E*L>=1/2; hence E>0. The weak bound A>=1
  deliberately avoids assuming Fenchel's theorem or nonzero signed turning.
* Periodicity and compactness produce a positive global maximum
  M=|k(s0)|>0. At that actual maximum k1(s0)=0. Periodic representatives make
  it a maximum over all R, including if the selected representative is an
  endpoint of [0,L]. No differentiation of |k| at a zero is used: k^2 is used.
* Integration by parts/Cauchy--Schwarz prove the actual pointwise and gradient
  estimates. The pure algebra modules are instantiated with these actual
  E,D,M,B=sqrt(B2), not with unrelated scalars satisfying assumed targets.
* Under the contradiction assumption r=sqrt(E*D)/M^3<=1/4096, those estimates
  give M/E<64, B<=2*sqrt(E)*M, and M^2/4<=|W(s)|<=M^2 for every s.
* W is therefore nonzero inside this contradiction argument. The functions
  1/conj(W) and W/|W| use Lean's total division, but every differentiation,
  inversion or positive-denominator estimate needing nonzero W receives
  this proved hypothesis. No arbitrary value at W=0 drives the conclusion.
* The actual derivative of W/|W| has norm k^2*|F|/(2*|W|^2). Integrating it
  gives a cone of width at most8*sqrt(2)*r, hence the projection lower bound.
* The actual closure identity is
    integral (k^2/2)V = - integral k*I*V'.
  The minus sign is derived from integral T=0 and periodic integration by
  parts. Both endpoint terms really cancel for this c.
* The same integral has lower bound E/(2*M^2) and upper bound
  16*sqrt(2*E*D)/M^4. This gives
    1<=32*sqrt(2)*r*(M/E)<1,
  the contradiction. Thus there is no surviving cone, stress-size,
  force-nonzero, scalar-inequality or trajectory hypothesis in the main result.

4. Constants and final statements
--------------------------------
energy_dissipation_controls_curvature(c,s) states exactly
  2^(-24)*|k(s)|^6 <= E*D
for every real s. Because an actual global positive peak is constructed,
this is also the maximum/sup-curvature statement; no essential-sup mismatch
arises for these continuous periodic functions.

absolute_curvature_coercivity(c) states exactly
  2^(-18)*E^5/A^6 <= D.
The arithmetic is consistent: 2E<=M*A implies M^6*A^6>=64E^6; multiply the
first inequality by A^6 and cancel E>0. A>=1 proves the denominator is
strictly positive. The constants 16777216=2^24 and262144=2^18 are correct.

The final theorems do not assume total absolute curvature is bounded by a
universal constant. A is the actual current curve's total absolute curvature,
which can vary between curves and along a flow. In particular, this is not
a uniform D>=c*E^5 estimate over arbitrary closed immersions.

5. Relation to the actual open question, and honest scope
--------------------------------------------------------
The paper's Section1.2 asks whether there is a single free elastic solution
with length growth other than O(t^(1/4)); see cached paper.txt lines182-195.
Its general estimate involves integral_0^t E(tau)^3 d tau. These static Lean
results give a useful new input to a trajectory argument, but do not supply
uniform A(t), actual time-dependent energy identities, or a single anomalous
long-time solution. A sequence of different initial curves is not a counter-
example to that question, and nothing in these modules constructs one.

For a given smooth flow, each fixed-time curve can mathematically be put into
this static arclength form. That operation alone gives no bound on A(t) and
does not justify differentiating a time-dependent choice of arclength record.
The existing human proof of quarter growth under a separate uniform A(t)
bound and the dynamic flattening note must remain labeled separately.
