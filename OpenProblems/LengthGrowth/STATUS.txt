On a closed curve normalized to unit RMS curvature, let P=sqrt(2EL).
If n intervals suffice to separate the excursions above +1/2 from
those below -1/2, the RMS elastic force is at least 1/2-C*n^(3/2)/P.
For the true normal flow, n=O((EL)^sigma), sigma<1/3, on late high-product
slices implies E(t)->0 and L(t)=O(t^(1/4)*(1+log(1+t))^(1/(1-3*sigma))).
Failure of a t^(1/4+epsilon) bound forces at least c_epsilon*(EL)^(1/3)
half-RMS reversals on slices at arbitrarily late times and high products.
The CriticalDensity modules prove these statements for actual immersions.
Research/CriticalInflectionDensity gives analytic proofs of matching
closed-curve examples and two criteria based on the turning of curvature arcs.

An eventually uniform bound on the number of curvature sign changes implies
L(t)=O(t^(1/4)*(1+log(1+t))) and E(t)->0 for a smooth forward normal flow.
FiniteSignTheorem.lean proves the scalar inequality and its implication
for the actual flow, including the time comparison and energy decay.
Research/FiniteSignChanges/FiniteSignChanges.md gives the proof.
InflectionBirths.md gives an analytic small-time construction.
FiniteSignStaticSharpness.md establishes the order of the static error
for a fixed sign-count bound by using distinct closed initial curves.

Strict signed cubic threshold and rigidity for the centered cubic moment

Write U=EL. On a slice normalized to length one, use curvature K,
b=int K^2, c=int K^3 and S=int K=2 pi n. Set G=|c|/b^(3/2) and
J=|2c-bS|/b^(3/2).

There is a universal constant 0<g*<1/2 such that liminf G>g* implies
L(t)=O(t^(1/4)) and E(t)=O(t^(-1/4)). Every turning-zero flow satisfies
liminf G<=g*. An explicit admissible constant g*=1/2-epsilon is given in
Research/CurvatureMomentGap/FiniteWindowThreshold.md. The proof in
Research/CurvatureMomentGap/StrictHalfThreshold.md gives the oscillator
integral estimate and the static concentration argument.

For each fixed nonzero turning number n, some delta_n>0 suffices for
liminf J>1-delta_n to force smooth circular convergence of the
length-normalized flow. Every closed flow satisfies liminf J<=1.
Equality holds exactly for convergence to the round circle with the
corresponding covering number. The proof derives a bound on U from the
moment condition. See Research/CurvatureMomentGap/CriticalMomentRigidity.md.

The Lean kernel checks the explicit static cubic threshold, its transfer
to each material-coordinate slice, and the two actual flow consequences.
The final theorems use the geometric flow hypotheses directly. The
explicit threshold, theorem names, build inputs and axiom audit are
documented in Research/CurvatureMomentGap/ExplicitThresholdVerification.md.
The compactness and circle-stability steps concerning the centered
moment J are developed in CriticalMomentRigidity.md in that directory.
The length-growth problem for general immersed curves remains open.

Signed curvature moments and quarter-power growth

For arclength ds, signed curvature k, length L and bending energy
E=(1/2) int k^2 ds, write G=sqrt(L) |int k^3 ds|/(2E)^(3/2).

The Lean theorem in CubicHalfThreshold.lean gives the fourth-root length
upper bound when liminf G>1/2. The proof obtains a bound on the actual
energy-length product from the signed total curvature and the finite initial
time interval. Every failure of fourth-root growth would force arbitrarily
late, arbitrarily high records of E L with G below 1/2 plus any prescribed
positive error.

When G>=1 at all sufficiently late times, CubicMomentGrowth.lean proves
that E L decreases on that tail. CubicMomentDissipation.lean gives an
exact identity for curvature oscillation and its dissipation, and
CubicMomentDecay.lean proves exponential decay in the clock int L^(-4) dt
and convergence of curvature oscillation to zero in physical time. The
turning integral is nonzero. In particular these conclusions hold when
the curvature on each late slice is bounded below by minus one half its
root-mean-square value or bounded above by plus one half that value.
The bounds apply to immersions, including self-intersecting curves.

The smooth circular limit and its coefficient 32 pi^4 n^4 in L(t)^4/t
use the published circle stability theorem. Research/LengthCurvatureSkewness.md
also gives analytic estimates at and below the half threshold and a
turning-number-dependent gap below the circular threshold. The scope of
the Lean calculations is recorded in Research/CurvatureMomentFormalization.txt,
Research/HalfThreshold.md and Research/CurvatureMomentVerification.json.

For an embedded convex time interval, monotonicity of area times squared
bending energy also excludes nonround curves returning to a similar shape.
SignedAreaVariation.lean proves the signed Green-area variation directly
from the position equation. ConvexAreaEnergy.lean keeps Gage's geometric
inequality and the turning-angle identity as explicit inputs. The full
analytic argument is in Research/ConvexFreeElasticFlow.tex.

The unrestricted upper growth rate remains open. The moment condition
can fail on immersed curves with opposite signs of curvature on
different arcs.

Ambient energy concentration

A single forward normal flow obeys the quarter-power length bound if and
only if there are constants R>0 and theta>0 such that at every time t some
closed planar disk of radius R/E(t) contains at least theta E(t) bending
energy. The disk may move with the flow. Its energy is the integral over
all visits of the actual curve to the disk, with multiplicity. The proof
uses the two coordinate projections of a bounded arctangent potential and
the periodic derivative of curvature times its arclength derivative.
The remaining open step is to establish this concentration on every orbit
or find an orbit that loses it. See quarter_length_iff_ambient_concentration.


quarter_length_iff_material_disk_energy states the disk condition using the
original position, speed and curvature. If the quarter bound fails, for
every positive epsilon there are arbitrarily late horizons t on which the
physical-time average of D/E^5 is smaller than epsilon. On suitable such
horizons every continuous weight between zero and one supported on times
with E*L below a fixed threshold also has arbitrarily small average.


Moving strips and elapsed physical time

quarter_length_iff_strip_frequency proves the following equivalence for one
actual smooth forward normal free elastic flow. Its length is O(t^(1/4)) if
and only if there exist positive R and eta such that for every sufficiently
large T the set of times in [0,T] at which the whole image fits in some
strip of half-width R/E(t) has Lebesgue measure at least eta T. A strip may
be infinitely long, and its center and direction can change at each time.
The set of such times on each finite horizon is compact. A mean value
argument for the unit tangent and the stress bound give the explicit
finite-horizon strip_time_energy_budget. Establishing the frequency for
arbitrary forward flows remains open.
