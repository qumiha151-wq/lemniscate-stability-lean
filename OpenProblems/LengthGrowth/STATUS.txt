Finite windows and curvature on a potential exceptional flow

Write e(t)=E(t)L(t). If e is unbounded, every K >= e(0)+2 admits a record
time t of height r=e(t)>=K and a time x in [0,t] such that

  e(x) >= sqrt(r^2-r),
  D(x)/E(x)^5 <= 2/(r*(r-1)),
  (abs(k(x,u))/E(x))^6 <= 33554432/(r*(r-1)) for every material point u.

RecordCurvature.exists_flat_slice_of_unbounded selects a minimum of D/E^5
on the finite window with integral E^4 equal to r. The same time x works
for every point of the curve. RecordWindowInterior controls the energy ratio
and the length in terminal energy units throughout each window. The open
step is to prove that a single free elastic flow cannot realize this regime,
or to construct one that does.


Forward rescaling of a high record window

For each actual normal flow with unbounded E(t)L(t), and each threshold
K >= E(0)L(0)+2, exists_rescaled_record_window produces a forward normal
flow b obtained by dilating the original position by r=E(t) and starting
at an earlier time s. On the finite interval [0,T], T=r^4(t-s), the terminal
energy is 1 and the terminal length is e=E(t)L(t)>=K. The duration is at least
e exp(-8/(e-1)), and the total dissipation is at most exp(2/(e-1))-1.
Throughout the interval, energy lies between 1 and exp(2/(e-1)), and length
lies between exp(-2/(e-1)) sqrt(e^2-e) and e. There is a time when the sixth
power of the ratio of absolute curvature to energy is bounded everywhere by
33554432/(e(e-1)). The unrestricted growth question still requires deciding
whether the assumed unbounded product can occur on a single flow.


Ambient energy concentration

A single forward normal flow obeys the quarter-power length bound if and
only if there are constants R>0 and theta>0 such that at every time t some
closed planar disk of radius R/E(t) contains at least theta E(t) bending
energy. The disk may move with the flow. Its energy is the integral over
all visits of the actual curve to the disk, with multiplicity. The proof
uses the two coordinate projections of a bounded arctangent potential and
the periodic derivative of curvature times its arclength derivative.
The remaining open step is to establish this concentration on every orbit
or find an orbit that loses it. See quarter_length_iff_ambient_concentration.

Moving strips and elapsed physical time

quarter_length_iff_strip_frequency proves the following equivalence for one
actual smooth forward normal free elastic flow. Its length is O(t^(1/4)) if
and only if there exist positive R and eta such that for every sufficiently
large T the set of times in [0,T] at which the whole image fits in some
strip of half-width R/E(t) has Lebesgue measure at least eta T. A strip may
be infinitely long, and its center and direction can change at each time.
The set of such times on each finite horizon is compact. A mean value
argument for the unit tangent and the stress bound give the explicit
finite-horizon strip_time_energy_budget. Establishing the frequency for
arbitrary forward flows remains open.


Sharp lower coefficient for growth

Every forward classical normal free elastic flow of a closed immersed curve
satisfies limsup L(t)^4/t >= 32*pi^4. For each positive b below that
constant, sharp_length_fourth_limsup_lower produces arbitrarily late times
with L(t)^4/t > b, for arbitrary smooth immersed initial curves.
The once-covered expanding circle attains equality.
Its actual position, curvature, normal velocity and limiting length

Dependence of the coefficient on the initial curve

The one-turn Andrews--Wheeler reflected-arc expanders have asymptotic
length coefficients C_m growing as an explicit positive multiple of
sqrt(m), where m is the symmetry order. This precludes a bound depending
only on turning number even at fixed initial length. The arc construction
is taken from their preprint. JellyfishGrowth.lean verifies the subsequent
limit calculation from its two derivatives and seam condition. See
Research/JellyfishLengthCoefficient.md for the statement and proof.
