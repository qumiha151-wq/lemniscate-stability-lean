LENGTH GROWTH: PARTIAL FORMALIZATION AND A REVIEWED GEOMETRIC OBSTRUCTION
12 November 2026

ScalarDissipation.lean derives a secant estimate from genuine derivative
hypotheses and proves a conditional fourth-power length bound. The geometric
PDE identities and the assumed normalized-energy bound are not formalized
or proved here. This does not solve unrestricted quarter-power growth.

FixedTurningObstruction.txt constructs smooth closed immersed curves in every
fixed turning-number class with EL arbitrarily large and initially increasing.
For nonzero turning number the construction and threshold are explicit.
The same curves have D/E^5 tending to zero. An independent analytic review
found no substantive gap; its scope is recorded in FixedTurningAudit.txt.

These are initial data varying with an index, not one evolving solution with
unbounded EL. Thus this obstruction does not answer the long-time question.
It rules out universal high-EL monotonicity thresholds and a coercivity bound
depending only on turning number. Dependence on the full initial shape is
still possible. The construction is NOT Lean-formalized.

check_fixed_turning.py is an optional numerical check of the explicit
identities; it is not part of the Lean proof or a substitute for the argument.

CoercivitySharpness.txt, reviewed in CoercivitySharpnessAudit.txt, sharpens
the obstruction: for every fixed turning number, actual closed curves have
E asymptotic to N^2 and D asymptotic to N^4. Hence every homogeneous static
bound with energy exponent greater than 2 fails uniformly in that class.
This family has initially decreasing EL. A finite-energy packing argument
also rules out directly placing infinitely many fixed-angle cells in one
smooth initial curve. Neither assertion settles long-time length growth;
these additional analytic results are not Lean-formalized.

ElasticStress.lean now proves the geometric foundation of a new quantitative
estimate. It starts from an actual periodic position curve with unit tangent
and its Frenet/curvature derivatives. It derives the closing integral, the
elastic-stress derivative, the exact squared stress norm and derivative,
the derivative and norm of inverse stress, and the closed integration-by-parts
identity. Inversion explicitly requires nonzero stress; the later quantitative
contradiction argument must derive that condition rather than assume it.
The enforced audit covers both FreeElasticScalar and LengthGrowth namespaces.
The module imports only Mathlib, preserving this independent problem project.

StressCoercivity.txt and StressCoercivityAudit.txt give an independently
reviewed HUMAN proof of ED>=2^-24 M^6 and D>=2^-18 E^5/A^6, for actual closed
curves, where E is half the curvature-square integral, D the elastic-force
square integral, M the maximum absolute curvature, and A total absolute
curvature. They reduce the individual-flow quarter-growth question exactly
to uniform boundedness of A. They do not prove that boundedness.

DynamicFlattening.txt and its audit derive an actual higher-order Lyapunov
quantity, space-time estimates, and uniform curvature flattening. The new
scalar consequences still permit a countermodel with length exponent1/3;
the countermodel is not a geometric flow. No novelty claim is made for
flattening. These quantitative estimates and dynamic deductions are not yet
Lean-formalized. The unrestricted length-growth problem remains unresolved.
