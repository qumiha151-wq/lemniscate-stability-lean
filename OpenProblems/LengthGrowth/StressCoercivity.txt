ELASTIC STRESS, CURVATURE CONCENTRATION, AND THE REMAINING DYNAMIC OBSTRUCTION
12 November 2026. Analytic proof draft; static estimates (1)--(2) now Lean verified.
The time-dependent consequences below remain analytic only. See
StaticCoercivityScope.txt for the exact formal statements and assumptions.
This note does NOT prove unrestricted quarter-power length growth.
No novelty or publication-priority claim is made.

1. STATEMENT.

Let gamma be any smooth closed regular planar immersion, parametrized by
arclength s in R/LZ. Let J be the fixed counterclockwise rotation by pi/2,
set N=JT, and write T_s=k N, N_s=-k T. Set

 E=(1/2) integral k^2,  F=k_ss+k^3/2,  D=integral F^2,
 M=max |k|,  A=integral |k|.

Then

                 E D >= 2^(-24) M^6,                         (1)
                 D >= 2^(-18) E^5/A^6.                     (2)

The constants are deliberately coarse. They are universal, independent of
length and turning number. E,M,A are positive by Fenchel. There is no
embeddedness assumption. Dependence on TOTAL ABSOLUTE CURVATURE A in (2)
is essential: it is not dependence only on the signed turning number.

The proof below is direct. It uses neither a uniform-in-length
Gagliardo--Nirenberg constant nor a compactness/ODE-limit argument.

2. ELEMENTARY PRELIMINARIES.

Fenchel and Cauchy--Schwarz imply EL>=2 pi^2>2. Put B=||k_s||_2.
Periodic integration by parts gives

 B^2=(1/2)integral k^4-integral k F
     <= E M^2+sqrt(2 E D).                                  (3)

There is a point with k^2<=2E/L. Integrating the derivative of k^2
from that point to a maximum point, then enlarging to the whole circle,
gives

 M^2 <= 2E/L+2 sqrt(2E) B <= E^2+2 sqrt(2E) B.               (4)

Suppose, toward a contradiction to (1), that

       r=sqrt(E D)/M^3 <= 1/4096,
       x=M/E.

Equations (3)--(4) imply

 x^2 <= 1+2 sqrt(2) sqrt(x^2+sqrt(2) r x^3).                (5)

In particular x<64. Indeed, if x>=64, use r<=1 and
1+sqrt(2)x<=2x to bound the right side by

 1+4 x sqrt(x) <= 1+x^2/2 < x^2,

a contradiction. Returning to (3), r x<=1/64 implies

                    B <= 2 sqrt(E) M.                     (6)

All estimates so far concern the actual periodic curvature, and no
assumption on sign or on the number of curvature zeros has been made.

3. THE ELASTIC STRESS CANNOT TURN ENOUGH TO CLOSE THE CURVE.

Define the planar stress vector and its magnitude by

 W=(k^2/2) T+k_s N,        w=|W|.

The Frenet equations give the exact identities

 W_s=F N,    (w^2)_s=2 k_s F.                              (7)

At a point s0 where |k|=M, one has k_s=0 and w(s0)^2=M^4/4.
By (6)--(7), at every point of the circle,

 |w^2-M^4/4| <= 2 B sqrt(D) <= 4 r M^4 <= M^4/1024.

Consequently W never vanishes, and the following coarser bounds suffice:

                    M^2/4 <= w <= M^2.                    (8)

Let e=W/w and v=e(s0). The component of W_s perpendicular to W gives

 |e_s|=|det(W,W_s)|/w^2=k^2 |F|/(2w^2).

Therefore, using (8),

 integral |e_s|
   <= (8/M^4) integral k^2 |F|
   <= (8/M^4) ||k^2||_2 sqrt(D)
   <= 8 sqrt(2) sqrt(E D)/M^3 = 8 sqrt(2) r < 1/2.          (9)

For all s, |e(s)-v|<1/2 and hence e(s).v>=1/2. This statement
uses the derivative of the UNIT stress, not the unweighted integral of
|F|; the latter would introduce an uncontrolled factor sqrt(L).

Set V=W/w^2. Differentiating inversion in the plane gives

 V_s=w^(-2)(I-2 e tensor e) W_s,
 |V_s|=|F|/w^2.                                           (10)

The map I-2 e tensor e is an orthogonal reflection, so the equality in
(10) is exact. The pointwise algebraic identity

                 T=(k^2/2)V-k_s J V                       (11)

follows by substituting W=(k^2/2)T+k_s N. Integrate (11), use
the genuine closing condition integral T=0, and integrate the second
term by parts. All factors are smooth and periodic, so there is no
boundary term:

           integral (k^2/2)V = - integral k J V_s.          (12)

The v-component of the left side is at least E/(2M^2), by (8)--(9).
The norm of the right side is at most 16 sqrt(2 E D)/M^4,
by (8), (10), and Cauchy--Schwarz. Thus

 E/(2M^2) <= 16 sqrt(2 E D)/M^4,
 1 <= 32 sqrt(2) r (M/E)
   <= 32 sqrt(2) (1/4096) 64 = sqrt(2)/2 < 1,

a contradiction. Hence r>1/4096, which proves the slightly weaker
non-strict assertion (1). Finally 2E=integral k^2<=M A; inserting
M>=2E/A into (1) proves (2).

4. CONSEQUENCES FOR AN ACTUAL FREE ELASTIC FLOW.

Now gamma(t) is a genuine smooth closed free elastic flow, with the same
normalization as the paper: normal speed -F. Its exact identities are

 E'=-D,  L'=integral k F,  (L')^2<=2 E D.

(a) A new unconditional space-time curvature bound follows from (1):

 integral_s^t ||k(u)||_infinity^6 du
      <= 2^23 (E(s)^2-E(t)^2).                             (13)

This has the correct scaling. It does not imply quarter-power growth.

(b) If A(t)<=A0 for all t, (2) implies

 E(t) <= [E(0)^(-4)+2^(-16) t/A0^6]^(-1/4).

The already established restart estimate and dyadic summation then yield
L(t)=O(t^(1/4)). Conversely, a bounded EL implies A^2<=2EL, so A is
bounded. Combined with the previously established equivalence between
quarter-power growth and bounded EL, this gives an exact new reduction:

 For an individual actual flow,

  L(t)=O((1+t)^(1/4))
    iff sup_t E(t)L(t)<infinity
    iff sup_t integral |k(t)| ds <infinity.                (14)

This is an equivalence, not a proof that these quantities are bounded.

(c) There is also a finite-time record bound which avoids an assumed
decay estimate. Put U=EL. If U'>0, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 D < 2E^3/L^2.

Combining with (2) gives

              U < 512 sqrt(2) A^3 < 1024 A^3.             (15)

For A_*(t)=max_{0<=u<=t} A(u), the usual first-crossing argument yields

 U(t) <= max{U(0),1024 A_*(t)^3}.                           (16)

For precision, fix a final time t and put K=max{U(0),1024 A_*(t)^3}.
If U(t)>K, take the connected component of {u in [0,t]: U(u)>K}
containing t, and let a be its left endpoint. Continuity gives U(a)=K
(also when a=0), and U>K on (a,t]. The mean-value theorem on [a,t]
provides c in (a,t) with U'(c)>0 and U(c)>K>=1024 A(c)^3,
contradicting (15).

Thus a counterexample to quarter-power growth must genuinely create
unbounded total absolute curvature over its evolution. A family of
different initial curves with many oscillations does not do this.

5. WHY THIS DOES NOT CLOSE THE ORIGINAL PROBLEM.

There is currently no proof here that A(t) stays bounded along every
free elastic flow, and no constructed flow for which it becomes unbounded.
The fourth-order equation does not give a Sturm zero-number principle.
Nor is A monotone: at times when all curvature zeros are simple,

 A'=2 sum_{k(z)=0} sign(k_s(z)) k_sss(z).                    (17)

To derive (17), differentiate integral |k| using
k_t=-F_ss-k^2 F and (ds)_t=k F ds. The zero-order terms cancel;
integration against the distributional derivative of sign(k) then yields
2 sum sign(k_s) F_s, and F_s=k_sss at a curvature zero.

One explicit family of actual closed initial curves has A'>0. Let q be
a nonzero integer, N>=4|q| an integer, and set on [0,2pi]

 K(u)=sin u-(1/9)sin(3u)
     =(2/3)sin u+(4/9)sin^3 u,
 phi(u)=-cos u+(1/27)cos(3u),
 theta(s)=q s+phi(Ns),
 T(s)=exp(i theta(s)), gamma(s)=integral_0^s T.

Successive 2pi/N cells of T differ by exp(2pi i q/N), whose geometric
sum vanishes because 0<|q|<N. Hence gamma is exactly closed, unit speed,
smooth, and has turning number q. Its curvature is k=q+N K(Ns).

At each zero, sin(Ns)=z where (2/3)z+(4/9)z^3=-q/N. This has a unique
z, with |z|<=3|q|/(2N)<=3/8. There are exactly two simple zeros per
cell. Since

 K'=cos u [(2/3)+(4/3)sin^2 u],
 K'''=2 cos u [1-6 sin^2 u],

one has sign(K')K'''=2 sqrt(1-z^2)(1-6z^2)>0 at each zero.
Equation (17) gives the exact positive initial derivative

       A'=8 N^5 sqrt(1-z^2)(1-6z^2)>0.

This last example only excludes a monotonicity shortcut. Like the earlier
static constructions, it is not a long-time counterexample.
