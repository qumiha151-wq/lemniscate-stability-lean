INDEPENDENT REVIEW: DYNAMIC LYAPUNOV AND FLATTENING NOTE
2026-11-12. Reviewer: length_migration subagent.
Reviewed: work/next_research/length_dynamic_flattening.txt.

Verdict: the stated PDE inequalities and deductions are correct. No
substantive sign, interpolation, or constant error was found. This is
analytic review, not Lean verification or a novelty assessment.

The source identity for Y'=d/dt integral k_s^2 was checked against the
cached original paper, work/paper.txt, Lemma3.1, beginning at line309:
  Y'=-2Z+5 integral k^2 k_ss^2-(5/3)integral k_s^4
      -(11/2)integral k^4 k_s^2.
The normal-gauge commutator and tangent identity in the note also agree
with that original proof.

Periodic integration by parts gives Y<=sqrt(2EX) and X<=sqrt(YZ).
Combining these, with the zero cases handled trivially, yields
  X<=(2E)^(1/3)Z^(2/3),  Y^3<=4E^2 Z.
These estimates do not have a hidden dependence on length. The Young
bound a z^(2/3)<=z+4a^3/27 is exact at z=(2a/3)^3. Substituting
a=5M^2(2E)^(1/3) gives the coefficient1000/27, as claimed.

Thus Y'<=-Z+(1000/27)EM^6 minus the two displayed nonnegative terms.
The stress estimate gives EM^6<=2^24 E^2D, while (E^3)'=-3E^2D.
The cancellation constant must therefore be1000*2^24/81, exactly the
value recorded. The resulting Y+C_*E^3 is nonnegative and nonincreasing.
Integrating controls all three nonnegative spacetime terms. Since E has
a finite limit, Y also has a limit; integral Y^3<infinity forces it to0.

The proof that L(t)->infinity, rather than just having an unbounded
subsequence, is valid. On each disjoint unit interval beginning with
L(t_n)<=R, the restart estimate gives L<=R+E(0). Fenchel then gives
E>=2pi^2/(R+E(0)), and the virial estimate gives
D>=16pi^6/(R+E(0))^5. Infinitely many such intervals contradict finite
total dissipation. The subsequent M^2 estimate and M(t)->0 follow.

The estimate
  integral F_s^2<=2Z+(9/2)integral k^4 k_s^2
is the elementary square inequality and is controlled by the Lyapunov
dissipation. In normal gauge T_t=-F_s N, so the claimed arclength-weighted
tangent action is indeed finite. It is squared action, not an assertion
that integral ||T_t||_2 dt is finite, and the spatial measure is moving.
Neither stronger assertion is needed or claimed in the draft.

The numerical powers in the explicitly labelled scalar diagnostic also
check out. For example Y^3=4E^2Z exactly, and already Y'<=-Z before
adding the positive forcing allowance. The example does not satisfy an
asserted geometric PDE; the note explicitly distinguishes these scalar
consequences from an actual curve, which is essential.

The remaining issue is unchanged: Y->0, M->0, and finite tangent action
on the expanding physical domain do not provide a bound on the total
variation A of the tangent. No claim of solving unrestricted length
growth is justified by these estimates alone.
