FIXED-TURNING, CLOSED-CURVE OBSTRUCTION TO NORMALIZED-ENERGY MONOTONICITY
12 November 2026. Human proof; not yet Lean-formalized.

This note proves a geometric obstruction, not an example of faster-than-quarter
long-time length growth. It is independent of the supplied lemniscate stability
theorem and stronger in turning-number scope than the earlier conditional
obstruction in work/global_length/scalar_obstruction.txt.

THEOREM.
For every nonzero integer q there is a sequence of smooth closed unit-speed
immersions gamma_N : R/(2 pi Z) -> R^2, all of turning number q and length 2 pi,
such that E(gamma_N)L(gamma_N) -> infinity and

  d/dt [E(gamma_N(t)) L(gamma_N(t))] at t=0 > 0,

where gamma_N(t) is their free elastic flow. In particular, for no nonzero fixed
turning number is there a finite threshold above which EL is nonincreasing for
every initial curve of that turning number. The construction below works for
every integer N >= 12 |q|.

CONVENTIONS.
E = (1/2) integral k^2 ds, F = k_ss + k^3/2, D = integral F^2 ds.
The free elastic flow has normal velocity -F, E'=-D, L'=integral kF ds.
Consequently U'= (EL)'= -L D + E integral kF ds.

1. THE PERIODIC WAVE.

Set m=2/3. Distinguish the complete elliptic integrals K_e and E_e from the
curvature K(s):

  K_e = integral_0^(pi/2) (1-m sin^2 x)^(-1/2) dx,
  E_e = integral_0^(pi/2) (1-m sin^2 x)^(1/2) dx,
  omega = 2 K_e/pi >= 1,  lambda = omega^2/3.

With the real Jacobi functions, define

  phi(s) = 2 arcsin(sqrt(m) sn(omega s | m)),
  K(s)   = phi'(s) = 2 omega sqrt(m) cn(omega s | m).

Because m<1, arcsin is applied strictly inside its smooth real domain. Since
sn has real period 4 K_e and dn is positive, phi is smooth and 2 pi-periodic.
The standard differential identities can also be obtained directly from
sn'=cn dn, cn'=-sn dn, dn'=-m sn cn and sn^2+cn^2=1:

  K'' + K^3/2 = (2m-1) omega^2 K = lambda K,
  K(s+pi)=-K(s),  max K^2 = (8/3) omega^2.

Write L=2 pi, A2=integral_0^L K^2 ds, A4=integral_0^L K^4 ds.
All odd moments of K vanish by its half-period antisymmetry. Changing variables
to the elliptic amplitude gives

  A2/L = 4 omega^2 (E_e/K_e - 1/3),
  A4 <= (8/3) omega^2 A2.

For completeness the first formula follows from
integral_0^(4K_e) sn^2 x dx = 4(K_e-E_e)/m and cn^2=1-sn^2.

We need an elementary, entirely non-numerical lower bound for E_e/K_e.
For x in [0,pi/2], put

  a=1-m sin^2 x, b=1-m cos^2 x, c=sqrt(1-m).

Then ab=1-m+m^2 sin^2 x cos^2 x >= 1-m, and

  (sqrt(a)+sqrt(b))/(1/sqrt(a)+1/sqrt(b)) = sqrt(ab) >= c.

Integrate the resulting inequality and use the change x -> pi/2-x to obtain
E_e >= c K_e. Hence

  A2/(2L)-lambda
    = (2 E_e/K_e-1) omega^2
    >= (2/sqrt(3)-1) omega^2
    > omega^2/7.

The final strict inequality is exact: 2/sqrt(3)>8/7 follows by squaring from
49>48. Thus A2>0 and lambda>0.

2. AN EXACTLY CLOSED CURVE OF PRESCRIBED TURNING NUMBER.

Choose integer q != 0 and integer N >=12 |q|. On the real line set

  theta_N(s)=q s+phi(Ns),
  T_N(s)=(cos(theta_N(s)),sin(theta_N(s))),
  gamma_N(s)=integral_0^s T_N(r) dr.

Let h=2 pi/N and let R be rotation by angle 2 pi q/N. Periodicity of phi gives
T_N(s+h)=R T_N(s), while T_N(s+2 pi)=T_N(s). If d=integral_0^h T_N ds, then

  integral_0^(2 pi) T_N ds = (I+R+...+R^(N-1))d = 0.

Indeed R^N=I and R != I because 0<|q|<N. Equivalently, the relevant complex
geometric series sums to zero. Therefore gamma_N is a smooth closed curve,
including all endpoint derivatives. It is an immersion of unit speed since
|T_N|=1. Its signed curvature is

  k_N(s) = theta_N'(s) = q+N K(Ns),

and its turning number is q, since phi is periodic. No embeddedness assertion
is made or required. Since N is an integer, all integrals of functions of K(Ns)
over [0,2 pi] equal those of the corresponding function of K(s).

3. EXACT INSTANTANEOUS ENERGY AND DISSIPATION FORMULAE.

At this initial curve,

  E_N = (L q^2 + N^2 A2)/2,

  F_N = lambda N^3 K(Ns) + (3q/2) N^2 K(Ns)^2
                         + (3q^2/2) N K(Ns) + q^3/2.

Using vanishing odd moments gives the exact polynomial identities

  L'_N = lambda A2 N^4 + 3 q^2 A2 N^2 + L q^4/2,

  D_N = lambda^2 A2 N^6
      + q^2 (3 lambda A2 + 9 A4/4) N^4
      + (15/4) q^4 A2 N^2 + L q^6/4,

  U'_N = lambda A2 (A2/2-L lambda) N^6
       + q^2 [(3/2) A2^2-(5/2)L lambda A2-(9/4)L A4] N^4
       - 2 q^4 L A2 N^2.

These identities involve the actual differential of the geometric functionals
at the actual closed initial curve, not independent scalar model histories.

4. A FULLY EXPLICIT POSITIVITY THRESHOLD.

Use A2/(2L)-lambda>omega^2/7 and lambda=omega^2/3 to bound the leading
coefficient below by L A2 omega^4/21. Drop the nonnegative (3/2)A2^2 term
and use A4 <= (8/3)omega^2 A2 to bound the next coefficient below by

  -(5/2)L lambda A2-(9/4)L A4 >= -(41/6)L A2 omega^2.

Thus

  U'_N > L A2 N^2 [omega^4 N^4/21
                   -(41/6) q^2 omega^2 N^2 - 2 q^4].

Set z=omega^2 N^2/q^2. Since omega>=1 and N>=12|q|, z>=144. The bracket is

  q^4 P(z),  P(z)=z^2/21-41z/6-2.

Now P(144)=10/7>0 and P'(z)=2z/21-41/6>0 for z>=144. Therefore U'_N>0.
Also U_N=L(L q^2+N^2 A2)/2 -> infinity. This proves the theorem.

5. FURTHER EXACT CONSEQUENCE AND LIMITATION.

For each fixed q, D_N/E_N^5 is O(N^(-4)) and hence tends to zero. Thus no
positive constant depending only on q can give D>=c(q) E^5 for all closed
immersions of turning number q. This rules out a standard direct coercivity
route to energy decay with quarter-power rate.

The conclusion U'_N>0 does NOT imply U is unbounded on any one trajectory.
The index N varies the initial curve; it is not a time parameter. The family
could have an arbitrarily large but finite excursion followed by quarter-power
growth for each individual N. In particular, the original long-time length
question is still unresolved by this construction. An argument using a bound
depending on the full initial shape, rather than only q or a universal U
threshold, remains possible.

APPENDIX: INCLUDING TURNING NUMBER ZERO WITHOUT A STABILITY HYPOTHESIS.

The same obstruction actually holds for every integer q, including zero, by
a closure correction. The explicit N>=12|q| threshold above applies only to
q!=0; the following construction is asymptotic but removes that restriction.

Let beta be ANY smooth closed unit-speed immersion of length L=2pi and turning
number q. Such a beta exists for q=0 by taking a smooth figure eight and scaling
and reparametrizing it by arclength. Write its tangent as T0(s)=exp(i theta0(s)),
with theta0(s+L)=theta0(s)+2pi q. Let

  g(s)=exp(i phi(s)),   r=(1/L)integral_0^L g(s) ds = 2 E_e/K_e-1 >0.

The last equality follows from cos(phi)=1-2m sn^2 and the odd symmetry of
sin(phi); positivity follows from the elliptic-integral estimate above.

Choose two fixed smooth real periodic functions h1,h2 such that the two real
vectors integral i T0(s) hj(s) ds are linearly independent. This is possible:
a nonconstant smooth closed unit-speed planar curve cannot have all its tangent
directions parallel. Pick two points with nonparallel tangents, and take small
nonnegative smooth bump functions near them. Their integrals are arbitrarily
close in direction to the corresponding independent normal vectors.

For alpha=(alpha1,alpha2) in R^2 set

  C_N(alpha) = integral_0^L T0(s) g(Ns) exp(i(alpha1 h1(s)+alpha2 h2(s))) ds.

Here N ranges over positive integers. Periodic averaging gives

  C_N(0) -> r integral T0 ds =0,
  D C_N(0) -> J,

where J has columns r integral iT0 hj ds and is invertible. These averaging
limits can be proved without a Fourier series: g-r has a bounded smooth
periodic primitive G, and integration by parts gives an O(1/N) error for each
smooth test function. The Hessians of C_N are uniformly bounded independently
of N and alpha on every fixed real ball, since |T0 g exp(i alpha.h)|=1.

Here is a quantitative justification for exact closure. Choose a small fixed
ball about alpha=0 so that its radius times a common Hessian bound, multiplied
by ||J^(-1)||, is at most 1/4. For all sufficiently large N,
||I-J^(-1) D C_N(0)||<=1/4. Hence the map

  H_N(alpha)=alpha-J^(-1) C_N(alpha)

has Lipschitz constant at most 1/2 on that fixed ball. For large N it preserves
the closed ball of radius 2||J^(-1) C_N(0)|| about zero, which is contained in
the fixed ball. Banach's fixed-point theorem gives alpha_N with C_N(alpha_N)=0
and |alpha_N| <= 2||J^(-1) C_N(0)|| ->0.

Define

  theta_N(s)=theta0(s)+phi(Ns)+alpha_N.h(s),
  gamma_N(s)=integral_0^s exp(i theta_N(r)) dr.

These are exactly closed smooth unit-speed immersions, with turning number q.
All derivatives of b_N(s)=theta0'(s)+alpha_N.h'(s) are uniformly bounded, and

  k_N(s)=N K(Ns)+b_N(s),
  F_N(s)=lambda N^3 K(Ns)+O(N^2),

uniformly in s. Consequently, using periodic averaging (or just integer-period
integration for the leading terms),

  E_N/N^2 -> A2/2,
  D_N/N^6 -> lambda^2 A2,
  L'_N/N^4 -> lambda A2,
  U'_N/N^6 -> lambda A2(A2/2-L lambda)>0.

Also U_N -> infinity. This establishes the high-EL positive-U' obstruction in
every turning-number class, including q=0, without assuming any long-time
stability result. For q=0 no numerical threshold for N is claimed.

As an additional geometric check, gamma_N converges uniformly to r beta after
the common choice of initial point: integrate the oscillatory mean-zero part
using the bounded primitive and use alpha_N->0. Thus these examples can lie in
a fixed bounded region even though EL diverges. They still give no one actual
trajectory with unbounded EL over time.
