# Whole-curve migration along a flow with one exact similarity return

Suppose one pair of distinct slices of a smooth regular closed free elastic flow starting at \(t=0\) agrees up to a direct similarity and a diffeomorphism of the parameter circle. Write \(K(t)\) for each actual image. Let \(c\) be the fixed point of the matching similarity \(S\), with dilation \(\lambda>1\). The flow admits a pair of strictly line-separated slices **if and only if** at least one slice in one recurrence period has \(c\notin\operatorname{conv}K(t)\). When the latter condition holds, *every* prescribed slice is strictly separated from slices at arbitrarily large times.

Indeed geometric uniqueness in both time directions gives a time \(T<0\) and the identity \(K(f(u))=S K(u)\) for all \(u\ge0\), where \(f(u)=T+\lambda^4(u-T)\). The proof of this step and of the common-centre property, including immersed curves with multiplicities, is in the [similarity return theorem](../../../SolitonClassification/Research/SimilarityReturns/SimilarityReturns.md). Each image is compact. Each time \(u\ge0\) can be written \(f^n(v)\) for \(v\in[0,f(0)]\), by taking successive inverse clock iterates until reaching the basic interval. Convex hulls transform by the same similarity, so if the centre belongs to each hull in that interval, all hulls contain it. Then two slices cannot lie in opposite strict half-planes.

If a phase \(v\) has an exterior centre, separation of the point from the compact convex hull gives a unit vector \(e\) and \(\delta>0\) with \(e\cdot(z-c)\ge\delta\) on \(K(v)\). Write \(R\) for the rotation part of \(S\). For \(z\in K(f^n(v))\), one has \((R^ne)\cdot(z-c)\ge\lambda^n\delta\). For an arbitrary prescribed earlier image \(K(u)\), the corresponding projection is at most \(\max_{z\in K(u)}|z-c|\), independently of \(n\). Any intermediate projection value strictly separates the images for all sufficiently large \(n\); also \(f^n(v)\to\infty\).

If the *initial* hull contains \(c\), some other phase may still have an exterior centre and the preceding proof separates that later phase from the initial slice. The exterior test for a flow with a discrete recurrence needs the entire basic interval. For a continuously self-similar flow, checking the initial slice suffices, as proved in [SimilarityMigration.md](../SimilarityMigration.md).

The Lean theorem \(\texttt{discrete_migration_equivalences}\) proves all three geometric conditions equivalent for any family of nonempty compact plane images satisfying the stated single-step clock and image recurrence. The angular multiplier may even vary from one step to the next. The module checks the clock iteration, reduction to the first period and arbitrarily late separation for every prescribed slice. Deriving that recurrence from one matching physical pair and geometric uniqueness is the mathematical argument cited above. An actual closed flow with an exterior phase remains to be found or excluded. [RecurringMigration.lean](../../Migration/RecurringMigration.lean)

## A symmetry at a single time

There is a second exclusion that requires no return and applies to time-dependent changes of shape. If at **any one time** the parametrized immersion is preserved up to diffeomorphism by a nonidentity rotation about a point \(c\), then its arclength centroid equals \(c\) at every time. In particular every slice has \(c\) in its convex hull. A migrating smooth closed flow cannot possess such a symmetry at any earlier or later time. For an embedded slice it suffices to check symmetry of its image, since an isometry preserving an embedded loop lifts through that embedding to a diffeomorphism of the parameter circle.

To prove this, compare \(X(t)\) directly with the fixed rotation \(S X(t)\). Both are geometric free elastic flows, agree up to reparametrization at the given time, and hence agree throughout every common smooth time interval by the two-sided uniqueness argument cited above. Arclength integration, including all traversals, is preserved under reparametrization. Its centroid therefore satisfies \(S\bar X(t)=\bar X(t)\). A nonidentity rotation has just one fixed point, giving \(\bar X(t)=c\). The centroid of a compact curve lies in its convex hull by arclength integration of linear functionals and finite-dimensional convex separation. This also prevents escape of the arclength centroid in this class.
