Projected stress, positive adjoint weights, and the expansion center
20 November 2026

Consider a smooth unit-speed periodic planar curve x, tangent T=x', normal
N=JT, curvature k, and the pure homothetic free-elastic-flow equation

  F=k''+k^3/2=-lambda (x.N),      lambda>0.

Here the origin is the actual expansion center. Write R=max |x| and
K=conv(x). If lambda R^4 <= 2, then 0 is in the interior of K. All positively
dilated copies of such a profile therefore have a common point in their
convex hulls. They cannot realize complete migration across a line.

This includes equality in the radial bound and imposes no curvature-sign,
symmetry, turning-number, embeddedness, or closeness hypothesis. It is
a sufficient condition for a pure homothetic profile. No conclusion for
unrestricted R or general time-dependent flows is asserted.

1. A one-dimensional adjoint equation

Rescale x by lambda^(1/4) and arclength by the same factor, so lambda=1.
Put p=x.N, q=x.T, v=k'. The actual profile system reads

  k'=v,  v'=-k^3/2-p,  p'=-kq,  q'=1+kp.

For the stress Q=k^2 T/2+k'N one has Q'=F N=-pN=-x+qT and
Q=x'''+(3/2)k^2x'. For each fixed direction e, h=x.e thus solves

  Lh=h''''+((3/2)k^2 h')'-q h'+h=0.

Repeated integration by parts around a full period implies the vector
identity int (L*phi) x ds=0 for every smooth periodic scalar weight, where

  L*phi=phi''''+((3/2)k^2 phi')'+(q phi)'+phi.

An everywhere nonnegative adjoint density, with positive integral, puts
the center in the convex hull. To get interior, suppose x.e>=0 for some
unit e. Then (L*phi)(x.e)=0 everywhere. The support of positive density
contains an open arc, on which the image must lie on the line x.e=0.
The unit-speed image is straight there, so k=v=p=0 there. The support
system with this initial jet has the unique solution k=v=p=0,
q(s)=q(s0)+(s-s0) for all s. This contradicts periodicity. A separating
or supporting line through 0 therefore cannot exist.

2. Explicit positive density and its radius threshold

Use s0=|x|^2/2, so s0'=q. With A0=(3/2)k^2 one gets

  s0'''+A0 s0'=v p+(1/2)k^2 q,
  (v p+(1/2)k^2 q)'=(1/2)k^2-p^2.

Consequently

  L*1=2+kp,
  L*s0=(1/2)k^2+2q^2+(1/2)kp |x|^2.

Set a=R^2/2 and phi=a+s0. Its density rho can be written

  rho=(1/2)[k+p(a+s0)]^2+
       R^2+2q^2-(1/2)p^2(a+s0)^2.

Here p^2<=R^2 and a+s0<=R^2. Hence

  rho >= (1/2)[k+p(a+s0)]^2 + 2q^2 + R^2(1-R^4/2).

Under R^4<=2 this is nonnegative. Moreover int rho ds=int phi ds>0
because L 1=1 and R>0 on a regular closed curve. The interior assertion
follows. Dilation back gives lambda R^4<=2 as stated.

3. The radius criterion is optimal for local radial multipliers

A radial weight has phi=f(s0), f of class C4. At a jet (k,v,p,q), the
only term of L*f(s0) depending on the independent curvature derivative v
is 4 f''(s0) q p v. Indeed the fourth-derivative chain rule gives the
term 4f'' s0' s0''' with s0'''=v p-k^2 q, and the terms multiplying
f' combine to k^2/2+q^2-p^2 after including (A0 phi')'+q phi'.
Every quadruple is a legitimate local initial condition of the smooth
profile ODE, with x=pN+qT and x'=T after integrating the Frenet equation.
For any prescribed 0<|x|<R one may take pq nonzero and v of either sign
and arbitrarily large magnitude. Thus pointwise nonnegativity on all
such jets forces f''=0 and f(s0)=A+B s0.

At q=0,p=r the density becomes

  2A + (B/2)k^2 + kr(A+Br^2/2).

A nonzero affine radial weight with this expression nonnegative for all
k and 0<r<R requires B>0 and A>0. Indeed B<0 fails at large k, and
B=0 forces A=0 by varying k. A=0 fails the quadratic discriminant for
r>0. Minimizing over k gives

  4AB >= r^2(A+Br^2/2)^2 >= 2AB r^4.

Letting r approach R gives R^4<=2. The previous construction attains it.
This sharpness refers to uniform positivity of a local radial certificate
on arbitrary admissible jets. It says nothing about which of those jets
belong to a closed orbit or about optimality of the geometric hull theorem.

4. Finite exact obstruction to unrestricted low-degree jet weights

The accompanying script migration_adjoint_weight_probe.py differentiates
polynomials using the displayed normalized ODE and checks the two formulas
of Section 2 over the rational numbers. It also produces a finite positive
rational convex combination of local jets on which L*phi has mean zero,
simultaneously for every invariant jet polynomial phi of total degree at
most five. Reflection changes signs of (k,v,p) and orientation reversal
changes signs of (k,p,q). Under reversal the odd spatial derivatives and
q switch signs together, so L* commutes with both actions. If a general
polynomial of the same degree had nonnegative and nonzero L*phi, its
group average would be invariant, nonnegative and nonzero: at a jet of
strict positivity its average includes that positive summand.

The degree-five invariant weight space has dimension 30. The certificate
has 31 atoms, and the exact matrix of adjoint monomials at these atoms
has full row rank 30. Indeed the 31-by-31 matrix obtained by appending the
constant-one row is invertible, as the script verifies. A nonnegative
density whose positive weighted mean on these atoms is zero must vanish
at all 31. Full row rank forces every invariant weight coefficient to be
zero. Thus even nonnegative, nonzero polynomial densities are ruled out.
A numerical LP only selects the support. The script independently solves
and verifies the entire moment system using exact rational arithmetic,
checking strict positivity and total mass one. The full record is
migration_adjoint_weight_probe.json.
An equivalent check reads the rational atoms directly and evaluates each
adjoint monomial on them. For the general geometric problem one could
still use positivity only along closed orbits, a higher-degree or
nonpolynomial weight, or a weight with nonlocal dependence.
