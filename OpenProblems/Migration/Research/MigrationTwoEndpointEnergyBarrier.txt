Two endpoint costs for complete half-plane migration of a closed free elastic flow
20 November 2026

Consider a smooth closed regular planar free elastic flow on [0,T].
Use arclength ds on each slice and the convention

     gamma_t=-F N,    F=k_ss+k^3/2,    E(t)=(1/2) int k^2 ds.

Then (ds)_t=kF ds and E'=-int F^2 ds in the normal material gauge.
A moving tangential parametrization can be changed to this gauge on the
compact time interval. All hypotheses and conclusions below concern images
and geometric integrals.

Write h=e dot gamma for one fixed unit direction and W(t)=max h-min h.
Miura and Wheeler ask whether there is an actual closed flow for which
max h(T)<min h(0). Equivalently a common separating line can be placed in
the strict gap. Set E0=E(0), ET=E(T), W0=W(0), WT=W(T).

THEOREM. A necessary condition for this strict separation is

    T (E0-ET) >= B(W0,E0) + B(WT,E0),

    B(W,E) = W^3 / (pi+sqrt(2 E W))^2.                        (1)

Each term is paid by energy dissipation in a different physical half-plane.
There is no curvature or derivative supremum in (1). It applies after
restarting at any two times by replacing T with the time difference.

Proof of the localized length estimate

Take the nondecreasing C1 function p on the whole real line given by

    p(z)=0                    if z<=0,
         sin(pi*z/2)^2        if 0<=z<=1,
         1                    if z>=1.

We have 0<=p<=1, and |p'|<=pi*sqrt(p), including the two joining points.
Put a=min h(0), b=max h(T). Strict separation means b<a. The two spatial
weights along the trajectory are

    phi_+(h)=p((h-a)/W0),     phi_-(h)=p((b-h)/WT).

Both widths are positive. A zero projection width would make the whole
closed regular immersion a curve in a line, contradicting Rolle's theorem
for the remaining coordinate. The regions where the weights or their
derivatives are nonzero lie in h>a and h<b respectively. Call these regions
H+ and H-. Write M_i(t)=int phi_i(h) ds and D_i=int_{H_i} F^2 ds.
The sets H_i are fixed physical half-planes. Their preimages on the
material circle may vary.

For either weight of width W, differentiate the weighted length without
differentiating an indicator function. The weight is C1, and compactness and
smoothness justify differentiation under the material integral. This gives

  M' = int [k phi(h)-phi'(h)(N dot e)] F ds,
  |M'| <= sqrt(D_i) ( sqrt(2 E0) + (pi/W) sqrt(M) ).          (2)

Indeed the spatial L2 norm of k phi is at most sqrt(2 E(t)) because
phi<=1. The norm of phi'(h)(N dot e) is at most (pi/W)sqrt(M).
Use the triangle inequality, Cauchy--Schwarz and E(t)<=E0.
The derivative itself uses the actual dissipation density on H_i.

Define J_W(v)=int_0^v dm/(sqrt(2E0)+(pi/W)sqrt(max(m,0))).
It is C1, with positive derivative everywhere since E0>0. Equation (2)
implies |(J_W(M))'|<=sqrt(D_i). Integrate in time and apply Cauchy--Schwarz.
If one endpoint mass is zero and the other is M*, then

  T int_0^T D_i dt >= J_W(M*)^2.

The density D_i is measurable as a parameter integral of a bounded measurable
function on this compact slab. Signed or time-reversed FEF is unnecessary
when estimating the change from zero to M*.

At time zero the positive mass satisfies M_+(0)>=W0. Indeed take arclength
on the initial parameter circle and choose a maximum at s=0. The height is
1-Lipschitz in arclength. Its range W0 is at most L(0)/2. The two arcs of
length W0 to the left and right of this maximum are therefore disjoint except
possibly at their endpoints. At distance r in [0,W0] from the maximum the
weight is at least cos(pi*r/(2W0))^2. Both integrals add up to W0.
At time T we have M_+(T)=0. Apply the same argument to -h at time T to get
M_-(T)>=WT, and observe M_-(0)=0. Since the integrand defining J_W is
nonincreasing for v>=0,

    J_W(W) >= W / (sqrt(2E0)+pi/sqrt(W)).

Apply this once to each weight and sum. The disjointness of the open
half-planes gives D_+ + D_- <= int F^2 ds at each time. Integrating the
energy identity proves (1).

Curvature energy required in a strip

For the following direct lower bound define the dimensionless constant

     I = int_0^(pi/2) sqrt(sin v) dv,       c = 2 I^2.

Every C2 closed regular immersion satisfies E W >= c on every axis.
Choose horizontal/vertical coordinates so the height has the chosen
vertical direction. At a horizontal-coordinate extremum the unit tangent
is vertical. Reverse the vertical axis if needed so that Ty=1 there.
The component (u,v) of {Ty>0} around that point is proper since int Ty ds=0.
It can be lifted as a finite arclength interval. At its endpoints Ty=0.
There is a continuous differentiable angle on the lift with theta in (0,pi)
inside and theta equal to 0 or pi at each endpoint. One can obtain its
endpoint regularity by lifting the smooth tangent through the unit circle.
At the chosen point theta=pi/2, and |theta_s|=|k|. On each of the two
legs, the total variation of an antiderivative of sqrt(sin theta) is at
least I. Consequently

     2 I <= int_u^v |k| sqrt(Ty) ds,
     4 I^2 <= (int_u^v k^2 ds)(int_u^v Ty ds) <= 2 E W.       (3)

This argument also handles zero turning number. An elementary lower bound
on I is 1 since sqrt(sin theta)>=sin theta on the integration interval.

For nonzero signed turning number n, the stronger bound E W>=4 |n| I^2
holds. Reverse parametrization when needed to have degree |n|. Start the
angle lift at theta(0)=0 and write theta(L)=2 pi |n|. For j=1,...,2|n|,
choose the first hit b_j of j*pi, and the last hit a_j of (j-1)*pi
before b_j. Then a_j>=b_{j-1} (b_0=0), and each open interval (a_j,b_j)
has strictly one sign of Ty. The angle traverses a full half turn there.
Using |Ty| instead of Ty in (3) charges at least 4I^2/W in squared
curvature on each interval. Sum over the disjoint intervals and divide by 2.

These factors have a useful geometric interpretation. Equality in the
Cauchy--Schwarz estimate on a single monotone half-turn would require
|k|=C sqrt(|sin theta|). On its open arc k^2=C^2 |sin theta| gives
2 k_s=C^2 sign(sin theta) cos theta. Differentiating once more gives
k_ss=-k^3/2. Thus the equality arc has zero interior elastic force. Large
forces arising at joins or from oscillatory derivatives need a separate
time- and space-integrated dissipation budget.

Consequences for short-time multiscale configurations

Define f(x)=x^3/(pi+sqrt(2x))^2 for x>0. The square root of f is
x/(pi/sqrt(x)+sqrt(2)), which increases strictly. Multiplying (1) by
E0^3 and applying (3) at each endpoint yields

     T E0^3 (E0-ET) >= f(c) + f(c E0/ET) >= 2 f(c).           (4)

One can use c=4|n| I^2 on a nonzero-turning-number evolution since the
turning number persists along a smooth family of immersions. The weaker
fixed constant c=2 works for all curves and yields 2 f(2)=16/(pi+2)^2.
This supplies a positive energy-scale lower bound for T E0^4 as well.
For a sequence with bounded E0 W0, T/W0^4 cannot tend to zero in a
sequence of complete migrations. This follows directly by dividing (4)
by (E0 W0)^4 and using E0-ET<=E0. Spatial derivatives of the curvature
may be arbitrarily large in this statement. None of the estimates
eliminates evolution over times above the lower bound.

The primary literature supplies a different sufficient condition for
nonmigration, namely Novaga's initial normalized-energy bound
L int k^2 ds <= 4 pi^2+8/3. His preprint dated 4 November 2026 is at
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07242. The original question concerns whole
closed curves in Miura and Wheeler, Section 1.2, DOI
10.1016/j.jfa.2025.111030. The two-endpoint calculation above is a
handwritten derivation for the current research. The long-time unrestricted
question and a full Lean implementation of this derivation remain open.
