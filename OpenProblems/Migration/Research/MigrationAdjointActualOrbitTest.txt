An affine radial adjoint weight can fail on a genuine closed expander
20 November 2026

The normalized closed asymmetric expander previously certified in the
CentroidEscape project has the origin in the interior of its convex hull.
This follows already from the checked shoots and their reflection; the
quantitative verification is in CentroidCandidateSupportExclusion.txt.
Here that real solution tests the proposed hull proof itself.

Write U=L*1, V=L*(|x|^2/2) for the two densities computed in
MigrationProjectedAdjointHull.txt. On the certified expander there is NO
pair (A,B) other than (0,0) for which AU+BV is everywhere nonnegative.
Thus the affine radial certificate can fail even when the weight can
depend on the entire chosen closed orbit. The example is a countertest to
that certificate. The actual expander has no complete migration.

The validated initial parameters (a,k0,L) of the arclength half-orbit lie
within sup distance 10^-28 of the exact rational CandidateEndpointChecks
center. At its starting seam x=(a,0), T=(0,1), k=k0 and k'=0. Thus in
the intrinsic support system (k,v,p,q) the initial box centered at
(k0,0,-a,0) encloses the actual initial state. Half-period L>17/4.

At the three times 0, 17/4 and 1, denote the two-component values (U,V)
by A0, A1 and A2. Interval arithmetic gives the strict oriented bounds

  det(A0,A1)>25,   det(A1,A2)>21,   det(A2,A0)>3.

For example, A0 is within 10^-26 of (-2.104635423947336540992998613,
0.72320142201018033869046233), and A1 is near (-3.01056,-11.21264).
For the precise outward intervals use migration_adjoint_closed_orbit.json.

The identity det(A1,A2) A0 + det(A2,A0) A1 + det(A0,A1) A2=0 and the
three strictly positive determinants place 0 inside their triangle.
Consequently every nonzero linear functional takes both signs at these
times. In particular no nonzero A,B give an everywhere nonnegative
density. This uses the exact closed solution selected by the earlier
root theorem. It does not promote a finite approximate shooting root to
an actual trajectory.

Numerical validation. The reproducible Arb script
migration_adjoint_closed_orbit.py independently propagates the enlarged
initial box for (k,v,p,q) using the normalized polynomial support system.
Each step has h=1/128. A tube enlarged by one in every component satisfies
z0+[0,h]F(tube) strictly inside tube, component by component; the usual
first-exit argument keeps the solution inside for that step. The script
evaluates Taylor coefficients through order 21 from the current enclosed
initial state. The order-22 coefficient is bounded on the entire Picard
tube by formal ODE-series recurrence, and supplies the Taylor remainder.
Horner evaluation advances the box at 180-bit Arb precision. The strict
determinant signs hold by many decimal places. This independent interval
calculation and the proof above are not part of the Lean kernel audit.

To replay, install python-flint and pass the path to the pinned
CentroidEscape/CandidateEndpointChecks.lean as the sole command-line
argument to migration_adjoint_closed_orbit.py. The SHA-256 is checked.
