DYNAMIC DILATION DEFECT AND MIGRATION
13 November 2026. Analytic research; not Lean formalized.

STATUS
The general migration problem remains open. This note proves an exact
actual-flow decomposition, a quantitative necessary condition for a
complete directional crossing, and rigidity of the zero-defect case.
It does not construct a migrating trajectory. No centroid-divergence
example or rotationally symmetric profile is called a migration example.

1. WHERE THE PUBLISHED ENERGY THRESHOLD IS USED

Primary source checked: M. Novaga, Non-migration for planar elastic flows,
arXiv:2611.07242v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07242v1 .
Its notation is B=integral k^2, U=LB, and speed -(2k_ss+k^3)N;
our E=B/2 and speed -F N differ by a constant time factor.

The threshold U<=4*pi^2+8/3 has three separate jobs: Lemma 2.2 identifies
turning number +/-1; Proposition 3.1 proves forward invariance of the
sublevel and monotonicity of U; Lemmas 4.2--4.4 compare centroid mobility
with two-sided directional margins using the same constant 3/4. The
last comparison yields the contradiction in Section 6. Embeddedness is
not the only use of the threshold. The Section 4 shape estimates retain
explicit dependence on U-4*pi^2, but their comparison need not hold at
large defect. Without forward control, an initial bound alone also does
not control later coefficients. Therefore deleting the threshold from
that argument is unjustified. The following computations instead use
actual forces and geometric matrices, with no turning or energy bound.

2. THE NORMAL-DISTANCE REGRESSION CENTER

Let gamma be a smooth closed regular immersed planar curve, parameterized
instantaneously by arclength. Use T=gamma_s, N=JT, T_s=kN,
F=k_ss+k^3/2, E=(1/2)integral k^2 ds, D=integral F^2 ds.
Let c=L^(-1)integral gamma ds be the arclength centroid. Define

  A=integral N tensor N ds,
  d=A^(-1) integral (gamma dot N)N ds,
  p=(gamma-d) dot N,
  J=integral p^2 ds.                                   (1)

The matrix A is positive definite. Indeed, if a nonzero vector e had
integral(e dot N)^2=0, continuity would make T everywhere parallel to e.
Its unit length and continuity would make T constant, contradicting
closure. Thus d is well-defined; it is the unique minimizer of
z -> integral ((gamma-z) dot N)^2 ds. In particular

  integral p N ds=0.                                    (2)

There is also an elementary quantitative bound A >= (2/E) I as quadratic
forms. To see it, fix a unit e and put f=T dot e. Closure of the component
T dot Je gives a point where that component vanishes, hence |f|=1 there.
Closure also gives integral f=0, so max f-min f>=1. Periodicity implies
integral |f_s|>=2(max f-min f)>=2. Since f_s=k(N dot e),
Cauchy--Schwarz gives 4<=2E integral(N dot e)^2, as asserted.
This controls invertibility but does not place d in the convex hull.

The center d transforms naturally under translations, rotations and
positive dilations. It is reparametrization invariant. It is NOT asserted
to belong to the convex hull: an existing independent smooth-curve
counterexample to that assertion is in
work/one_sided/migration/balance_not_hull_obstruction.txt.

The stress W=(k^2/2)T+k_sN satisfies W_s=FN. Periodic integration gives

  integral F N ds=0,
  integral F p ds=-E.                                  (3)

For the second identity, integrate W_s dot (gamma-d), obtaining
-integral W dot T=-E. E>0 for a closed immersion, so (3) also proves J>0.

3. EXACT BEST-DILATION DECOMPOSITION OF THE ACTUAL FORCE

Set

  a=E/J >0,    w=F+a p,    Delta=D-E^2/J.

Then, without any approximation,

  integral w N ds=0,  integral w p ds=0,
  Delta=integral w^2 ds >=0,
  gamma_t=-FN=a p N-wN.                               (4)

Thus Delta is the exact squared normal-velocity residual after removing
the best normal dilation about d. The coefficient a minimizes
integral(F+b p)^2 over real b; it is positive by (3).
Equivalently, E^2/J is the sharp lower bound on dissipation using both
translation balance and the scaling identity at that actual curve.
This bound is shape-dependent, not a replacement for a universal
energy coercivity exponent.

All quantities are smooth in time along a smooth closed flow, because
A stays positive definite and J>0. Under ambient dilation by lambda,
a scales as lambda^(-4), w as lambda^(-3), and Delta as lambda^(-5).

There is an exact centroid identity with the same decomposition:

  c'=a(c-d)+(1/L)integral w k(gamma-c) ds.               (5)

Proof: translation balance implies L c'=integral F k(gamma-c).
Put q=(gamma-d) dot T, so q_s=1+kp. Integration by parts yields

  integral kp(gamma-c) ds=-integral q T ds=-L(c-d).

The last equality uses integral pN=0 and
(gamma-d)=qT+pN. Substitute F=-ap+w to obtain (5).

This separates actual centroid motion into a dilation contribution and
a true residual contribution. Large centroid drift alone still gives
no half-plane crossing.

4. SHARP DIRECTIONAL CENTROID BUDGETS WITHOUT AN ENERGY THRESHOLD

For a unit vector e, put h_e=k(gamma-c) dot e. In the Hilbert space
L2(ds), let P be orthogonal projection onto the finite dimensional span
of N_1,N_2,p, and define

  Q_e=integral |h_e-P h_e|^2 ds.

Since w is orthogonal to this span, (5) implies

  |c'_e-a(c_e-d_e)| <= sqrt(Delta Q_e)/L.               (6)

This is the optimal Cauchy--Schwarz bound using these orthogonality
constraints and the norm of w. It does not presume that d lies in the
hull or that the dilation part has a favorable sign.

A separate direct bound uses projection P_N onto span(N_1,N_2): set
  M_e=integral |h_e-P_N h_e|^2 ds.
Then |c'_e|<=sqrt(D M_e)/L. Consequently, if the entire curve is in
{x dot e>=h} at t0 and in {x dot e<=h} at t1, its actual margins obey

  rho_e^-(t0)+rho_e^+(t1)
     <= integral_t0^t1 sqrt(D M_e)/L dt
     <= sqrt(E(t0)-E(t1))
          * sqrt(integral_t0^t1 M_e/L^2 dt).            (7)

This gives a threshold-free necessary dissipation/shape budget for
migration. It is not an unconditional bound on that budget. Projection
can only strengthen the unprojected centroid estimate; on any round
circle, including a multiple cover, M_e=0.

5. A SUPPORT-BASED DYNAMIC OBSTRUCTION

The following goes directly to the whole curve rather than its centroid.
Fix a unit vector e and define the support value and regression-center
margin

  H_e(t)=max_u gamma(t,u) dot e,
  m_e(t)=H_e(t)-d(t) dot e.

At any support maximizer u, T dot e=0, hence N=+e or -e. From (4),

  gamma_t(t,u) dot e = a m_e-w(t,u)(N dot e).           (8)

The maximum of a smooth function over the compact parameter circle is
locally Lipschitz in time. Its right derivative is the maximum of the
time derivatives at the active maximizers; in particular, whenever the
ordinary derivative exists, (8) gives

  H_e' >= a m_e-||w||_infinity.                         (9)

Therefore the explicit condition

  ||w(t)||_infinity <= a(t)m_e(t)  for all t in [t0,t1] (10)

forces H_e to be nondecreasing. A complete migration from the upper to
the lower half-plane in direction e is impossible under (10): a closed
regular immersion cannot lie entirely in the separating line, so its
initial H_e is strictly greater than h, while its final H_e is at most h.

In particular, if d belongs to the interior of the convex hull and
r_d=dist(d,boundary(conv(gamma)))>0, the direction-independent condition

  ||w||_infinity <= a r_d                              (11)

throughout a time interval makes every support value nondecreasing.
Thus the convex hulls are nested outward on that interval. This is a
sufficient dynamic criterion, not a claim that free flow preserves it.

An actual migration must have a time at which (10) fails. More precisely,
H_e decreases between its endpoint times, so H_e'<0 on a positive-measure
set. At almost every such time,

  ||w||_infinity > a m_e.                              (12)

If m_e<0 then the regression center has already passed beyond the
support line in that direction; otherwise a nonzero residual must
overcome the outward dilation speed. This gives two distinct mechanisms
that a construction would have to realize. Neither is produced here.

One can replace the sup norm in this test by measurable quadratic data.
For any periodic scalar w, choosing a point with w^2<=Delta/L and
integrating (w^2)_s=2ww_s gives

  ||w||_infinity^2 <= Delta/L+2 sqrt(Delta Z_w),
  Z_w=integral w_s^2 ds.                               (13)

Here the derivative is actual and explicit:
  p_s=-kq,  w_s=F_s-a kq.
Thus at a decrease time with m_e>=0, a necessary condition is

  Delta/L+2 sqrt(Delta Z_w) > a^2 m_e^2.               (14)

This is a proved quantitative obstruction involving the real force and
position. It contains no small-energy, turning-number or embeddedness
assumption. Global estimates forcing its opposite have not been found.

6. ZERO DEFECT ON AN INTERVAL IS RIGID

If Delta vanishes at one time, continuity gives F=-ap on that slice.
If Delta(t)=0 for every t in a time interval, then the geometric flow
on that interval is a homothety about one FIXED point d0, and

  d(t)=d0, gamma(t)=d0+rho(t)(gamma(t0)-d0)

as unparameterized immersed curves, with rho'>0.

Here is a direct argument which does not assume the conclusion. From
(4), gamma_t=a[(gamma-d)-qT]. A smooth tangential reparametrization on
any compact time subinterval removes the term -a qT: solve its ordinary
parameter-flow equation, whose coefficients are smooth on the compact
parameter circle. The resulting beta obeys

  beta_t=a(t)(beta-d(t)).

Solving this scalar-coefficient linear vector ODE gives
  beta(t,u)=rho(t) beta(t0,u)+b(t),
  rho'=a rho, b'=a b-a d, rho(t0)=1, b(t0)=0.
By reparametrization invariance and similarity covariance of the center
in (1), d(t)=rho(t)d(t0)+b(t). Differentiating this equality and using
the ODEs gives d'=a(rho d(t0)+b-d)=0. This proves the fixed-center claim.

Consequently, in the zero-defect case, migration is possible only if
the fixed center lies outside the initial convex hull. Conversely, a
strictly separated homothetic profile gives migration after a suitable
ambient translation, as already proved in the independent Migration
criterion. The reduction does not prove that such a profile exists.

7. CONCRETE FAILURE OF A UNIVERSAL CENTROID-MARGIN REPLACEMENT

The small-defect directional centroid margins in Novaga's argument
cannot simply be replaced by rho_e^+/- >= C/E for all closed immersions,
with any fixed C>0. The following exact geometric family shows why.

For 0<u<1 take the regular arc

  alpha(u)=( exp(-1/(u(1-u))), u(1-u)(u+1/2) ),

extended at both endpoints by alpha(0)=alpha(1)=(0,0).
The first coordinate is positive inside and flat to all orders at the
endpoints. Its derivative vanishes only at u=1/2 in the interior; there
the second coordinate derivative is 1/4. At u=0 and 1 the second
derivatives with respect to the parameter are not needed for regularity:
the first derivatives of the second coordinate are respectively 1/2
and -3/2. Thus alpha is regular, starts vertically upward and ends
vertically downward. After arclength parametrization its curvature and
all curvature derivatives vanish at the endpoints, allowing smooth
joining to straight vertical segments.

For H>0 concatenate: the upward segment (0,0) to (0,H), the translate
alpha+(0,H), the downward segment back to (0,0), and the reflection
(alpha_x,-alpha_y). This is a smooth closed regular immersion. The
two long straight segments overlap; embeddedness is deliberately not
assumed in the original migration problem.

Let ell_alpha be the fixed cap length, E_alpha its positive bending
energy, and M_alpha=integral_alpha x ds>0. The resulting curve satisfies

  L_H=2H+2ell_alpha, E_H=2E_alpha,
  min x=0, c_x=M_alpha/(H+ell_alpha).

Hence the lower centroid margin in the x direction tends to zero while
E_H remains fixed: E_H rho_x^- ->0. Translating the whole curve slightly
right makes it initially lie in a strict half-plane without changing
this margin. This is not a migrating solution and not a counterexample
to Novaga's theorem: U_H grows without bound. It isolates one minimal
geometric degeneration that any threshold-free centroid argument must
handle. A different center, such as (1), avoids the length-mass bias but
introduces the separate, genuine issue that it can lie outside the hull.

8. REMAINING GAP AND NEXT TEST

The usable new objects are a(t), d(t), Delta(t), and the actual support
margin m_e(t). No invariant region controlling them has been proved.
The preceding estimates would support either of two rigorous routes:

* prove persistence of d in the hull together with a residual bound
  strong enough for (10) or an integrated version of (9); or
* construct an actual trajectory for which the center exits the relevant
  support region, or the residual overcomes expansion for long enough
  to reverse the whole-curve support. Local support decrease is not
  itself complete migration.

The exact zero-defect reduction shows why a strict-half-plane expander
would suffice, but does not justify repeating the already unsuccessful
finite half-return searches. The positive-defect mechanism genuinely
allows shape change; it requires new dynamical control or a validated
time-dependent construction. At present neither route is completed.
