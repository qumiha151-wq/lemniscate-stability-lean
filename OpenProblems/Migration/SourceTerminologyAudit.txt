1. What Andrews--Wheeler actually claim

Primary source: Ben Andrews and Glen Wheeler, Jellyfish exist,
arXiv:2601.21227v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21227v1 .
Relevant location: Introduction, Figure 2 caption and the first paragraph of
the immediately following subsection Open questions and outlook, before
Section 2. In the current HTML text these are lines154--157.

The paper describes its numerically observed asymmetric examples as
non-symmetric "one-sided" jellyfish. The caption identifies the black point
as the expansion center and describes lack of dihedral symmetry. The following
paragraph leaves rigorous existence of these additional profiles open.

NEITHER that paragraph NOR the figure caption says that the entire curve lies
in a strict half-plane whose boundary passes through the expansion center.
They do not assert that the center is outside the convex hull. Therefore the
word "one-sided" here must not be silently upgraded to the exact separation
condition needed for a pure-homothety migration example.

Recommended status wording:
  Numerical asymmetric jellyfish-like profiles (called "one-sided"
  descriptively in Figure2) are reported; their rigorous existence is left
  open. This wording does not assert strict half-plane separation from the
  expansion center and supplies no migration example.

The older note work/one_sided/migration/structural_constraints.txt, Section5,
already states this distinction correctly. This audit confirms its reading.
The currently certified asymmetric profile is an independently specified ODE
root. One should not claim identity with a particular raster Figure2 profile
without an additional comparison of its actual shooting data.

4. What would be a genuinely new next step

If pursuing this class, first independently refine a specific old max-to-max
record into a genuine, well-resolved boundary-value candidate. Then continue
the reversible half-return family B=(v(l),q(l))=0 in (k0,p0,l), monitoring the
angle holonomy, seam types, and geometric support. Pseudo-arclength avoids
assuming a particular coordinate stays nonsingular. Only integer half-angle
holonomy roots close with the same single reflection axis; rational roots
with a nontrivial rotational symmetry force the center into the hull.

Any strict-half-plane candidate would still require a continuous-curve lower
bound, not the convex hull of finitely sampled points. No such candidate or
new converged root has been obtained by this source audit.
