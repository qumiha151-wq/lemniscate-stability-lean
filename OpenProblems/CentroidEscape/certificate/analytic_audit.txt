Independent audit of the closed-expander / escaping-centroid certificate
Reviewer: translator_formal subagent; 2026-11-11

Scope. Read-only review of one_sided/arb_shoot.py,
one_sided/validate_certificate.py, certificate_plan.txt, certificate_center.json,
validated_certificate.json, and their logs. No expensive shooting integration
was rerun. I independently reconstructed the inexpensive rational/Arb checks
from the stored certificate, with 220 decimal digits. The conclusions below
depend on the actual stored interval results and the trusted Python/flint/Arb
runtime; this is NOT an end-to-end Lean certificate or an independent runtime.

Result. No fatal analytic or implementation gap was found. Subject to the
listed runtime trust and stored interval integrations, the certificate proves
an exact shooting zero, a smooth closed unit-speed expander with positive
arclength centroid, and consequently an FEF example with unbounded centroid.
It does not prove half-plane migration, classify closed solitons, or establish
that all remaining open problems have been solved.

1. State equation and symmetry
z=(x,y,u,w,k,v,m),
z'=(u,w,-kw,ku,v,-k^3/2+xw-yu,x),
z(0)=(a,0,0,1,b,0,0).
The shooting map is F(a,b,L)=(y(L),u(L),v(L)).
For the six geometric coordinates R=(x,-y,-u,w,k,-v), direct expansion gives
f(Rz)=-R f(z). Both shooting endpoints are fixed by R. Extending the first
half by R z(2L-s) makes a C^1 solution at L; the polynomial ODE then makes it
smooth. The endpoint at 2L equals the initial state and all jets agree by
the same equation, giving a smooth periodic solution. The invariant
(u^2+w^2)'=0 and initial value 1 give a unit-speed immersion.

2. Weighted analytic constants
Weights (1,1,4,4,16,64,1), maximum norm. On the outer tube
(|x|,|y|,|u|,|w|,|k|,|v|)<=(1.6,2.4,1.1,1.1,3.5,6),
with m unrestricted, the asserted estimates are valid:
|f|_W<=2, |Df|_W<=8, |D^2 f|_W<=44, |D^3 f|_W<=192.
The critical first-derivative oscillator row is 3.5+4(1.1)=7.9.
The critical second derivative row is 12(3.5)+1/4=42.25.
The cubic third derivative row is 3*16^3/64=192.
The m equation adds only a row of norm 1 and no higher derivatives.

3. Taylor propagation and bootstrap
The normalized derivative coefficient bounds give the majorant
2+8q+22q^2+32q^3 <= 2(1+3q)^3.
The scalar solution w(t)=((1-12t)^(-1/2)-1)/3 has coefficient bounds
12^n/3. Thus an order-160 step h<=.01 has tail at most
tau=(.12)^161/[3(1-.12)], approximately 2.121e-149.
750 exp(60) tau <1e-118. The code keeps the full Arb interval through
every step: no midpoint reset in shoot/step. Rounding therefore already
propagates in the polynomial recurrence; adding the global truncation
budget once is correct. The midpoint refinement in arb_shoot.main is not
executed when the validation harness imports the module.
The recurrence computes all RHS coefficients at order n before assigning
the order n+1 state coefficients; its in-place polynomial lists are sound.
The stored inner bounds (1.45,2.26,1.001,1.001,3.02,4.15) have minimal
weighted margin .02475 to the outer tube. The within-step majorant is
w(.01)<.022002, leaving more than .0027 before microscopic error budgets.
This closes the first-exit bootstrap, including between mesh points.

4. Uniform shooting neighborhood and second derivatives
The radius is r=1e-80 and difference step delta=1e-70.
The center plus delta+r is less than 7.5. This explicit inequality is
not currently asserted in the harness, although it holds with margin
greater than .005; adding it would make the time-budget dependency clear.
With E=exp(60)<2e26, the weighted perturbation bound
(E+2)(delta+r) is less than 1e-43, far below the tube slack.
The flow second variation is bounded by (44/8)E(E-1)<6E^2;
the mixed time derivatives cost 8E each and the second time derivative
costs 16. Extraction to the shooting norm costs at most 64, so H=1e56
is a safe uniform Hessian bound for F in the full parameter box.
A centered difference needs only a second-derivative bound here:
entrywise derivative error <=H*delta/2; the max-row-sum matrix error
has the factor 3 used by the harness.

5. Exact root check
B is the matrix of exact rational decimals recorded in the certificate,
not an unchecked floating-point inverse. Its norm is below 2 and its
determinant is nonzero (approximately .0168358).
Independent reconstruction from the saved +/- interval outputs gives
q=1.16814635533589386656107074915e-13 (with negligible interval width),
eta=||B F(center)|| <=6.74e-118.
Hence q<1e-8 and eta+q*r<r. The map p -> p-BF(p) is a contraction
of the closed radius-r max-norm ball into itself. Banach's theorem gives
a fixed point; invertibility of B gives an exact F zero. Smooth ODE
dependence and the explicit tube bootstrap supply the required domain.

6. Geometric consequence
The m integral at the exact root remains above 3.7, while L<7.5.
The reflected full curve has centroid (m(L)/L,0), so its x centroid is
greater than .49. The independently recomputed center enclosure is
0.50482486610354154010948068575887418066487097731861
with uncertainty below 4e-51, before a still tiny root-variation error.
With normal nu=(-w,u), the ODE gives k_ss+k^3/2=xw-yu=-<beta,nu>.
Thus rho(t)=(1+4t)^(1/4) gives the expander; a smooth circle
reparametrization removes its tangential velocity. Its arclength centroid
is rho times the centroid of beta. Translating by the fixed initial
centroid makes c(0)=0 and c(t)=(rho(t)-1)c_beta, unbounded.
The example is NOT a migration example: both its initial positive-x
point and its endpoint negative-x point lie on the symmetry axis, so
the origin belongs to its convex hull.

7. Serialization and provenance checks
At 220 dps I reconstructed the saved balls, centered differences, B,
all matrix checks, analytic constants, and positive moment check without
rerunning shooting. All inequalities passed. python-flint's Arb radius
constructor was checked on an uncertain radius and encloses its upper
endpoint; radius rounding is outward. Strict Arb comparisons return
False on overlapping/uncertain bounds. Decimal .str output reparses as

Boundary. This audit is a mathematical/code review plus an inexpensive
reconstruction, not a second high-precision integration. A full Lean
claim still requires formally connecting interval arithmetic/Taylor
enclosures, polynomial ODE existence and smooth dependence, the root,
periodic closure, geometry, and FEF. Algebraic identities or a conditional
Banach theorem alone do not constitute that end-to-end formalization.
