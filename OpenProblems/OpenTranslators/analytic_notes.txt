RESEARCH OBSERVATION: COMPLETE OPEN TRANSLATORS FOR THE FREE ELASTIC FLOW
Status: human mathematical proof, independently checked at the equation level;
not Lean-formalized; novelty has not been established. This is not a resolution
of the closed-curve self-similar classification, length-growth, or migration
questions in Miura--Wheeler, arXiv:2404.12619v3.

1. Precise class and convention

We allow arbitrary smooth immersions beta: R -> R^2 whose induced metric is
complete. No properness, embeddedness, bounded curvature, finite bending energy,
or uniform bounds on spatial derivatives are assumed. We will prove existence in
this broad class. In particular, the adjective 'nonproper' is NOT asserted:
properness of the constructed examples is left undecided.

Let tau=(cos(theta),sin(theta)), nu=(-sin(theta),cos(theta)), and k=theta_s.
The free elastic flow convention is normal velocity -F nu, where
F=k_ss+k^3/2. We seek a curve translating with V=(1,0), so that
V dot nu=-sin(theta). Thus the profile equation is F=sin(theta).

PROPOSITION A. For every real initial triple (theta_0,k_0,v_0), there is a
unique smooth global solution of

  theta'=k,   k'=v,   v'=-k^3/2+sin(theta)                 (A1)

on all of R, with those data at s=0. Defining

  beta(s)=integral from 0 to s of (cos(theta(r)),sin(theta(r))) dr,

gives a complete unit-speed immersed profile satisfying F=sin(theta). After
an explicit smooth reparametrization, beta+tV gives an eternal pure-normal
free elastic flow. For example, the initial triple (pi/2,1,0) gives a
nonstraight profile and nonzero normal velocity at s=0.

Proof of global existence.

The right-hand side of (A1) is smooth on R^3. Local existence, uniqueness, and
the finite-dimensional ODE continuation theorem therefore apply. On a maximal
solution interval (a,b) containing 0, introduce

  H(s)=v(s)^2/2+k(s)^4/8 >= 0.

Differentiating, and using (A1), gives the exact cancellation

  H'=v(-k^3/2+sin(theta))+(k^3/2)v=v sin(theta).

Consequently |H'| <= |v| <= sqrt(2H). To avoid dividing by zero, for epsilon>0
differentiate sqrt(H+epsilon):

  |(sqrt(H+epsilon))'|
    = |H'|/(2 sqrt(H+epsilon)) <= 1/sqrt(2).

Integration between 0 and s, followed by epsilon -> 0, gives

  sqrt(H(s)) <= sqrt(H(0))+|s|/sqrt(2).                   (A2)

On any bounded interval of s, (A2) uniformly bounds both |v| <= sqrt(2H) and
|k| <= (8H)^(1/4). The identity theta'=k then also bounds theta on that
interval. If b were finite, the trajectory (theta,k,v) would stay inside a
fixed compact subset of R^3 on [0,b). The continuation theorem would extend
the solution beyond b, a contradiction. Applying the same argument to a
proves a=-infinity and b=+infinity. Smoothness follows by differentiating
the smooth ODE. In particular, no global spatial derivative bound is asserted.

Proof of the geometry and completeness.

By definition beta_s=tau and |tau|=1. Thus beta is a smooth immersion, and
its induced metric is exactly ds^2 on R, which is complete. Also
tau_s=k nu and nu_s=-k tau, so its signed curvature is k. Equation (A1)
gives k_ss+k^3/2=sin(theta). Therefore X(t,s)=beta(s)+tV satisfies

  (X_t)^perp=(V dot nu)nu=-sin(theta)nu=-F nu.

This is already the geometric free elastic flow with a tangential component.
For a pure-normal parametrization, solve

  partial_t phi(t,u)=-cos(theta(phi(t,u))),  phi(0,u)=u.  (A3)

The scalar vector field is smooth, hence locally Lipschitz, and has absolute
value at most 1. Therefore its solution exists for every real t,u, with
|phi(t,u)-u| <= |t|. Smooth dependence on initial data holds locally around
every (t,u), and the variational equation gives

  partial_u phi(t,u)
   = exp(integral from 0 to t of
       k(phi(r,u)) sin(theta(phi(r,u))) dr) > 0.

This integral is finite on every finite time interval because phi remains
in a bounded spatial interval there. For fixed t, the displacement bound
implies phi(t,u)->+infinity as u->+infinity, and ->-infinity at the other
end. Thus phi(t,.) is an orientation-preserving smooth diffeomorphism of R.
Set Y(t,u)=beta(phi(t,u))+tV. Since phi_u>0, its Frenet normal and signed
curvature are those of beta at phi. Direct differentiation gives

  Y_t=V-(V dot tau)tau=(V dot nu)nu=-F nu.

Each Y(t,.) remains a complete immersion, because it is a reparametrization
and translation of beta. For (theta_0,k_0,v_0)=(pi/2,1,0), k(0)=1 rules out
a straight profile; F(0)=1 makes its normal motion nonzero. This completes
the proof of Proposition A.

2. Bounded-curvature rigidity

PROPOSITION B. Every smooth complete planar translating profile for the free
elastic flow with a nonzero translation vector and globally bounded curvature
is a straight line parallel to that vector. No finite-energy assumption is
needed for this statement.

It suffices to prove this for V=(1,0). A nonzero vector can be brought into
this form by rotation and dilation of the profile (translation speeds scale
by the inverse cube of the spatial factor). A complete oriented open curve
can be parametrized by arclength on R; its tangent has a smooth real angle
lift theta because R is simply connected. The closed case also follows by
lifting its periodic arclength parametrization to R.

Proof.

Write v=k_s and suppose |k| <= K on R. The profile equation implies
|k_ss| <= M := K^3/2+1. Taylor's formula with integral remainder at spacing
one reads

  k(s+1)-k(s)=k_s(s)+integral_0^1 (1-r) k_ss(s+r) dr.

Hence |v(s)| <= 2K+M/2 for all s. Define the elastic force vector

  P(s)=v(s)nu(s)+(k(s)^2/2)tau(s).

The Frenet equations give

  P_s=(v_s+k^3/2)nu=sin(theta)nu.

Taking the first component gives

  P_1=-v sin(theta)+(k^2/2)cos(theta),
  P_1'=-sin(theta)^2.                                  (B1)

Since both k and v are bounded, P_1 is bounded. Its monotonicity in (B1)
shows that it has finite limits at both spatial ends and

  integral_R sin(theta)^2 ds < infinity.                (B2)

The function f=sin(theta) is Lipschitz, since f'=k cos(theta) is bounded.
A Lipschitz L^2 function on R tends to zero at both ends: otherwise a sequence
of points where |f|>=delta produces, by the Lipschitz bound, disjoint intervals
of a fixed positive length on which |f|>=delta/2, contradicting L^2
integrability. If its Lipschitz constant is zero, the assertion follows
immediately from L^2 integrability. Therefore sin(theta(s))->0 as s->+/-infinity.

For all sufficiently large positive s, |sin(theta(s))|<1/2. The set of real
angles satisfying this inequality is the disjoint union of intervals
(n*pi-pi/6,n*pi+pi/6), n in Z. The continuous image of a connected ray lies
inside a single such interval. As sin(theta)->0, theta must converge to its
center n_+*pi. The same argument on a negative ray gives theta->n_-*pi.

We use the following elementary derivative-limit fact: if a C^2 function g
has a finite limit at +infinity and |g''| <= C, then g'->0. For fixed h>0,
Taylor's formula gives

  |g'(s)| <= |g(s+h)-g(s)|/h + Ch/2.

Taking limsup as s->+infinity, then h down to zero, proves the claim. Applying
the assertion to g(-s) covers -infinity as well.

First apply the fact to g=theta: theta has limits, and theta_ss=v is bounded.
It follows that k=theta_s->0 at both ends. Next apply it to g=k: k has limits
zero and k_ss is bounded. It follows that v=k_s->0 at both ends. Thus the
formula for P_1 in (B1) shows P_1->0 at both ends. A nonincreasing function
with both endpoint limits zero is identically zero. Equation (B1) then gives
sin(theta)=0 everywhere. By continuity theta is a constant multiple of pi;
hence k=0 and beta is a straight line parallel to V. QED.

Corollary. Every nontrivial profile produced in Proposition A has unbounded
curvature on R. In particular, in unit-speed parametrization it is outside
the class of curves having all positive-order derivatives globally bounded.
This argument does not decide whether such profiles are proper or embedded.

PROPOSITION C. In the broad complete-immersion class of Proposition A,
finite total bending energy already forces a straight line. Consequently
the nontrivial examples of Proposition A have infinite bending energy.

Proof. It suffices to show that I=integral_R k^2 ds<infinity implies bounded
k, and then apply Proposition B. Suppose some point has |k|=A>=2. Finite
I supplies one point to its left and one to its right where |k|<A/2.
On the compact interval between these points, |k| attains an interior
maximum B>=A. At a maximizer s_0, multiply k by a sign epsilon in {+1,-1}
so that g=epsilon*k satisfies g(s_0)=B and g'(s_0)=0. Take the connected
component of {g>B/2} in that compact interval which contains s_0. Both
endpoints have g=B/2, since the original interval endpoints have |k|<A/2.
Throughout this component, |k|<=B, and the translator equation gives

  |g''|=|k''| <= B^3/2+1 <= B^3.

Taylor's formula from s_0 therefore gives

  g(s) >= B-(B^3/2)(s-s_0)^2

until either boundary is reached. To drop to B/2 requires
|s-s_0|>=1/B. Thus the full interval [s_0-1/B,s_0+1/B] has |k|>=B/2,
and

  I >= (2/B)(B^2/4)=B/2 >= A/2.

Since the original point was arbitrary, |k|<=max(2,2I) everywhere.
Proposition B now proves the assertion. QED.

3. Verified primary-source context and limits of interpretation

Miura--Rupp, A new energy method for shortening and straightening complete
curves, Journal fuer die reine und angewandte Mathematik 830 (2026), 1-50:
https://doi.org/10.1515/crelle-2025-0077
https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/crelle-2025-0077/html

Section 2 explicitly uses dot-C-infinity for smooth maps with bounded
positive-order derivatives (definition in Section 3.1). Theorem 2.7 treats
proper immersed initial curves in that class, with speed bounded below and
finite bending energy. Corollary 8.2 states rigidity of non-scaling or
shrinking free-elastic solitons in dot-C-infinity with finite bending energy.
Section 8 ends by observing that infinite-energy nonstationary non-scaling
solitons are not known. Proposition A lies outside their bounded-derivative
class; it therefore does not contradict their theorem. The final broad
remark does not itself explicitly restate the class, so its intended scope
must not be silently inferred or claimed resolved.

Miura--Wheeler, arXiv:2404.12619v3, introduction p.2, also mentions unknown
nontrivial open special solutions, particularly translators. That paragraph
does not specify properness, energy, or bounded curvature. Proposition A
answers a very broad literal immersed-complete formulation, but it must not
be described as settling a more restrictive, unspecified translator question.
It has no direct consequence for their main CLOSED-curve open problems.

The other primary source checked was Solitons to the Willmore flow,
https://doi.org/10.1515/ans-2023-0150, Sections 5.1 and 7. Its convention is
translation by -tV, so its scalar translator equation has the corresponding
opposite choice of translation vector. Section 7 derives profile ODEs and
some rigidity statements; Section 5.1 contains numerical graphical examples.
No claim of novelty for Propositions A or B is made here.
