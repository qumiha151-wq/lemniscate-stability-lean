FINAL ACTUAL-GEOMETRY INTERFACE
OpenTranslator.Immersion.geometric_translator_affine_rigidity takes:
 - actual coordinate functions x,y:R->R, both C-infinity;
 - actual unit speed (deriv x)^2+(deriv y)^2=1 everywhere;
 - a nonzero constant velocity, expressed as vx!=0 OR vy!=0;
 - the geometric normal-speed equation
     vx*(-deriv y)+vy*deriv x = -elasticForce x y;
 - a bound |curvature x y|<=K everywhere.

Both curvature and elasticForce are computed from ACTUAL POSITION DERIVATIVES:
 k=x'*y''-y'*x'', F=k''+k^3/2. Unit speed justifies this arclength curvature.
The theorem concludes that there exist constants a,b such that
 a^2+b^2=1, vx*b-vy*a=0, and for every real s,
 x(s)=x(0)+s*a, y(s)=y(0)+s*b.
Thus the actual complete unit-speed coordinate immersion is an affine straight
line with unit direction parallel to the arbitrary nonzero translation velocity.
The sibling curvature theorem explicitly proves k=0 and normal force=0 globally.

DIRECT PROJECTION ARGUMENT
Define from actual tangent coordinates
 f=vx*y'-vy*x' = -V dot N, q=vx*x'+vy*y' = V dot T.
Frenet differentiation proves f'=kq and q'=-kf.
Actual unit speed gives f^2+q^2=L=vx^2+vy^2, with L>0 from nonzero velocity.
The actual translator equation gives v'= -k^3/2+f, where v=deriv k.

ProjectedRigidity proves rigidity from this system by deriving every bound:
f,q are bounded using their invariant; bounded k bounds v', and the previously
proved actual mean-value estimate bounds v. The projected stress
P=-v*f+(k^2/2)*q is differentiated to get P'=-f^2. It is bounded and antitone,
hence has finite limits. Bounded P'' and DerivativeLimit force f->0; bounded
(kq)' forces kq->0; bounded k gives kf->0; the identity
 L*k^2=(kf)^2+(kq)^2 then gives k->0, using L>0. DerivativeLimit gives v->0,
so P->0 at both ends, P is identically zero, f=0, and then k=0 because q!=0.
All projected hypotheses are explicitly constructed from actual coordinates
in ImmersionRigidity. Finally actual x''=y''=0 is integrated to the displayed
affine coordinate formula. No rotation, dilation or cube-root normalization
is needed anywhere in this argument.
