EXACT RIGIDITY HYPOTHESES AND CONCLUSIONS
Input is any actual global normalized ODE solution
 theta'=k, k'=v, v'=-k^3/2+sin(theta)
and a real K with |k(s)|<=K for every s in R.
bounded_curvature_rigidity proves k(s)=0 and sin(theta(s))=0 everywhere.
bounded_curvature_position_line proves that the ACTUAL integrated positions
from Geometry satisfy betaX(s)=c*s, betaY(s)=0 for a constant c with c^2=1.
Thus the reconstructed unit-speed immersion is a straight line parallel to V=(1,0).

No v bound, stress relation, endpoint limit, integrability, finite energy,
angle convergence, or no-zero-crossing hypothesis is assumed.

DERIVED ANALYTIC CHAIN
1. The ODE gives |v'|<=M=K^3/2+1.
2. A generic actual two-derivative estimate gives |v|<=2K+M. It uses a
   mean-value point in [s,s+1] and the Lipschitz bound for v derived from v'.
   No Taylor formula or derivative bound is an input to the final theorem.
3. Actual stress P=-v*sin(theta)+(k^2/2)*cos(theta) has derivative
   P'=-sin(theta)^2. The formula is differentiated directly using the ODE.
   Bounded k and derived bounded v bound P; monotonicity gives finite limits
   at both spatial ends.
4. P''=-2*sin(theta)*cos(theta)*k is bounded. The independently verified
   DerivativeLimit theorem (finite function limit plus Lipschitz derivative
   forces derivative ->0) gives sin(theta)^2 ->0 at both ends. Continuity
   of square root gives sin(theta)->0.
5. The actual derivative of sin(theta) is k*cos(theta), whose derivative
   is v*cos(theta)-k^2*sin(theta), and is bounded by the preceding estimates.
   DerivativeLimit therefore gives k*cos(theta)->0.
6. Bounded k and sin(theta)->0 give k*sin(theta)->0. The trigonometric
   identity k^2=(k*sin(theta))^2+(k*cos(theta))^2 gives k->0.
   This avoids any angle-lift limit or decomposition into trigonometric intervals.
7. DerivativeLimit applied to k with bounded v' gives v->0. The actual
   stress bound |P|<=|v|+k^2/2 now gives P->0 at BOTH ends.
8. Antitone P with both endpoint limits zero is identically zero. Its actual
   derivative forces sin(theta)=0. Differentiating this identity, using
   cos(theta)^2=1, gives k=0. The actual angle is constant, and integrating
   the actual tangent proves the straight position-coordinate formula.
